## G-1 — Educational primer

**Headline (≤58 chars):** What is a mineral right? An owner primer.
**Body (≤1,500 chars):**

```
Mineral rights are a separate ownership category from surface
rights. They give the holder a share of production, paid by the
operator as a royalty, or the right to lease or sell that share.

A quick primer for owners:

- Mineral interest (MI): ownership of the subsurface share.
- Royalty interest (NRI): the percentage of production paid to
  the owner, free of production costs.
- Working interest (WI): the operator's share, which carries the
  cost of drilling and operating.
- Division order: the document that sets who gets paid what,
  based on title.
- Lease: the agreement that gives an operator the right to drill
  in exchange for a bonus and a royalty.

Our free learning center walks through each term in plain language
and explains what to ask any buyer, any operator, or any
attorney. Start at
https://mineralrightsxchange.com/learning-center/
```

**Body (≤1,500 chars):**

```
A change in royalty check amount is one of the most common
questions owners ask. There are several non-alarming reasons:

- Natural decline: wells produce less over time, especially
  after the first 12-36 months.
- Operator timing: check cycles can shift month to month.
- Working-interest deductions: post-production costs can change.
- New wells on the same unit: production mix can shift.
- Maintenance or shut-in periods: temporary reductions.

The educational first step is to read your own division order
```

## G-6 — Lease vs. sale

**Headline (≤58 chars):** Lease or sale? An owner-side primer.
**Body (≤1,500 chars):**

```
A mineral-rights owner is usually choosing between two
structures:

- Lease: you keep ownership of the minerals and receive a
  royalty on production. You typically get an upfront bonus and
  then monthly royalty payments while the operator produces.
- Sale: you sell the minerals (or a defined share) outright
  and receive a one-time payment. Future production goes to the
  buyer.

The right answer depends on the owner's situation — liquidity
need, family timeline, tax posture, and how long production is
expected to continue. This is general educational information;
for decisions specific to your situation, a CPA and an estate
attorney in the relevant state are the right professionals.
```

**Body (≤1,500 chars):**

```
A decline curve is the rate at which a well's production
decreases over time. Most modern wells follow a steep early
decline followed by a long tail of lower production.

What an owner can do:

- Read your own division order to confirm your NRI and the
  effective month of the check.
```

## G-8 — Title records

**Headline (≤58 chars):** How to title-check mineral rights.
**Body (≤1,500 chars):**

```
An owner can confirm what they actually own using public
records:

- County recorder: where deeds are filed.
- County appraisal district: where mineral accounts are
  sometimes indexed.
- State land office (in some states, including Texas): where
  state-owned mineral interest is recorded.
- Probate court: where inherited interests are documented.
- Operator division-order file: where the operator's view of
  ownership is recorded.

If a public-record check does not match what an owner believes
they own, the right next step is a licensed attorney in the
relevant state who can order a title opinion.

This is general information, not legal advice and not a title
opinion. For matters that require a licensed professional,
please consult an attorney.
```

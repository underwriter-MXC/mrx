## Mandatory packet fields

Each row preserves only the evidence necessary to reproduce the reported entry:

- source document type, filename or stable reference, statement date, and source location;
- payor and the property, lease, well, or unit identifier exactly as reported;
- product and production or sales period;
- reported cost label, code, amount, unit, volume, and stated calculation basis when displayed;
- related adjustment, reversal, suspense, or rebooking reference;
- supporting-document status and one evidence status: `matched`, `conflict`, `missing`, or `unresolved`;
- one neutral clarification question for every conflict, gap, or unresolved item; and
- retrieval date without overwriting the source date.

## Cannibalization and compliance boundaries

The article may not:

- decode an entire royalty statement or calculate royalty cash flow;
- interpret a lease, amendment, division order, title instrument, or contract;
- determine whether a reported cost or deduction is permitted, correct, improper, recoverable, or owed;
- perform a royalty audit, resolve a discrepancy, or establish an owner decimal, payment entitlement, damages, or remedy;
- treat Railroad Commission production data as owner payment accounting;
- treat a Comptroller tax page as proof that a particular owner statement line is correct;
- reach a legal, tax, title, accounting, appraisal, engineering, valuation, offer, or transaction conclusion; or
- invent a code definition, cost category, amount, source, identifier, period, or document relationship.

The royalty-statement guides own decoding and reconciliation. The lease-rate guide owns general deduction context. The basis-differential guide owns the distinction between market basis and deductions. The valuation guides own cash-flow and value modeling. This article owns only source preservation, provenance matching, evidence status, and neutral question routing.

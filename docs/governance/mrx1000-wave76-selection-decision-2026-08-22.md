## Mandatory cannibalization and compliance boundaries

This article owns only the mechanics of documenting a one-variable scenario run against an existing worksheet. It may not:

- calculate, estimate, appraise, certify, or recommend a property value;
- analyze an offer or recommend whether to sell, hold, sign, negotiate, or transact;
- create a forecast, price deck, decline curve, discount rate, development case, ownership decimal, NRI, or other model assumption;
- rank variables or claim that one assumption is most important, controlling, probable, expected, or more likely;
- assign probability weights, predict development, or convert nearby activity into a property-specific conclusion;
- supply proprietary, market, universal, or owner-specific assumptions or numerical examples;
- treat a public record, statement, lease, division order, map, or worksheet as proof of ownership, acreage, decimal, title, value, payment entitlement, or future activity; or
- provide individualized legal, tax, financial, engineering, title, appraisal, or transaction advice.

Each scenario row changes exactly one disclosed input. The row records the fixed-assumption version, input name and unit, source and access date, direction of the change, existing worksheet output, and an unresolved-evidence note when applicable. Input categories are illustrative, not recommendations.

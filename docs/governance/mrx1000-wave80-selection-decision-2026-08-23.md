## Mandatory register fields

Each row preserves only the document and terminology evidence needed to reproduce the observation:

- exact displayed term and surrounding label;
- document title or record type and stable source reference;
- effective date, report date, or `not stated`;
- named preparer or `not stated`;
- stated standard and edition or `standard not stated`;
- stated property, geographic, formation, depth, or report scope without expanding it; and
- one status: `term displayed`, `standard stated`, or `standard not stated`, followed by a neutral question for every missing field.

## Cannibalization and compliance boundaries

The article may not:

- certify, assign, correct, reconcile, or translate a reserve or resource classification;
- make engineering, geological, securities-disclosure, accounting, legal, title, tax, or regulatory-compliance conclusions;
- attribute a term to the owner's tract, unit, depth, title, ownership, payment, or mineral interest without separate evidence;
- predict operator intent, drilling, completion, production, timing, probability, cash flow, or development;
- construct or validate a DCF, price deck, offer, appraisal, or value conclusion;
- decide that labels from different standards, reports, preparers, dates, or scopes are equivalent or comparable; or
- invent a term, standard, preparer, date, scope, classification, reserve quantity, property fact, or development result.

The DCF article owns producing-versus-future-location modeling. The acreage-contiguity article owns geometry and development-context limits. The valuation-methodology article owns economic-method boundaries. This article owns only terminology provenance and neutral question routing.

## Authoritative source plan

1. [GovInfo, 17 CFR 210.4-10](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CFR-2025-title17-vol3/pdf/CFR-2025-title17-vol3-sec210-4-10.pdf) supports the SEC definitions and the need to preserve the governing definition rather than infer a classification.
2. [GovInfo, 17 CFR 229.1208](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CFR-2025-title17-vol3/pdf/CFR-2025-title17-vol3-sec229-1208.pdf) supports the SEC acreage-disclosure terminology and demonstrates that an acreage label has its own stated context.
3. [SEC, Oil and Gas Reporting Modernization — Small Entity Compliance Guide](https://www.sec.gov/rules-regulations/oil-gas-reporting-modernization-small-entity-compliance-guide) supports the rule framework, preparer context, and separate PUD disclosure requirements.
4. [Society of Petroleum Engineers, 2018 Petroleum Resources Management System](https://info.specommunications.org/rs/833-LLT-087/images/petroleum_resources_management_system-2018.pdf) supports preserving a named classification system and edition rather than treating an acronym as universal.
5. [Railroad Commission of Texas, Production Data Query FAQs](https://www.rrc.texas.gov/about-us/faqs/oil-gas-faq/production-data-query-system-faqs/) supports keeping regulator-reported production observations separate from reserves or acreage classifications and preserving query dates.

These sources support only terminology provenance, source context, and recordkeeping boundaries. They do not classify an owner's property, certify a report, establish comparability, predict development, or determine value.

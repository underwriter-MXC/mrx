## Editorial boundary

The article-specific search job is an evidence hierarchy for understanding how decline-curve assumptions can affect future royalty cash flow without presenting an engineering reserve report or owner-specific valuation. It must:

- distinguish reported production history, a fitted production curve, a future-volume forecast, and a royalty cash-flow scenario;
- identify the reporting level, well or lease identity, product stream, production month, processing date, corrections, first or partial months, downtime, workovers, recompletions, new wells, and commingling before fitting a curve;
- explain that a decline curve estimates a production path and does not establish title, ownership decimal, royalty rate, realized price, deductions, taxes, product quality, payment timing, reserves classification, or sale value;
- explain the practical differences among exponential, hyperbolic, and segmented assumptions without prescribing one universal model;
- keep observed production separate from projected production and label every forecast start date, horizon, terminal assumption, and scenario;
- explain that EIA's published decline-curve methodology is a public analytical example with defined data treatments and does not validate a particular owner, well, reserve report, or transaction forecast;
- explain that Railroad Commission data are reported regulatory data that can be corrected and can differ in reporting granularity, and do not replace payor statements or owner-specific records;
- preserve the distinction between regional EIA legacy-production metrics and individual-well decline curves;
- use the Texas Comptroller discounting manual only as public property-tax appraisal context for production, income, timing, and discounting—not as a private-sale formula;
- use SEC oil-and-gas reporting guidance only to show that reserve classifications have defined evidence and certainty requirements—not to classify an owner's interest or forecast;
- separate production, price, ownership decimal, deductions or taxes, timing, and discount-rate assumptions so the same risk is not counted twice;
- present multiple supported scenarios when data history, model form, future operations, price, or ownership inputs remain uncertain;
- disclose that MRX may have an economic interest and is not issuing a reserve report, engineering opinion, professional valuation opinion, payment forecast, tax conclusion, or individualized legal, accounting, investment, or operational guidance; and

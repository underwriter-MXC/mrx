## Exclusive content job

This article gives an owner a practical, non-legal framework for an internal family conversation before the owner decides whether to move forward with a mineral-rights assessment. It helps co-owners, adult children, heirs, and other family stakeholders frame the conversation, identify what each person needs to understand, surface disagreement, and determine whether the family is ready to gather facts for an assessment.

It may provide an opening script, a short agenda, questions for each participant, methods for separating facts from preferences, and a way to close the conversation with a next information-gathering step.

It must not:

- state or imply that family alignment creates, replaces, or determines legal authority, consent, title, or signature requirements;
- give co-ownership, partition, forced-sale, probate, estate-planning, tax-distribution, title, deed, or contract advice;
- teach a formal communication log, decision log, assumption register, diligence-request tracker, or offer-comparison framework;
- retell the selling-process sequence, assessment-booking steps, consultation preparation, or no-obligation service scope;
- estimate value, recommend a sale, tell the family whether to accept an offer, or imply that every family member must reach the same decision; or
- substitute for qualified legal, tax, estate-planning, accounting, valuation, or mediation professionals.

## Evidence scopes

1. University of Minnesota Extension transition-planning guidance: adaptable support for writing goals and questions in advance, involving affected parties, and holding a family meeting after facts are assembled; it is not mineral-rights law.
2. University of Minnesota Extension farm-business transfer guidance: adaptable support for brief, specific, non-accusatory communication and structured family-business meetings; it is not a rule governing mineral ownership or sale authority.
3. Utah State University Extension estate-planning guidance: bounded support for a calm setting, clear intentions, input from affected people, and an agenda that may span multiple conversations; it is not mineral-specific legal advice.
4. USDA Forest Service heirs-property overview: bounded background showing that inherited property can involve multiple heirs and fragmented interests; it does not determine this reader's title, authority, or required signatures.
5. MRX inherited-mineral-rights, seller-control, and decision-log guides: first-party context for gathering facts, preserving seller agency, and keeping the family conversation distinct from a formal transaction or legal determination.

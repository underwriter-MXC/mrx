## Exclusive content job

This article teaches a Texas mineral owner how to build a tract-by-tract administrative crosswalk from the words and identifiers already present in two or more documents. The crosswalk records each source page, county, survey, abstract, section, block, stated tract or acreage, stated interest language, and any stated depth, formation, substance, lease, unit, or well reference.

Its only permitted outcomes are `appears aligned`, `difference shown`, and `insufficient information`. It does not determine that descriptions are legally identical, accurate, complete, or controlling.

It must not:

- build or replace the Article 145 document-package index;
- calculate price per net mineral acre or infer net mineral acres;
- compare price, timing, deductions, warranties, remedies, or other substantive offer terms;
- estimate value, fairness, probability, or transaction outcome;
- establish ownership, title, acreage, authority, marketability, or the legal effect of any description;
- resolve conflicts among deeds, leases, probate records, tax records, regulatory data, correspondence, offers, or conveyance instruments;
- interpret depth, formation, substance, reservation, exception, lease, unit, well, royalty, mineral, or conveyance language;
- rank a buyer or recommend acceptance, rejection, counteroffer, negotiation, lease, or sale; or
- imply that RRC, GLO, county, tax, operator, or buyer records alone prove ownership or title.

## Evidence scopes

1. Railroad Commission of Texas Public GIS Viewer: supports the existence of survey, lease, and well search fields and provides the controlling warning that GIS data are informational, approximate, non-authoritative, and unsuitable as legal, engineering, or surveying proof.
2. Railroad Commission of Texas oil and gas well records guidance: supports recording county, lease or well name and number, operator, field, API or other Commission identifiers, and survey, abstract, section, and block when those references appear.
3. Texas General Land Office Land Grant Database: supports the availability of county, abstract number, part section, and survey/block/township search fields; it does not establish the reader's current ownership or the legal effect of an offer description.
4. Texas Property Code Chapter 5, Subchapter F: supports the bounded observation that the statutory disclosure for a covered mailed offer refers to a description of the property being conveyed. It is not used to decide whether a particular communication is covered, compliant, enforceable, void, or legally sufficient.
5. MRX document-package index and price-per-net-mineral-acre guide: establish the adjacent corpus boundaries. The crosswalk begins after files are inventoried and ends before economics are normalized.

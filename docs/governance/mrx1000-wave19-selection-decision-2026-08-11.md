## Editorial boundary

The article-specific search job is a plain-language framework for reviewing discount-rate assumptions in a mineral-rights DCF. It must:

- explain present value without presenting a universal discount rate or an owner-specific valuation conclusion;
- distinguish a discount rate from a production-decline rate, royalty decimal, lease royalty rate, capitalization rate, and buyer-specific return requirement;
- explain that the forecasted cash-flow stream and the discount rate must use consistent nominal or real, pre-tax or after-tax, and asset or equity conventions;
- explain that changing the timing, price, production, cost, development, title, or payment assumptions can change value separately from changing the discount rate;
- describe sensitivity analysis as a comparison tool rather than a method for selecting whichever rate produces a preferred answer;
- explain that the SEC and accounting standardized measure uses a prescribed 10 percent annual discount factor for proved oil-and-gas reserve disclosures and is not itself fair market value, a universal owner offer formula, or proof that 10 percent is appropriate for a specific interest;
- use IRS business-valuation guidance only for the general consistency principle that the selected benefit stream and discount or capitalization rate must match the valuation method and relevant risks, not as a mineral-property appraisal rule;
- use Treasury yield data only as dated market-reference context, not as a complete mineral-interest discount rate;
- explain that Railroad Commission production records are operator-reported regulatory data, may be lease-level rather than well-level, and may change as corrected or delinquent reports arrive;
- present a reproducible assumptions worksheet without estimating an owner-specific price, reserve quantity, development probability, legal outcome, tax result, or investment recommendation;
- disclose that MRX may have an economic interest and is not issuing a title opinion, legal opinion, reserve report, engineering opinion, professional valuation opinion, tax conclusion, or individualized legal, accounting, investment, or operational guidance; and
- avoid invented owners, wells, leases, production volumes, forecasts, prices, costs, percentages, reserves, transaction values, market premiums, testimonials, or promises.

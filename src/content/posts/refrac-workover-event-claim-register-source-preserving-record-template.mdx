> **Source-preserving boundary.** This register organizes unverified event claims, source wording, identifiers, dates, reported periods, unknowns, conflicts, and follow-up assignments. It does not confirm or interpret a refrac, workover, recompletion, filing, or operator statement; establish a property connection; infer causation; analyze production; build or segment a decline curve; estimate reserves, upside, cash flow, present value, or value; compare offers; decide ownership, title, lease, operating, or payment status; or provide legal, tax, title, land, engineering, accounting, appraisal, investment, or financial advice. MRX may have an economic interest in a later transaction; when that applies, the buyer relationship is disclosed in writing before an agreement is signed.

A **refrac workover event claim register** is a source log, not an event finding. Give every claim its own row. Copy the event term exactly as the source states it, record the well or record identifier if shown, preserve the source and dates, list displayed post-claim reporting periods without attributing a cause, and route each unknown to a follow-up owner.

The register is complete when another reviewer can locate every source and see every unresolved item. It is not complete because someone has decided that an intervention occurred or changed production or value.

## Start with one row per event claim

One well may appear in a regulator record, an operator release, a presentation, an owner email, and a production query. Those sources may use different terms or dates. Do not compress them into one confident statement.

Create a separate row whenever the file contains a distinct:

- source-stated event term;
- well, lease, API/UWI, or other identifier;
- source document, filing, page, exhibit, release, portal entry, or correspondence item;
- source publication date, retrieval date, or claimed event date;
- operator name or originating party;
- property-connection label;
- displayed reporting period; or
- conflict or missing item that needs a different records custodian.

Use a neutral register ID such as `EVENT-CLAIM-01`. Do not put a Social Security number, bank account, taxpayer number, signature, login credential, or other sensitive identifier into a worksheet that may be shared. Use the [document redaction checklist](/blog/mineral-rights-document-redaction-checklist-before-sharing-records/) before sending source copies.

## Use only the locked register fields

The register needs thirteen controlled fields:

| Field | What to record | What not to decide |
| ----- | ----- | ----- |
| Register ID and status | Neutral row ID plus open, source located, conflict noted, or routed | Whether the event is verified |
| Source-stated event type | Exact wording such as refrac, workover, or recompletion when the source uses it | Whether the terms mean the same thing |
| Well or record identifier | Well name, API/UWI, lease, field, permit, filing, or source-provided identifier | Whether the identifier proves a property connection |
| Source document or channel | File name, URL, portal, filing, release, presentation, email, or query | Whether the source is conclusive |
| Publisher or custodian | Name exactly as shown | Whether the party is correct or current |
| Source and retrieval dates | Publication or filing date plus the date the source was obtained | Whether a claim remains current |
| Claimed event date | Date or date range only when expressly stated | When work actually began or ended |
| Operator name | Name exactly as shown in that source | Current operator status or legal responsibility |
| Property-connection status | Unverified, possible, confirmed by separate record, or not applicable | Ownership, title, or acreage |
| Post-claim reporting periods | Displayed months or periods and the query source | Whether the event caused a change |
| Exact excerpt and locator | Short quotation or precise paraphrase plus page, section, exhibit, or timestamp | What the passage means technically |
| Unknowns and conflicts | Missing identifiers, different dates, different terms, or inconsistent sources | A guessed resolution |
| Follow-up | Responsible person, requested record, and next action date | The eventual professional conclusion |

Keep `event claim` in the heading. That label prevents a copied phrase from looking like a verified MRX statement.

## Preserve the source's language

Do not replace a source's word with a term that seems more precise. If one document says `recompletion` and another says `workover`, make two source rows. If a source uses a broader phrase such as `well intervention`, copy that phrase and do not silently convert it to `refrac`.

For each source, capture:

- the displayed publisher, custodian, filer, or originating party;
- the complete document, form, filing, or page identity;
- the publication, filing, effective, or retrieval dates that are actually shown;
- the exact identifier needed to find the item again;
- a short excerpt or precise locator for the event wording; and
- whether a newer, older, or conflicting item is already present.

The [RRC oil and gas well-records page](https://www.rrc.texas.gov/oil-and-gas/research-and-statistics/obtaining-commission-records/oil-and-gas-well-records/) describes record channels that may include drilling permits, completion or recompletion reports, plugging reports, logs, and miscellaneous records. Use it to locate a record and preserve the request or retrieval details. Do not interpret the file or treat its presence as proof of the event claim.

The [RRC online well-records page](https://www.rrc.texas.gov/oil-and-gas/research-and-statistics/obtaining-commission-records/oil-and-gas-well-records-online/) explains how lease PDFs can contain several record types. Record the PDF identity, profile, well or lease reference, page, and retrieval date. Do not cite the entire file as though every page supports the same statement.

## Keep form identity separate from form meaning

The [RRC oil and gas forms page](https://www.rrc.texas.gov/oil-and-gas/oil-and-gas-forms/) lists official form names and revisions. Some titles refer to completion, recompletion, or re-entry. A form title can help you identify what to request or how to label a source. It does not by itself establish what occurred at a specific well.

When a form appears in the file, record:

- the exact form name and number;
- the revision date shown on the form, if any;
- the filing, received, or approved date only when displayed;
- the API, lease, field, permit, or other identifier shown;
- the page or section containing the source-stated event term; and
- any missing page, attachment, signature, or identifier as an unknown.

Do not decide whether filing material was required, complete, timely, technically accurate, or legally effective. Route those questions to the appropriate regulator, records custodian, or qualified professional.

## Record a property connection as a limited status

An event claim may name a county, operator, field, lease, unit, or well that looks familiar. Similarity is not a confirmed connection to the owner's interest.

Use only four connection labels:

- `unverified` when no separate matching record is attached;
- `possible` when one or more identifiers may match but the connection remains unresolved;
- `confirmed by separate record` when the register identifies that separate record and its locator; or
- `not applicable` when the source is retained only as general operator or market context.

Do not change `possible` to `confirmed` based on proximity, a similar well name, the same operator, or a map impression. The [offset activity property-connection cross-check](/blog/offset-activity-property-connection-cross-check/) owns the separate matching workflow. This register only records its result and source reference when one exists.

## Keep reported periods non-causal

The [RRC production-data page](https://www.rrc.texas.gov/oil-and-gas/research-and-statistics/production-data/) links to official production-query channels. If a dated query displays periods before or after a claimed event date, the register may preserve the query date, lease or well identifier, product, reporting periods, and saved result location.

Write `reported periods observed; no cause assigned` in the row. Do not write `production increased because of the workover` or `the refrac restored the well`. A change may reflect reporting timing, corrections, downtime, allocation, commingling, another well, market constraints, or facts outside the register. This worksheet neither selects an explanation nor analyzes the series.

The live [decline-curve guide](/blog/decline-curves-and-future-royalty-cash-flow/) explains why operational and reporting discontinuities need careful treatment in a separate technical analysis. This register does not clean data, fit a curve, choose a segment, forecast production, or estimate reserves.

## Log company filings and operator materials by exact identity

The [SEC filing-search page](https://www.sec.gov/search-filings) provides access to public-company filings and search tools. When a filing mentions an intervention program or operating activity, record the filer, form, filing date, accession or stable filing reference, exact passage locator, and retrieval date.

A public-company filing may discuss a basin, program, group of wells, budget, or company-wide activity. Do not turn broad language into a claim about the owner's property. Use `not applicable` or `unverified` for the property-connection field unless a separate identified record supplies the link.

Treat a first-party operator release, presentation, operating update, portal item, or owner-relations response the same way:

- preserve the exact page or file identity;
- record the issuer and displayed date;
- quote only the relevant event wording or provide a precise locator;
- keep marketing, forward-looking, and historical language in its source context; and
- write every missing property identifier or date as an unknown.

The register does not judge the credibility of a source, reconcile company statements with regulator records, or determine whether a statement is technically or legally sufficient.

## Preserve conflicts instead of merging them

When two sources differ, keep both rows. Link them with the same conflict reference, such as `CONFLICT-03`, and describe the difference neutrally.

Useful unknowns include:

- `Which source identifies the API number for this claim?`
- `Which record states the event date rather than the publication date?`
- `Does a separate record connect this well to the owner's tract or interest?`
- `Why do the two sources use different event terms?`
- `Which records custodian can supply the missing attachment or page?`
- `Which qualified reviewer should assess the conflicting technical descriptions?`

Avoid conclusions such as `operator record is wrong`, `the filing proves a refrac`, or `production confirms the workover`. The register shows the conflict and assigns the next request. It does not resolve the conflict in advance.

## A blank event-claim template

Copy these fields into one spreadsheet row or document section for each event claim. Keep every field blank until a source supplies the information, so no fictional well, operator, event, or production fact looks like a real record.

- **Register ID and status:** `__________`
- **Source-stated event type:** `__________`
- **Well or record identifier:** `__________`
- **Source document or channel:** `__________`
- **Publisher or custodian:** `__________`
- **Source and retrieval dates:** `__________`
- **Claimed event date:** `__________`
- **Operator exactly as shown:** `__________`
- **Property-connection status:** `__________`
- **Non-causal reporting periods:** `__________`
- **Excerpt and locator:** `__________`
- **Unknowns or conflicts:** `__________`
- **Follow-up owner, request, and date:** `__________`

If a field does not apply, write `not applicable` and identify who made that administrative choice. If the answer is absent, write `unknown`. Do not leave a blank that another reader could mistake for a completed review.

## Stop before analysis, valuation, or a decision

The register is ready to share when:

- every claim has a distinct row and neutral record ID;
- every event term is copied from an identified source;
- every source has a date, locator, and retrieval reference;
- well, lease, API/UWI, and operator identifiers appear only when a source shows them;
- property connection uses one of the four limited labels;
- any displayed reporting periods are explicitly non-causal;
- conflicting sources remain separately visible;
- every unknown has a follow-up owner, requested record, and next action date; and
- sensitive information has been reviewed before sharing.

Stop if the next step would require confirming an event, interpreting filing content, resolving property identity, assigning causation, cleaning or segmenting production history, forecasting production, estimating reserves, choosing DCF or discount-rate assumptions, valuing the interest, analyzing an offer, or recommending a transaction.

An agency records custodian, operator, qualified petroleum engineer, attorney, landman, title professional, accountant, tax adviser, appraiser, or financial professional may be appropriate depending on the question. The completed register helps that person locate the claims and gaps. It does not replace their work.

### Key Takeaways

- **The assessment is a structured process, not a single conversation**: Inputs are gathered, the methodology is applied, and the deliverable is written. Each step is documented.
- **Public production data is the foundation**: Well-by-well production from the [Texas Railroad Commission](https://www.rrc.texas.gov/) and the [EIA Texas state profile](https://www.eia.gov/state/?id=TX) is the starting point for every assessment.
- **Owner-supplied documents sharpen precision**: Lease, division order, and royalty checks confirm what public data implies. The two are reconciled before the value range is finalized.

- **The output is a range, not a point estimate**: The single most useful property of the deliverable is that uncertainty is acknowledged rather than hidden.

## What the Assessment Process Is

The mineral rights assessment process is the structured workflow that produces a defensible value range for the interest. The process is described in detail on the [MRX methodology page](/methodology/) and is built on three independent methods that converge on the same answer.

- **Discounted cash flow (DCF).** Forecasted production is multiplied by a price deck and discounted back to present value at a market-clearing discount rate.
- **Comparable transactions.** Recent sales of similar interests in the same county and formation provide a market-based price per net mineral acre.
- **Royalty income multiple.** The trailing monthly royalty income is divided into the value range to produce the implied multiple. The [MRX published methodology](/methodology/) uses the multiple as one of three cross-checks to confirm the DCF and comparable-transactions methods are producing a market-consistent answer.

The deliverable is the value range, with the inputs and the math attached.

## The Six Inputs to the Assessment

Every assessment, regardless of the underlying interest, is built on the inputs named in the [MRX published methodology](/methodology/). Each input is documented in the deliverable so the owner can verify each one.

1. **Production data.** Well-by-well production from the [RRC public records](https://www.rrc.texas.gov/) and the [EIA Texas state profile](https://www.eia.gov/state/?id=TX). The production data is the foundation; the rest of the assessment is built on it.
2. **Decline curve and remaining reserves.** The fitted decline curve and the remaining recoverable volume for each well. The decline curve is selected from observable production history; the remaining reserves are the present value of the unproduced volume.
3. **Lease terms and royalty percentage.** The owner's royalty percentage, the lease term, and any deductions. Sourced from the owner's lease and division order.
4. **Operator and well control.** The operator on the well, the working-interest ownership, and any permits or exceptions. Sourced from the RRC and the owner's division order.
5. **Comparable transactions.** Recent sales of similar interests in the same county and formation. Sourced from county records and the methodology's comparable-transactions selection.
6. **Commodity price context.** The long-term oil and gas price deck used in the DCF. Sourced from observable market data and the [EIA annual outlook](https://www.eia.gov/outlooks/aeo/).

Each input is documented; each input is verifiable. The owner sees the source for every number that drives the value range.

## How the Methodology Is Applied

The [MRX published methodology](/methodology/) describes the steps in detail. The summary is the following sequence.

### Step 1: Pull Production History

The production history is pulled from the [RRC public records](https://www.rrc.texas.gov/) for each well within the area of interest. The production is the input to the decline curve fit.

### Step 2: Fit the Decline Curve

A decline curve is fitted to the historical production. The fitted decline determines how fast production falls off and how much volume is left to recover. The decline curve is held consistent across comparable interests.

### Step 3: Build the DCF

The forecasted production is multiplied by the price deck, the operating costs are subtracted, and the resulting net income stream is discounted back to present value at the market-clearing discount rate. The DCF is run for each interest and summed to a total value range.

### Step 4: Select Comparable Transactions

Recent sales of similar interests in the same county and formation are selected. The price per net mineral acre and the implied royalty multiple are recorded for each transaction. The comparable-transactions selection is documented.

### Step 5: Cross-Check with the Royalty Income Multiple

The trailing monthly royalty income is divided into the value range to produce the implied multiple. The [MRX published methodology](/methodology/) uses the multiple as one of three cross-checks to confirm the DCF and comparable-transactions methods are producing a market-consistent answer. The published methodology does not state a fixed market range; the multiple is a comparison tool, not a threshold.

### Step 6: Reconcile the Three Methods

Where the three methods overlap, that range is the defensible value range. Where they diverge, the divergence is documented and explained in the deliverable. The owner sees the math, not just the answer.

## What the Owner Receives

The deliverable is a written assessment that includes:

- A defensible value range expressed as dollars per net mineral acre and as a total offer range.
- A DCF summary showing the production, price deck, and discount rate used.
- A comparable-transactions summary showing the recent sales in the county and formation.
- A royalty income multiple check showing the implied multiple of the trailing monthly royalty income.
- A list of every input document the owner provided or that was pulled from public data.
- A clear statement that the assessment is not a third-party prepared professional appraisal report, not legal guidance, and not tax guidance.

The deliverable is auditable. Any qualified buyer can replicate the math and identify the specific input that produces a different number.

## What the Process Does Not Do

The assessment is precise about what it does and does not promise.

- **It does not produce a third-party prepared professional appraisal report.** The assessment is a market analysis built on public data and the published methodology. For court, tax, or estate contexts where a third-party prepared professional appraisal report is required, engage a state-licensed appraiser.
- **It does not provide legal guidance.** For title, probate, contract, or estate questions, the next step is a qualified attorney.
- **It does not provide tax guidance.** For basis, capital gains, or exchange treatment, the next step is a qualified CPA.
- **It does not guarantee a specific sale price or closing.** The valuation is a market-based range; the actual transaction depends on negotiation, due diligence, and title.

These boundaries are documented on the [MRX methodology page](/methodology/) and are restated in every deliverable.

## Why the Process Is Built on Public Data

Public data is verifiable. The [Texas Railroad Commission](https://www.rrc.texas.gov/) maintains well-by-well production records. The [EIA Texas state profile](https://www.eia.gov/state/?id=TX) provides the basin-level production context. Both are free to access and free to use.

A buyer who claims a different valuation for the same interest has to defend the claim against the same public production numbers. The owner can take the deliverable to any qualified buyer and ask the buyer to point to the specific input that produces a different number. This is the difference between an opinion and a market analysis.

## Texas Basin Productivity Context

Texas basin productivity varies by formation and by operator. The [EIA Texas state profile](https://www.eia.gov/state/?id=TX) provides the basin-level production context for Permian, Eagle Ford, Haynesville, and Barnett acreage. The basin context is documented in the deliverable and is part of the comparable-transactions selection, since an interest in the Permian is not directly comparable to an interest in the Barnett without a basin adjustment.

## How Long the Assessment Takes

Most assessments are completed within a few business days once the supporting documents are in hand. The bottleneck is usually the comparable-transactions selection, not the DCF. Interests in basins with limited recent comparable transactions take longer because the comparable-transactions component requires more outreach.

The underwriter communicates the timeline upfront and notifies the owner if the assessment will take longer than the standard window.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is the mineral rights assessment process?

The mineral rights assessment process is the structured workflow that produces a defensible value range for the interest. The process is described in detail on the [MRX methodology page](/methodology/) and is built on three independent methods: discounted cash flow, comparable transactions, and a royalty income multiple check. The deliverable is a written value range with documented inputs.

### What inputs go into the assessment?

The inputs are production data, decline curve and remaining reserves, lease terms and royalty percentage, operator and well control, comparable transactions, and commodity price context. Each input is sourced from public records or owner-supplied documents and is documented in the deliverable.

### Where does the production data come from?

Production data is pulled from the [Texas Railroad Commission](https://www.rrc.texas.gov/) and the [EIA Texas state profile](https://www.eia.gov/state/?id=TX). The RRC maintains well-by-well production records; the EIA provides the basin-level production context used in the assessment.

### What is the DCF method?

The DCF (discounted cash flow) method forecasts the future royalty income from a well, discounts each year's income back to present value at a market-clearing discount rate, and sums the discounted cash flows to produce a present value. It is one of three methods used in the [MRX methodology](/methodology/), and it is the most detailed of the three because it builds the forecast from public production data.

### What is the royalty income multiple check?

The multiple check is the ratio of the value of the interest to its trailing monthly average royalty income. The [MRX published methodology](/methodology/) uses the multiple as one of three cross-checks to confirm the DCF and comparable-transactions methods are producing a market-consistent answer. The published methodology does not state a fixed market range; the multiple is a comparison tool, not a threshold.

### What is the difference between an assessment and a third-party prepared professional appraisal report?

An assessment is a market analysis built on public data and the published methodology. A third-party prepared professional appraisal report is a report prepared under applicable professional appraisal standards prepared by a licensed appraiser and is a legal document for court, tax, and estate contexts. The assessment is appropriate for owner decision-making; the third-party prepared professional appraisal report is appropriate for legal proceedings.

### How long does the assessment take?

Most assessments are completed within a few business days once the supporting documents are in hand. Interests in basins with limited recent comparable transactions may take longer because the comparable-transactions component requires more outreach.

## Answer first

No valuation methodology can ensure that a transaction is fair or produce a guaranteed outcome. A useful review instead makes its records, assumptions, dates, limitations, and unanswered questions clear enough for an owner to evaluate the discussion and decide what additional advice may be needed.

![MRX valuation review workspace labeled “mineral rights valuation methodology questions”.](/assets/articles/inline/questions-to-ask-about-mineral-rights-valuation-methodology/mineral-rights-valuation-methodology-questions.webp)

## Ask what the review covers

Start by asking what property and interest the discussion concerns and whether the reviewer has the documents needed to understand the scope. Ask which information comes from records, which parts are assumptions, and which subjects are outside the review.

For example, the [Texas Comptroller's Property Classification Guide](https://comptroller.texas.gov/taxes/property-tax/docs/96-313.pdf) distinguishes oil-and-gas or mineral interests from surface interests for its classification purposes. That distinction is useful background, but it is not a title determination, an appraisal, or an ownership opinion for a particular property.

## Use RRC information within its limits

The Texas RRC offers [Oil and Gas Production Data](https://www.rrc.texas.gov/oil-and-gas/research-and-statistics/production-data/) and [Well Information](https://www.rrc.texas.gov/oil-and-gas/research-and-statistics/well-information/) resources. These can help an owner identify reported production and public well-status context to discuss with a reviewer.

They do not establish title, ownership, or a dollar value. A careful discussion should say when public information is being used as context and when a question must be addressed through other records or professionals.

## Questions that improve transparency

- What records and reporting periods were reviewed?
- Which inputs are reported facts, and which are assumptions?
- What information was not available or not verified?
- What could change the discussion or the proposed terms?
- Does any question call for legal, tax, title, or certified-appraisal advice?

These questions do not determine whether a price is fair. They help an owner understand the basis of a discussion before choosing a next step.

## Review written terms separately

Methodology and transaction terms are different things. Ask for enough time to read written terms, identify contingencies, and seek independent advice where appropriate. A clear explanation of review boundaries is more useful than a promise about an outcome.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can a valuation review guarantee a fair result?

No. A review can explain information and assumptions, but it cannot guarantee a result or determine what every party should accept.

### What can RRC records tell me?

They can provide reported production and public well-information context. They do not prove ownership or resolve title questions.

### Does the Comptroller guide determine my mineral interest?

No. It provides Texas property-classification context and is not a title, ownership, or value conclusion for your property.

### When should I seek outside advice?

Seek the appropriate qualified professional when you need legal, tax, title, or certified-appraisal guidance for your transaction facts.

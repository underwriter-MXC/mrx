> This article provides general federal tax education. It does not determine whether a mineral interest is real property, whether an exchange qualifies, who the taxpayer is, how basis or gain should be calculated, or which documents should be signed. Engage a qualified tax professional, attorney, and qualified intermediary before closing.

## Answer first

**A Texas mineral-rights sale may be structured as a Section 1031 exchange only if the specific property and transaction satisfy the current federal rules.** Start by classifying the relinquished interest, confirming it was held for investment or productive business use, identifying the taxpayer, and putting the deferred-exchange structure in place before closing.

Texas law and recorded instruments help describe the property, but Texas location does not create a separate Section 1031 shortcut. The federal real-property, like-kind, timing, receipt, basis, related-party, and reporting rules still control the intended federal tax treatment.

## Classify what is actually being transferred

A mineral transaction can include more than one item: minerals in place, a royalty interest, an overriding royalty, leasehold rights, accrued royalties, receivables, equipment, or contractual rights. Do not assume every component receives the same treatment because the agreement calls the package “mineral rights.”

The [Form 8824 instructions](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i8824) describe the federal real-property framework. [IRS Publication 544](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p544) explains that qualifying property must be held for investment or productive use in a trade or business rather than primarily for sale. Give the proposed deed, lease, assignments, payment schedules, and ownership history to the tax and legal advisers before the transfer date.

## Confirm the owner and holding purpose

Write down who owns the relinquished interest today and who is expected to acquire the replacement property. Individuals, trusts, estates, partnerships, disregarded entities, and co-owners can create different taxpayer questions. Do not move title between entities merely to fit a closing schedule without professional analysis.

Also preserve evidence of why the interest was held: acquisition documents, inheritance or probate records, lease and division-order history, royalty statements, prior returns, and any business records. A long holding period alone does not answer every holding-purpose question, but a complete file gives the adviser facts to evaluate.

## Establish the structure before the sale closes

The IRS [real-estate tax tips](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/like-kind-exchanges-real-estate-tax-tips) explain that Section 1031 now applies to exchanges of real property. A deferred exchange commonly uses a qualified intermediary so the owner does not receive or control the proceeds in a way that defeats the intended treatment.

Before closing, document:

- the relinquished property and exact interest conveyed;
- the taxpayer and vesting for both sides of the exchange;
- the intermediary agreement and control of proceeds;
- the intended replacement-property criteria;
- the identification and receipt calendar; and
- the adviser responsible for each unresolved tax or legal question.

Signing an exchange agreement after taking the proceeds may be too late. Do not let an offer expiration or buyer closing date force the tax structure to be invented at the settlement table.

## Track the federal timing rules precisely

The [Form 8824 instructions](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i8824) state that replacement property in a deferred exchange generally must be identified within 45 days after the relinquished property is transferred and received within 180 days or by the tax-return due date, including extensions, if earlier.

Identification must be written and must describe the replacement property with enough specificity under the applicable rules. Record the actual transfer date, identification delivery evidence, recipient, replacement closing date, and return due date. A verbal list or internal spreadsheet is not a substitute for compliant identification.

## Separate cash and other transaction components

Money or non-like-kind property can cause current gain recognition even when another part of the exchange qualifies. Liabilities, retained cash, accrued royalties, prorations, receivables, personal-property items, and exchange expenses may affect the calculation.

Require a settlement statement that separates material components. Ask the tax professional to review draft numbers before closing rather than reconstructing them after the proceeds have moved.

## Preserve basis and reporting evidence

Section 1031 generally defers qualifying gain rather than erasing it. [IRS Publication 551](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p551) provides general basis and recordkeeping principles, while Form 8824 reports the exchange and calculates recognized and deferred amounts.

Keep acquisition and inheritance records, depletion or other basis adjustments, partial-sale allocations, improvement or transaction costs, closing statements, intermediary records, replacement-property documents, and filed returns. If the mineral interest came through an estate, give the adviser the valuation and administration records instead of assuming a basis number from a royalty statement.

## Compare the exchange with a taxable sale

Model at least two paths: a taxable sale and the proposed exchange. Compare current liquidity, estimated tax, transaction costs, replacement-property risk, concentration, expected income, management burden, and future basis. Tax deferral can be useful, but a rushed or unsuitable replacement asset can create a poor economic result.

> **Administrative source-map boundary.** This article organizes Andrews County record-search leads. It does not conduct a title search; validate an instrument; verify ownership; establish a chain of title, authority, acreage, boundaries, royalty decimals, payment rights, lease meaning, legal effect, or property connection; interpret production or development; estimate value; assess an offer or buyer; recommend a transaction; or provide legal, title, land, tax, accounting, appraisal, engineering, investment, or financial advice. Online systems may be delayed, incomplete, approximate, restricted, or organized under different fields. MRX may have an economic interest in a later transaction; when that applies, the buyer relationship is disclosed in writing before an agreement is signed.

An **Andrews County mineral rights public-record locator** is a source map, not a findings report. It tells another reviewer where you searched, what you entered, what identifier the system displayed, when you searched, what the source says about its limits, and what request comes next.

The useful local detail is administrative. Andrews County directs users from an official county page to its selected online records portal and publishes a normal posting interval. Andrews County Appraisal District uses a separate search system with different fields and a different purpose. The Texas General Land Office preserves historical land-grant references. The Railroad Commission of Texas operates regulatory query, well-record, and map systems. These sources should never be merged into one implied ownership story.

## Build the locator around eight evidence fields

Create one row for each system or record request. Preserve these fields:

- **Locator row ID:** a neutral internal label such as `COUNTY-REF-01`.
- **Official office or system:** the exact county, district, GLO, or RRC source name.
- **Access route:** the official starting page and the linked portal or query used.
- **Search field and value:** the exact field, spelling, punctuation, date range, and filter entered. Keep private owner data out of a shared locator.
- **Displayed identifier:** the instrument, property, abstract, file, API, lease, well, or other identifier exactly as shown.
- **Access and source date:** when you searched and any displayed posting, update, filing, or effective date.
- **Source limitation:** the source's stated limit plus the unresolved scope issue.
- **Result status and next request:** `lead located`, `no result located`, `access restricted`, `conflict preserved`, or `needs qualified review`, followed by the next specific request.

Do not leave an unexplained blank. Write `not displayed`, `not searched`, or `unknown` so a later reviewer can distinguish an absent field from a completed conclusion.

## Start at the Andrews County official records page

The official [Andrews County Online Official Public Records Search](https://www.co.andrews.tx.us/322/Online-Official-Public-Records-Search) page is the county-record starting point. It identifies the County Clerk as recorder and custodian of official public records and links the county-selected online portal. Preserve both URLs: the county page proves how you reached the interface, and the portal URL identifies the system actually searched.

The county page states that posting normally occurs within **two to three business days from filing**. Treat that as a normal administrative interval, not a guarantee for every instrument or a reason to assume that a recent empty search is complete. Record the search date and write `normal county posting note: 2 to 3 business days from filing` in the limitation field when timing could matter.

The same page sets a clear boundary: the County Clerk does not conduct title searches, determine validity, verify ownership, or give legal opinions. Copy that limitation into the locator. An index result is not a current-title conclusion, and an empty result is not proof that no instrument or interest exists.

The county portal may offer name, document, date, instrument, volume-and-page, or other interface fields at the time you search. Record only fields the live interface actually shows. Do not invent a grantor, grantee, date range, instrument type, or legal description to force a result. If a retained document lacks a usable field, mark the row `needs qualified review` and identify the missing reference.

## Route office questions to the County Clerk

The official [Andrews County Clerk](https://www.co.andrews.tx.us/181/County-Clerk) page identifies real-property and land records among the office's record classes and notes that some records may be closed by law. Use this row when the issue is administrative: which office holds the record, whether an older record is online, whether an index or image is available, or how to request an accessible copy.

Do not assume every record is digitized, open, indexed under the expected name, or contained in the same portal. Record the exact question asked, the office or channel used, the date, and the response locator. If access is restricted, write `access restricted` and preserve the office instruction. Do not describe restricted access as suspicious or fill the gap from another source.

The county page lists copy and certification fees, but a locator does not require a purchase. This workflow stops before ordering a paid copy. If a specific paid record later becomes necessary, its exact charge and purpose should be evaluated at that point.

## Enter Andrews CAD through the official district site

The [Andrews County Appraisal District](https://andrewscad.org/) site identifies the district's appraisal role and links its official property-search destination. Preserve the district page as the access route, then record the actual search interface separately as [Andrews CAD Property Search](https://esearch.andrewscad.org/).

At the time of review, the search interface displayed fields such as owner, street number and name, abstract, Property ID, property type, Owner ID, Geographic ID, tax year, subdivision, and related filters. Record the exact field used. If a source document supplies an abstract but no property ID, do not silently switch fields and call the returned record a match. Preserve each attempt in its own row.

The property-search interface describes its information as a research resource and warns that legal descriptions and acreage are maintained for appraisal use and should be verified before legal use. That limitation is central to the locator. A property card can be an administrative lead, but it does not prove mineral title, present ownership, a severance or reservation, net mineral acres, a royalty decimal, or the legal sufficiency of a description.

An appraisal amount is also not an MRX mineral-sale value, offer benchmark, or market conclusion. Keep any displayed value out of this locator unless it is necessary to identify the source record; even then, label it `appraisal-system display only; no mineral-sale inference`.

## Keep historical land-grant fields separate

The Texas General Land Office [Land Grant Search](https://www.glo.texas.gov/archives-heritage/search-our-collections/land-grant-search) provides historical search fields including county, abstract, original grantee, patentee, class, file number, certificate, patent dates, patent number and volume, part or section, survey, block, and township.

Use the narrowest field already supported by a retained record. Copy the displayed GLO identifier and search field exactly. A land-grant result is a historical archive locator. It does not establish the current chain of title, identify every later conveyance, reservation, probate event, or severance, or prove that a private document describes the same interest.

When several results appear plausible, keep each identifier and mark `conflict preserved`. Do not choose the result that merely shares a surname or looks geographically close. State the additional document, survey evidence, or qualified land or legal review needed to distinguish them.

## Split RRC work into query, well-record, and GIS rows

The Railroad Commission [Research Queries](https://www.rrc.texas.gov/resource-center/research/research-queries/) page is a directory to regulatory systems. Create a row for the exact system chosen, not one generic `RRC searched` row. Preserve the query name, field, date, and result identifier. The Commission describes online datasets as informational and continually updated rather than authoritative records with legal force.

The Commission's [Oil and Gas Well Records](https://www.rrc.texas.gov/oil-and-gas/research-and-statistics/obtaining-commission-records/oil-and-gas-well-records/) page explains which details help locate a file. It identifies the county, the approximate drilling date, the lease or well name and number, the operator, the field or reservoir, the Commission identifiers, and the location reference. A source may express the location reference as a survey, an abstract, a section, or a block. Record only details already supported by the source file. A well record does not establish who owns minerals, who should be paid, or whether the well is connected to the owner's tract.

Keep the [RRC Public GIS Viewer](https://www.rrc.texas.gov/resource-center/research/gis-viewer/) in its own map row. Preserve the layer, search field, popup identifier, access date, and the Commission's limitation that map data may be approximate, is informational, and has no legal force. A map point or apparent proximity is not a legal boundary, survey, tract match, well-to-interest connection, development conclusion, or value input.

## Keep the four source roles from bleeding together

| Source role             | Preserve                                                                            | Do not infer                                                               |
| ----------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| County Clerk and portal | official route, field, displayed instrument reference, search date, posting note    | validity, current title, ownership, legal effect                           |
| Andrews CAD             | official route, field, displayed property identifier, tax year, research limitation | mineral title, acreage for legal use, mineral-sale value                   |
| Texas GLO               | historical search field and land-grant identifier                                   | present ownership, tract coverage, current chain of title                  |
| Texas RRC               | exact query, regulatory identifier, record or popup locator, access date            | private property connection, payment rights, production entitlement, value |

If two systems display similar text, preserve both rows and use a neutral cross-reference such as `POSSIBLE-SAME-REFERENCE-01`. Do not merge the identifiers. A later professional conclusion should remain a separate evidence object with its own author, date, scope, and supporting records.

## Handle empty, delayed, conflicting, and restricted results

An administrative locator is most useful when it preserves failure states accurately.

- **No result located:** save the source, field, exact spelling, filters, date range, access date, and screenshot or stable locator if permitted. Do not write `no record exists`.
- **Posting delay possible:** preserve the county's normal posting note and the filing date if the source shows one. Do not predict when a particular record will appear.
- **Conflict preserved:** keep each system's identifier and describe the difference without selecting a winner.
- **Access restricted:** identify the office instruction and next lawful request. Do not attempt to bypass access controls.
- **Needs qualified review:** state the unresolved question without suggesting the answer, such as `Which complete instruments are needed to evaluate this index lead?`

The next request should be narrow. Examples include asking the clerk which index covers a stated date range, asking the appraisal district which field generated a displayed property ID, requesting a specific RRC well file by a source-provided identifier, or asking a qualified reviewer to compare complete records. Avoid open-ended requests to `confirm my minerals` because that turns an administrative search into an unsupported conclusion.

## Final quality check

The locator is ready for the next reviewer when:

- every row starts from an official county, district, GLO, or RRC route;
- the portal, query, field, spelling, filters, and access date are reproducible;
- identifiers are copied exactly and remain source-specific;
- the county posting note and each source limitation are preserved where relevant;
- no blank, delayed, restricted, or conflicting result is converted into proof;
- CAD information remains appraisal-purpose evidence rather than mineral-title or mineral-value evidence;
- GLO information remains historical and RRC information remains regulatory;
- no GIS result is described as a boundary, survey, tract match, or well-to-interest connection;
- private owner data is redacted from shared examples; and
- the locator makes no title, ownership, acreage, legal-effect, production, development, value, offer, buyer, or transaction conclusion.

A strong Andrews County public-record locator is intentionally limited. It preserves the county-specific administrative path and makes the next request traceable. That discipline helps an owner avoid repeating searches while keeping owner-specific conclusions with complete evidence and the appropriate qualified professionals.

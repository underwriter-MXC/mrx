> **Public-record boundary.** This blank worksheet documents one authorized manual SEC EDGAR search attempt. A displayed search result does not establish that a filer sent an offer, that similarly named entities are identical, or that anyone is legitimate, authorized, funded, financially capable, property-affiliated, or offering fair value. Keep real offer materials, queries, identifiers, filing references, and completed worksheets in an authorized controlled record. MRX may have an economic interest in a later transaction; when that applies, the buyer relationship is disclosed in writing before an agreement is signed.

An **SEC EDGAR mineral rights offeror filing search** can create a useful research trail only when its limits travel with the result. The worksheet on this page preserves one route, one submitted query, one set of selected controls, and one observed outcome. It does not turn a public-filing search into an identity check or an evaluation of a mineral-rights offer.

That distinction matters because a name displayed in retained correspondence and a similar name displayed in EDGAR can look connected even when the search alone supplies no reliable basis for connecting them. The worksheet keeps the retained correspondence reference, the SEC search state, the source-displayed filer fields, and any later human review in separate lanes.

## Direct answer

Create one worksheet ID for one authorized manual SEC EDGAR route and one search attempt. Copy the exact company name, ticker, or CIK from one retained offer or correspondence item into the controlled record. Record the official route, retrieval timestamp and timezone, exact submitted query, selected match mode or filters, source-displayed filer name and CIK when shown, and one selected filing row when applicable.

Record exactly one outcome: `located`, `not located`, or `unverified`. Place the limitation beside that outcome. `Located` means only that the recorded attempt displayed a result. `Not located` means only that the recorded attempt did not locate a result. `Unverified` means an access, interface, ambiguity, readability, or review limitation prevented the bounded observation.

Stop there. Do not reconcile names, decide whether a filing concerns the retained offer, infer financial capacity, judge offer quality, interpret legal effect, or recommend a transaction step.

## Use one route and one attempt

The SEC offers more than one search interface. Do not combine their fields or results into a single attempt record. The worksheet should name the exact route used, such as `SEC Search Filings: Company Search` or `SEC EDGAR Full Text Search`, and preserve the final official HTTPS route in the controlled record.

Start a separate worksheet ID or version when any of these changes:

- the official route;
- the retained offer or correspondence item;
- the exact submitted query;
- the match option or filter state;
- the retrieval timestamp or timezone;
- the displayed result state;
- the selected filing row; or
- the retained reference or source limitation.

This separation prevents a later reviewer from treating two searches as one continuous result. It also preserves the difference between what one interface displayed and what another interface might display under different controls.

It does not prove that the filer is the retained offer sender, that two names identify the same entity, or that the filer is legitimate, authorized, solvent, funded, financially capable, affiliated with a property, bound by an offer, or offering fair value.

## Record the SEC Search Filings route as displayed

The SEC [Search Filings](https://www.sec.gov/search-filings) page describes free public access to EDGAR filings. Its current company-search field accepts a name, ticker symbol, or CIK. The page also displays match options and additional search controls, and it describes access to registration statements, periodic reports, and other forms.

Those source features support a narrow provenance record. Capture the query type actually used, the exact query, the selected match option when shown, the retrieval time, and the displayed result state. If the route displays a filer name or CIK, copy it exactly into the controlled record.

Do not expand that observation. A CIK is a source-displayed identifier assigned in the SEC filing system; this worksheet does not decide whether a filer is the same person or entity named in an offer. It also does not decide what business a filer may conduct, whether the displayed record remains current, or whether that record has any relationship to the mineral interest or transaction.

## Keep full-text search in its own record

The SEC [EDGAR Full Text Search](https://www.sec.gov/edgar/search/) page currently provides a search field for keywords, ticker, company name, CIK number, or an individual's name. It also displays filing-type and other filters. Result columns can include form, filed date, reporting-for field, filing entity or person, and CIK.

If an authorized reviewer uses that route, record its exact query and selected filters in a separate worksheet. When one filing row is selected for retention, preserve only the source-displayed fields and stable filing or accession reference that are actually available. Use `when shown`, `when applicable`, `unavailable`, `not applicable`, or `unresolved` rather than filling a gap from memory or another source.

Do not use a full-text result to infer that the filing concerns the offer, property, sender, or transaction. Filing relevance requires a separate authorized review. Contract meaning, legal effect, investment analysis, and financial capacity are also outside this worksheet.

## Preserve three controlled outcomes

Use only these attempt-level outcomes:

- **Located:** the recorded route, query, and filter state displayed at least one result captured in the controlled record.
- **Not located:** the recorded route, query, and filter state did not display a result during that attempt.
- **Unverified:** the attempt could not support either bounded observation because of an access failure, unresolved interface state, ambiguous query, unreadable display, or another recorded limitation.

Do not convert `located` to `verified`, `legitimate`, `registered`, `authorized`, `funded`, `capable`, `reputable`, `safe`, or `fair`. Do not convert `not located` to `does not exist`, `unregistered`, `illegitimate`, `unauthorized`, or `fraudulent`.

The outcome is an observation about one search attempt. It is not a due-diligence conclusion and does not rank, approve, reject, validate, or invalidate an offeror or offer.

## Keep correspondence and filing evidence separate

The retained offer or correspondence item supplies the query text. It does not prove that an EDGAR result belongs to the sender. The EDGAR result supplies source-displayed filing evidence. It does not prove that the filer authored, approved, funded, or is bound by the retained offer.

Give each source its own controlled pointer. Do not paste private correspondence into a public worksheet, combine the two records into one evidence object, or create a merged identity label. If a qualified reviewer later compares names, addresses, identifiers, signatures, affiliates, filing passages, or contractual terms, that is a separate purpose with separate authority and evidence controls.

The [offer sender identity cross-check](/blog/mineral-rights-offer-sender-identity-cross-check/) explains why sender identifiers need a separate bounded process. The [offer correspondence index](/blog/mineral-rights-offer-correspondence-index/) organizes communication items without turning them into proof of identity or offer quality. This worksheet does neither job.

## Protect private and transaction data

The public template needs no completed query, person, company, identifier, filing row, offer amount, address, property description, mineral-interest detail, signature, email, phone number, or correspondence excerpt. Keep completed values in an authorized system with appropriate access controls.

Use authorized manual access and follow the official site's current access requirements. This article does not provide automated scraping, bulk collection, request-evasion, or rate-limit-workaround instructions. If repeat research or data access is needed, an authorized reviewer should choose a source-supported method and document that separate purpose.

Any training or demonstration copy should remain blank or use obviously neutral labels. Do not create a plausible fictional company, CIK, filing, or offer that could be mistaken for real evidence.

## Stop at authorized-human review

Close the worksheet with the controlled outcome, source limitation, unresolved question, and next authorized role. A useful handoff asks a bounded question, such as whether the displayed filer identity needs qualified comparison or whether a selected filing passage needs legal or financial review.

Do not complete that later analysis inside this worksheet. Identity matching, filing relevance, corporate affiliation, contractual meaning, legal effect, title review, valuation, financial-capacity analysis, offer comparison, and transaction decisions each require their own purpose, qualifications, sources, and safeguards.

This worksheet documents one manual public-filing search attempt. It does not provide legal, tax, title, appraisal, valuation, investment, financial, compliance, identity-verification, offer, or transaction advice.

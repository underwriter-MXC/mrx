A net royalty acre (NRA) is a comparison unit used to express acreage after accounting for both mineral ownership and the royalty burden. It is often normalized to a one-eighth royalty, but the convention is not universal. A number labeled “NRA” is incomplete unless the calculation explains its formula and basis.

Net revenue interest (NRI) is different. NRI is the decimal share of production revenue attributed to an owner for a particular well, unit, or property, subject to the governing records. NRA helps normalize an interest for comparison; NRI helps describe its revenue share.

## Start with net mineral acres

Net mineral acres (NMA) express an owner's fractional mineral acreage in a tract:

**Gross tract acres × mineral ownership fraction = net mineral acres**

For example, a 160-acre tract multiplied by a one-quarter mineral ownership fraction equals 40 NMA.

That calculation addresses mineral ownership, not the royalty fraction under a lease. Two owners can each hold 40 NMA but have different royalty terms. Their expected revenue shares can therefore differ.

The arithmetic does not prove ownership. The acreage and fraction need support from the chain of conveyances and other relevant records.

## How net royalty acres are commonly calculated

Under a common one-eighth normalization, the calculation is:

**NRA = NMA × (lease royalty ÷ 1/8)**

If 40 NMA is subject to a one-quarter royalty, the normalized calculation is:

**40 × (1/4 ÷ 1/8) = 80 NRA**

The result does not mean the owner gained physical acreage. It means the royalty burden is twice the one-eighth normalization basis used for this calculation.

Some offer materials use a different base or a different definition of “royalty acre.” Per-NRA figures are comparable only after the same convention is confirmed for both. Useful supporting details include the gross acres, mineral fraction, royalty fraction, normalization basis, and resulting math.

## How NRI connects ownership to revenue

For a simple, unpooled illustration, NRI can begin with:

**Mineral ownership fraction × lease royalty rate = revenue fraction**

A one-quarter mineral ownership fraction multiplied by a one-quarter lease royalty produces one-sixteenth, or 0.0625. That simplified result can change when a tract participates in a pooled unit, production is allocated among wells or tracts, or other ownership details apply.

A division order can show the decimal used by the payor. The Railroad Commission of Texas notes that division orders include the payee's fractional or decimal interest and type of interest. The division order is useful evidence of the payment setup, but it does not replace the deeds, lease, pooling records, and other documents behind the decimal.

For a producing interest, the decimal on the division order can be compared with the decimal on royalty statements. If it changes, the operator's or payor's calculation can clarify the difference.

## Why NRA and NRI matter to a value review

Ownership fractions determine how much of the relevant economics is attributed to the owner. An overstated NMA, NRA, or NRI can overstate projected revenue; an understated figure can do the opposite.

NRA can make proposals easier to compare when each uses the same normalized royalty-acre convention. NRI can connect reported production and realized prices to the owner's revenue share. Neither number, by itself, establishes mineral rights value.

A review may also consider current production, the pattern of production over time, well count, development status, location, lease terms, title confidence, and market assumptions. The [mineral rights value hub](/mineral-rights-value/) explains how those inputs work together, while the guide on [how mineral rights are valued](/blog/how-are-mineral-rights-valued/) explains the broader review process.

## Records that support the calculation

The records can be organized by the question each one can help answer:

- **What tract is involved?** Deed descriptions, plats, and county records.
- **What mineral fraction is owned?** Deeds, probate records, reservations, and conveyances.
- **What royalty burden applies?** The lease and relevant amendments.
- **What decimal is being paid?** Division orders and royalty statements.

Pooling or unit records can also be relevant. Public regulator data can help identify wells and production, but it generally does not establish private ownership fractions.

If the records conflict, a per-acre proposal is not a settled calculation. A qualified land or title professional may be needed to analyze the governing documents for the specific property.

## Compare proposals on the same denominator

When a proposal cites NMA, NRA, or NRI, a written conversion can show the denominator being used. It can also identify whether the stated amount is total consideration or a per-acre figure and whether closing adjustments can change the recognized acres or decimal after review.

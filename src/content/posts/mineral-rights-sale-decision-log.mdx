> **Educational recordkeeping boundary.** This article provides an organizational template. It does not determine title, ownership, acreage, royalty, authority, document validity, recordability, contract meaning, value, offer fairness, expected owner net, tax treatment, whether to sell, or any transaction outcome. Use qualified legal, title, tax, accounting, appraisal, land, engineering, and closing professionals for owner-specific conclusions. MRX may have an economic interest in a later transaction; when that applies, the buyer relationship is disclosed in writing before an agreement is signed.

A mineral rights sale decision log is a dated audit trail that tells you what changed, which record supports the change, which question remains open, who owns the next action, and which later entry supersedes an earlier one. It should link to the original evidence without pretending to replace that evidence.

The log is useful when emails, calls, public records, owner documents, offer revisions, and professional questions begin arriving at different times. Its job is organizational: keep one process history readable without turning a note into a title opinion, contract interpretation, valuation, or sell recommendation.

## A decision log is not the same as a document folder

A document folder stores the artifacts: deeds, leases, probate papers, royalty statements, tax records, maps, public-data exports, written proposals, correspondence, and professional work. The decision log stores the relationship among those artifacts.

For example, the folder may contain three proposal PDFs. The log should show when each arrived, the filename and version, the property scope stated by its sender, whether it replaced an earlier version, which separate comparison used it, and any unresolved question. The log does not decide which proposal is better.

This distinction keeps the file inspectable:

- the original artifact shows what the source actually said;
- the log shows how the process used or questioned it;
- a working note may summarize it;
- a qualified professional conclusion remains labeled as that professional's conclusion; and
- a later correction does not erase the earlier history.

## Start with a stable log header

Create the log before adding individual events. The header should define the organizational subject so entries do not drift across properties or decisions.

Include:

1. **Log name and owner.** Identify the person responsible for maintaining the record.
2. **Property scope.** Record the available county, tract, legal-description reference, lease, unit, well, depth, formation, and substance identifiers. Mark the source of each item.
3. **Interest under discussion.** Label the interest type and whether any acreage, royalty, or decimal is owner-reported, document-reported, third-party reported, assumed, conflicted, or professionally confirmed.
4. **Decision stage.** Use a neutral stage such as collecting records, organizing questions, reviewing written proposals, professional review pending, or closed.
5. **Created and last-updated times.** Include a time zone when timing may matter.
6. **Artifact directory.** Link to the controlled folder that holds originals and working copies.
7. **Confidentiality rule.** State where private identifiers may be stored and which details must be removed before sharing.

The header is not a representation to a buyer or a public filing. It is the boundary for the owner's own process record.

## Give every entry the same fields

Consistency matters more than elaborate software. A spreadsheet, database, or secured table can work when every entry uses the same field set.

### Entry identity

Assign an entry ID, event date, entry-created date, and responsible person. A simple sequence such as `DL-001`, `DL-002`, and `DL-003` is easier to cite than “the note from last week.”

### Event and source

Write one factual sentence describing the event: a record arrived, a scope changed, a question was raised, a revised document replaced an earlier version, or a professional referral was requested. Record who supplied the information and how it arrived.

### Artifact reference

Link to the preserved original and record its filename, format, document date if shown, received date, and version label. Keep a working copy separate. If the source has no formal version number, create a neutral receipt label instead of altering the original file.

### Evidence class

Classify the entry as owner-reported, source-document statement, public-data observation, third-party statement, analytical assumption, qualified professional conclusion, or unresolved conflict. Do not collapse those categories into “confirmed.”

### Affected question

Name the question that the entry changes or leaves open. Examples include property scope, identity matching, document completeness, offer-version identity, authority referral, or closing-record receipt. The field identifies a workflow question, not its legal or economic answer.

### Status, owner, and next action

Use a small status set: received, review pending, clarification requested, professional referral pending, superseded, resolved by identified source, or closed without conclusion. Assign one responsible person and one next action. If there is no action, record why.

### Supersession link

When a new record changes an earlier entry, point both ways: the new entry supersedes `DL-004`, and `DL-004` is superseded by the new entry. Never silently rewrite the old row.

## Use a fictional example to test the structure

Suppose an owner receives a revised written proposal after clarifying that one tract should not be included. A bounded log entry might read:

- **Entry:** DL-012
- **Event:** Revised proposal received after written scope clarification
- **Source:** Sender identified in the preserved email
- **Artifact:** `proposal-received-02.pdf`; original preserved; working copy separate
- **Evidence class:** Third-party statement and document version
- **Affected question:** Which written proposal version is current for the separate comparison workflow?
- **Status:** Review pending
- **Responsible person:** Log owner
- **Next action:** Confirm the comparison uses the revised scope and preserve the prior version as superseded
- **Does not conclude:** price fairness, contract meaning, title, expected owner net, or whether to sell

This example records a process change. It does not evaluate the proposal.

## Record public data with its limits attached

Public data should have enough retrieval context for another reader to understand what was observed. Record the URL or system, query fields, reporting period, access date, exported filename, and stated source limitation.

The [Railroad Commission of Texas research-query page](https://www.rrc.texas.gov/resource-center/research/research-queries/) provides production, well, permit, operator, and other informational queries. It also says the data sets are continually updated, are not intended as authoritative public records, and have no legal force or effect. A log entry can preserve the query and access context without promoting that result into private ownership, title, acreage, reserves, or value.

The [Texas Secretary of State filing options](https://www.sos.state.tx.us/corp/options.shtml) describe services for searching business entities, obtaining entity information, and ordering records. A decision log can preserve the exact legal name searched, source, access date, and result type. That is an identity record, not an endorsement of a counterparty or a conclusion about financial capacity or transaction quality.

Texas [Property Code Chapter 12](https://statutes.capitol.texas.gov/?artSec=12.001&chapter=PR.12&code=PR&tab=1) addresses recording instruments concerning property. A private decision log can record whether a copy was received or whether a recording reference was reported, but it cannot determine whether an instrument is valid, recordable, effective, or sufficient for a particular transaction. Those questions require the actual instrument and qualified review.

## Keep version rules simple and strict

Use five rules:

1. Preserve the received original without renaming its contents or overwriting it.
2. Give every working copy a visible version label and creation date.
3. Record who made a change and why.
4. Mark a version as superseded without deleting it from the history.
5. Link every summary or decision entry to the exact artifact version it used.

These rules prevent a later conversation from relying on a different draft than the one actually reviewed. They do not determine which version is binding or legally operative.

## Add escalation states instead of guessing

The most useful log entry may be “professional conclusion required.” Use that state when the open question concerns title, deed meaning, contract obligations, signer authority, tax treatment, accounting, an independent appraisal, land records, engineering, or closing controls.

The current [MRX methodology](/methodology/) separates records, assumptions, scenarios, and limitations in a directional review. The [MRX FAQ](/faq/) and [terms](/terms/) also distinguish educational and directional work from professional valuation, title, legal, and tax conclusions. Carry those boundaries into the log: record the referral, the specific question, the professional's identified work product when received, and which earlier assumption it affects.

Do not paste sensitive personal, banking, tax, or identity data into a broadly shared log. Store the protected original in an appropriate secure location and link only the minimum reference needed for the workflow.

## Key takeaways

- Build one stable header and use the same field set for every entry.
- Preserve originals; keep working copies and summaries separate.
- Label reported facts, source observations, assumptions, conflicts, and professional conclusions distinctly.
- Add corrections and superseding entries instead of rewriting history.
- Record a responsible person and next action for every open item.
- Keep title, legal, tax, appraisal, engineering, fairness, and sell decisions outside the log itself.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is a mineral rights sale decision log?

It is a dated index of scope changes, evidence received, document versions, open questions, referrals, decisions, responsible people, and next actions. It points to the underlying records but does not replace them.

### Should I edit an old entry when new information arrives?

Preserve the old entry and add a dated correction or superseding entry. That keeps the history visible and prevents a later reader from mistaking a changed assumption for the original record.

### Does a decision log prove mineral ownership or acreage?

No. It can record an owner report, document statement, public-data result, or qualified professional conclusion as separate evidence types, but the log itself does not establish title, acreage, royalty, or authority.

### Can the log tell me whether an offer is fair?

No. It can identify which offer version and scope were reviewed and link to a separate comparison, but it does not calculate value, expected owner net, fairness, or whether an owner should sell.

### Who should receive the log?

Share only the relevant portion with the appropriate participant. Remove private identifiers that are not needed, use secure channels, and ask qualified professionals what records they need for the specific question.

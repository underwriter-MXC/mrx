> **Educational contact-protocol boundary.** This article describes a voluntary administrative role after a family has decided to receive mineral-rights inquiries. Naming a point person does not determine ownership, title, acreage, authority, consent, agency, fiduciary status, negotiation power, signature rights, legal capacity, value, tax treatment, or whether anyone should lease or sell. A power of attorney, trust, entity record, estate instrument, court order, contract, or other legally operative authority is a separate matter for qualified counsel and the actual documents. MRX may have an economic interest in a later transaction; when that applies, the buyer relationship is disclosed in writing before an agreement is signed.

A mineral rights family point person is one person who receives inquiries, preserves the original messages, acknowledges receipt without making a commitment, and routes the same information to the appropriate family members.

The role can reduce duplicated calls and conflicting administrative replies. It does not make that person the family’s decision-maker, negotiator, spokesperson on substantive terms, owner, legal agent, or signer.

This protocol begins **after** the family has decided that it is willing to receive inquiries. If the family has not reached that point, start with the separate guide on [how to talk to your family before deciding whether to sell mineral rights](/blog/how-to-talk-to-your-family-before-you-decide-to-sell-mineral-rights/).

## Define the role in one sentence

Before choosing a person, agree on a narrow role description:

> “The family point person may receive, preserve, acknowledge, and circulate mineral-rights inquiries. The point person may not accept terms, state another person’s decision, disclose private records, negotiate, sign, or claim authority for anyone.”

That sentence gives the role a positive job and a visible edge. “Handle everything” is not a usable description. It can encourage senders to treat convenience as authority and relatives to assume that someone else is reviewing substantive questions.

University of Minnesota Extension [transition-planning guidance](https://extension.umn.edu/transfer-and-estate-planning/preparing-meet-your-transition-and-estate-planning-team) recommends preparing goals and questions in writing and involving affected parties. The source concerns farm and estate transitions, not mineral-rights law. Its useful practice here is limited: write down the purpose of the role and who needs the information before assigning the task.

## Choose for reliability, not seniority

The oldest relative, largest apparent owner, person holding the records, or family member who first received the letter is not automatically the best point person. Choose someone who can consistently perform the administrative job.

Look for six qualities:

1. **Responsive.** The person can acknowledge a message within the family’s chosen time window.
2. **Organized.** The person preserves the original email, letter, voicemail, or text instead of relying on memory.
3. **Neutral.** The person can forward information without editing it to favor one family position.
4. **Careful with private information.** The person does not send sensitive records merely because someone asks.
5. **Comfortable with boundaries.** The person can say, “I can route that question, but I cannot answer it for another owner.”
6. **Available to hand off.** The person will use a backup when traveling, ill, overwhelmed, or personally involved in a disagreement.

Ask the candidate whether they want the task. A reluctant contact who agrees only from family pressure is unlikely to provide consistent routing. The role can be rotated or replaced without deciding anything about ownership or legal authority.

## Separate four allowed actions from four prohibited actions

The family can keep the protocol simple by using two short lists.

The point person may:

- receive an inquiry through a designated phone number, email address, or mailing address;
- preserve the original communication and any attachments exactly as received;
- send a neutral acknowledgment that creates no commitment; and
- forward the same source message to the family distribution list or the specific people identified by the family.

The point person may not, merely because of this role:

- state that a person owns an interest, must agree, has agreed, or will sign;
- accept or reject a lease, sale, option, exclusivity request, confidentiality term, deadline, or other proposal;
- provide an account number, taxpayer identifier, bank detail, signature copy, identity document, password, one-time code, or other sensitive record; or
- interpret a deed, lease, division order, probate record, trust, offer, purchase agreement, tax issue, or title question.

These are role boundaries, not conclusions about what the law permits in a particular family. If someone needs to act for another person, qualified counsel should review the actual facts and any legally operative instrument.

## Use one designated intake channel

Choose one primary channel the point person can manage reliably. A dedicated email address often preserves the original text and attachments more clearly than relayed phone calls. A phone number may still be useful, but important requests should be requested in writing.

Tell relatives which channel to use when they receive a direct inquiry. They can forward the original message to the point person without answering substantive questions. The family does not need to publish the point person’s personal contact information broadly. Use a channel appropriate to the family’s privacy and security needs.

The [Utah State University Extension Estate Planning Toolkit](https://extension.usu.edu/finance/files/estate-planning-toolkit.pdf) recommends setting a clear intention, considering people directly affected, using an agenda, and recognizing that more than one conversation may be needed. That source is not mineral-specific. Here, the same discipline supports a clear handoff: everyone should know why the channel exists, what enters it, who receives the forwarded message, and which questions stop the administrative response.

## Send a neutral acknowledgment

The first reply should confirm receipt without accepting a deadline, validating the sender, or suggesting that the point person speaks for every owner.

A practical acknowledgment is:

> “Thank you. I received your inquiry and will circulate the original message to the appropriate family members. This acknowledgment is not an acceptance, decision, authorization, or commitment by any person. Please send your full name, organization, callback information, the property reference as you understand it, and your request in writing.”

Do not add “we are interested,” “the family agrees,” “I can approve this,” or “send the contract to me” unless the relevant people and qualified advisers have separately established what may be said and by whom. The point person’s safest default is accurate receipt and routing.

The American Association of Professional Landmen [Code of Ethics and Standards of Practice](https://www.landman.org/discover-the-land-profession/about-us/code-of-ethic.html) describes expectations for AAPL members that include fair dealing, accurate public representations, confidentiality, competence, and disclosure of certain interests. The code governs participating AAPL members, not every caller and not the family contact. It provides a bounded reason to expect a land professional to identify the person and organization involved and communicate accurately. It does not prove that a particular sender follows the code or authorize the point person to act.

## Forward the original, then add a short cover note

Forward the source message unchanged whenever practical. Above it, add only the context needed for family members to understand why it is arriving:

- when and through which channel it arrived;
- the sender’s name and organization as stated;
- the property, owner, lease, well, or county reference as stated;
- what the sender is asking for; and
- any stated response date, labeled as the sender’s statement rather than an accepted deadline.

Do not rewrite the inquiry into a more favorable or alarming version. Do not add a valuation opinion, recommendation, family vote, or interpretation. If a voicemail must be summarized, label it as a summary and preserve the audio when permitted and practical.

This cover note is not a decision log or document-package index. It exists only to route one original inquiry with enough context for the recipients to recognize it.

## Give every recipient the same source message

Use a family-approved distribution list. When the same information concerns several people, distribute the same original rather than giving each person a different paraphrase. If privacy or legal considerations require different access, qualified advice and the controlling records should determine the appropriate recipients.

University of Minnesota Extension [farm-business transfer guidance](https://extension.umn.edu/transfer-and-estate-planning/farm-business-transfer-strategies) recommends brief, specific communication that avoids accusation, insult, and blame. It is not a mineral-rights rule. It supports a simple administrative standard here: forward what was received, identify the question, and avoid commentary about another person’s motives.

The point person can gather administrative questions such as “Which tract does this concern?” or “Can the sender provide the request in writing?” Substantive questions about ownership, authority, price, lease terms, deed scope, tax, or whether to proceed belong in separate family and professional workflows.

## Keep legal authority separate from contact convenience

Texas Law Help’s [powers-of-attorney fact sheet](https://texaslawhelp.org/article/powers-of-attorney-fact-sheet) explains that a power of attorney is a legal document that authorizes one person to act for another and that its powers may be broad or limited. That is fundamentally different from asking a relative to receive and forward messages.

An informal point-person protocol should therefore say what it does **not** do. It does not create a power of attorney, agency relationship, fiduciary duty, ownership interest, voting rule, consent, negotiation authority, or signature power. It does not change a deed, trust, estate, entity, contract, court order, or other controlling record.

Do not assume that the point person may act for someone who is unavailable or incapacitated. Do not assume that family agreement cures an authority problem. Qualified counsel must review any owner-specific authority question using the actual documents and applicable law.

## Create a backup without creating two voices

Choose one backup contact and define when the handoff begins. Examples include a planned absence, no acknowledgment within the family’s time window, illness, a conflict involving the primary contact, or an explicit family reset.

When the backup takes over, send one administrative notice:

> “For current inquiry routing, please use [backup channel]. This change concerns message receipt only and does not state anyone’s decision or authority.”

Avoid having the primary and backup send parallel replies. One intake channel at a time makes it easier to preserve the original and reduces contradictory administrative messages.

## Use stop rules for substantive or sensitive requests

The point person should pause the reply and route the matter when an inquiry asks for:

- a verbal or written acceptance;
- confirmation that every owner agrees;
- a signature, initials, notarization, identification document, or tax form;
- banking information, payment credentials, passwords, or one-time codes;
- an unredacted deed, probate, trust, tax, royalty, or identity record without a verified need and secure channel;
- a legal, title, tax, valuation, engineering, geological, or investment conclusion;
- a decision on price, property scope, lease terms, sale terms, exclusivity, confidentiality, or a deadline; or
- a statement that the point person has authority to bind another person.

The broader [seller-control guide](/blog/selling-mineral-rights-selling-process-for-mineral-owners/) explains why owners should keep written proposals, agreements, deeds, payment, delivery, and recording as separate transaction states. The point-person protocol stops before that process. Its job is only to get the original inquiry to the right people without manufacturing a commitment.

## Reset the protocol when the role expands

If the point person begins making substantive replies, stop and reset the role before more communications go out.

The family can:

1. preserve the messages already sent and received;
2. tell the sender that administrative contact did not create acceptance or authority;
3. identify which question exceeded the role;
4. route any possible reliance, contract, agency, authority, or legal concern to qualified counsel;
5. restate the allowed and prohibited actions; and
6. keep the current contact, appoint the backup, or choose someone else.

Do not delete an uncomfortable message or silently rewrite the history. Preservation supports accurate professional review without converting this contact protocol into a formal decision or communication log.

## A fictional example

Suppose four adult relatives have already agreed that they are willing to receive written inquiries about a mineral interest. They choose Elena because she checks email daily, can forward source messages without commentary, and is comfortable refusing substantive questions.

A land professional emails Elena with a property reference and asks whether the family would consider a sale. Elena preserves the email and sends the neutral acknowledgment. She forwards the original to the family distribution list with the sender’s name, organization, property reference, request, and stated response date.

The sender then asks Elena to confirm that every relative owns an equal share and will sign. Elena does not answer. She says she can route the question but cannot state ownership, authority, consent, or signature intentions for anyone. The family sends the actual ownership and authority question to qualified counsel.

Elena performed the point-person job successfully. She kept one channel orderly without turning message receipt into a family decision or legal authorization.

## Frequently asked questions

### What does a mineral rights family point person do?

The point person receives inquiries through a designated channel, preserves the original message, confirms receipt without making a commitment, and routes the same information to the appropriate family members. The role is administrative, not a sale or signing role.

### Who should the family choose as its point person?

Choose someone who is responsive, organized, neutral, careful with private information, willing to circulate the same facts to everyone, and comfortable saying that ownership, authority, value, legal, tax, and decision questions require separate review.

### Does the point person have authority to negotiate or sign?

Not merely because the family uses that person as a contact. An informal contact protocol does not establish ownership, agency, consent, fiduciary status, negotiation authority, or signing power. Qualified counsel should review any legally operative authority for the actual people, interests, and documents.

### What should the point person say when an inquiry arrives?

A neutral acknowledgment can state that the message was received, that the point person will circulate it, and that no decision or commitment is being made. It can also request the sender’s full name, organization, callback information, property reference, and the request in writing.

### What if the point person starts making decisions for the family?

Pause outgoing replies, preserve the communications, tell the sender that prior administrative contact did not create a commitment, and have the family restate or replace the protocol. Route any ownership, authority, contract, consent, or reliance concern to qualified counsel.

## Keep one contact and many independent decisions

The best family point person makes communication more accurate without becoming the source of anyone else’s decision. Define the role, choose for reliability, use one intake channel, preserve originals, route the same information, and stop whenever a request becomes substantive, sensitive, or legally operative.

> **Source-separated research boundary.** This article explains how to document four Reeves County public-record searches without merging them. It does not determine mineral ownership, title, acreage, value, taxes owed, lease status, well ownership, property connection, legal effect, or whether to sell. It does not authenticate a person or record. Keep real names, account numbers, instrument references, screenshots, and private documents in an authorized controlled location. MRX may have an economic interest in a later transaction; when that applies, the buyer relationship is disclosed in writing before an agreement is signed.

A **Reeves County mineral records search** log is useful only when each source stays in its own lane. The Reeves County Clerk property-record portal, Reeves appraisal or tax search, Texas General Land Office, and Railroad Commission of Texas do not answer the same question. A similar name, number, survey label, or location across them is not permission to merge the records.

The deliverable is four reproducible source rows. Each row preserves what was searched, where, when, which field and privacy-safe value were used, what reference the interface displayed, where the evidence is retained, which bounded result applies, and which narrow source-specific question remains.

## Prepare one controlled reference before searching

Begin with one Reeves County reference from a record you are authorized to use. It may be a person or entity name variant, instrument number, account reference, survey or abstract label, lease or well identifier, or another field actually accepted by the selected official interface.

Do not publish the real input merely to demonstrate the worksheet. Give it a neutral working ID such as `RC-SEARCH-01` in the public log and keep the source-entered input in a restricted crosswalk. Preserve spelling, punctuation, spacing, leading zeros, and any source label exactly. If the input changes, start a new attempt instead of silently editing the prior row.

One reference may not fit every source's available fields. Record the field the interface actually offers. Do not force an instrument number into an owner-name box or rewrite a survey reference to make it resemble an account number. If the route cannot accept the controlled reference, the observation remains `unverified` rather than an invented search.

## Use the Reeves County Clerk row for index leads

The current [Reeves County Clerk Official Records Search](https://reeves.tx.ds.search.govos.com/) identifies itself as the County of Reeves property-record route. Its current quick-search interface describes searches for grantor or grantee, subdivision, document type, or document number and offers recorded-date controls.

Record the exact final portal URL, access time and timezone, selected search scope, field, controlled value, date range if used, and the displayed instrument reference when one appears. Keep an authorized evidence pointer to the result page or retained observation.

Treat the output only as an index lead. `Record located` means the interface displayed a result matching the recorded search criterion closely enough to preserve for the next review. It does not mean the complete image was reviewed, the record concerns the intended tract, the instrument conveyed minerals, the named person is the same person, or the instrument has a particular legal effect.

Do not pay for an image, copy, certification, subscription, or cart item to complete this public workflow. If a later task genuinely requires a purchase, separate that decision from the search log.

## Keep the appraisal or tax row separate

The [Texas Comptroller Reeves County directory](https://comptroller.texas.gov/taxes/property-tax/county-directory/reeves.php) identifies the county's current appraisal-district contact and website. The linked [Reeves County property search](https://www.reevescountytax.org/search) currently presents search choices such as account number, owner name, owner address, and property location.

Record only the field and value actually tested, access timestamp, displayed account reference or property-type label when available, evidence pointer, bounded result, and next appraisal-source question. Do not import a County Clerk party, instrument number, or legal conclusion into the CAD row simply because a displayed owner or description looks familiar.

An appraisal or tax observation does not prove current mineral title, a royalty decimal, net mineral acres, a lease interest, an amount owed, or a market value. Keep any displayed appraisal or tax value out of a public example. Questions about appraisal records belong with the appraisal district; title and legal questions belong with qualified professionals reviewing the actual instruments.

## Use the GLO row only for original land-grant context

The [Texas General Land Office Mineral Rights FAQ](https://www.glo.texas.gov/sites/default/files/2025-01/Minerals%20FAQ_updated%202023.pdf) explains that the GLO keeps original land-grant files, while later conveyance records are usually found in the county deed records kept by the county clerk. It also cautions that determining mineral ownership requires examination of the deeds and leases affecting the claim.

That division defines the GLO row. Record the GLO route, access timestamp, exact land-grant or survey field tested, displayed archival reference if any, controlled evidence pointer, result label, and next GLO-specific retrieval question.

Do not convert an original-grant reference, grantee name, abstract, survey label, or map observation into a present ownership conclusion. Do not carry a GLO name into the Clerk or CAD row as an identity match. `No record displayed` for one attempted GLO field does not rule out a different spelling, collection, index, or county record.

## Use the RRC row only for regulator-hosted oil-and-gas data

The [Railroad Commission Public GIS Viewer page](https://www.rrc.texas.gov/resource-center/research/gis-viewer/) describes a map route for oil, gas, and pipeline data and links current viewer guidance. Its records and map features serve a regulator-data role that is different from a county instrument index, appraisal account, or original land-grant file.

Record the RRC route, access time and timezone, selected tool or field, exact controlled value, displayed API, lease, survey, or feature reference when one appears, evidence pointer, result label, and one next RRC-specific question. Preserve any viewer limitation or route problem in the row.

An RRC result does not prove that the controlled person or entity owns a well, lease, royalty, or tract. A map feature does not resolve title, acreage, a payment decimal, lease status, or a property connection. Keep those questions outside the search log.

## Apply four bounded result labels

Use the same labels across all four rows, but interpret each only within its source:

- **Record located:** the official route displayed a reference that met the documented search criterion closely enough to retain as a lead.
- **No record displayed:** that exact route, field, value, and timestamp produced no displayed result.
- **Route unavailable:** the official destination could not be reached or completed during the recorded attempt.
- **Unverified:** source identity, input, interface behavior, retained evidence, or result meaning was insufficient to support another label.

`No record displayed` never means `no record exists`. `Record located` never means `same property`, `same person`, `owned`, `valid`, `active`, `taxable`, `payable`, or `valuable`. The label belongs to one source row and one search attempt.

## Close each row without merging the sources

A source row is complete when another authorized reviewer can reproduce the route, timestamp, field, value, displayed reference, evidence pointer, bounded result, and next question. The four rows do not need to agree. Differences are expected because the systems have different custodians, fields, dates, and purposes.

Finish by checking that no sentence turns the combined rows into conclusions about ownership, title, tax, lease, acreage, value, property connection, or legal effect. The search log organizes leads and limitations. Qualified title, legal, land, appraisal, tax, engineering, and valuation review remain separate jobs.

## Understanding Our Valuation Methodology: Ensuring Fairness and Accuracy in Your Mineral Rights Review

> Our mineral rights valuation methodology combines discounted cash flow analysis, comparable-transactions review, and royalty income multiple checks, all built on public production data from the Texas Railroad Commission and the U.S. Energy Information Administration. The output is a defensible value range, not a single point estimate, and every input is documented so the math is auditable.

### Key Takeaways

- **Three methods, one conclusion**: The valuation methodology cross-checks DCF, comparable transactions, and a royalty income multiple review. A defensible range is the intersection where all three converge.
- **Public production data is the foundation**: Well-by-well production comes from the [Texas Railroad Commission](https://www.rrc.texas.gov/) and the [EIA Texas state profile](https://www.eia.gov/state/?id=TX). Owner-supplied documents sharpen precision; public data is the floor.
- **Inputs are not opinions**: The discount rate, decline curve, price deck, and multiple selection are sourced from observable transactions, not negotiated between buyer and seller.
- **Royalty vs. working interest vs. net mineral acres**: Federal property law under [26 USC § 614](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/614) distinguishes these interests, and the methodology handles each separately.
- **The published methodology lists and weights every input**: The [MRX published methodology](/methodology/) names the factors the review weighs, production history, decline-curve context, royalty terms, risk-adjusted discount rate, operator quality, commodity price assumptions, title, and offer terms, and shows the influence each one has on the resulting range.

## What the Valuation Methodology Is

The [MRX valuation methodology](/methodology/) is the published set of procedures, inputs, and cross-checks that produces the value range delivered to mineral rights owners. It is built on three independent methods that converge on the same answer.

- **Discounted cash flow (DCF).** Forecasted production is multiplied by a price deck and discounted back to present value at a market-clearing discount rate.
- **Comparable transactions.** Recent sales of similar interests in the same county and formation provide a market-based price per net mineral acre.
- **Royalty income multiple.** The trailing monthly royalty income is multiplied by a market range, then cross-checked against the DCF and comps.

The single number you receive is a range, not a point, and is supported by all three methods. If the methods diverge, the divergence is documented and explained in the deliverable.

Every input is a public record or an owner-supplied document. The methodology does not use a buyer-controlled pricing model.

Owner-supplied documents (lease, division order, royalty checks) are used to confirm what public data implies. If the two agree, the review is precise. If they diverge, the divergence is investigated before the value range is finalized.

## How DCF Drives the Valuation

The DCF approach is a standard technique in finance and energy valuation. For each well within the area of interest, the methodology:

1. Pulls monthly production from the [RRC public records](https://www.rrc.texas.gov/).
2. Fits a decline curve to the historical production.
3. Forecasts remaining recoverable volume using the fitted decline.
4. Multiplies the volume by a long-term price deck.
5. Subtracts severance tax, operating costs, and the operator's working interest share.
6. Discounts the resulting net income stream back to present value at a market-clearing discount rate.

The DCF is run for each interest, then summed to a total value range. The discount rate is selected from comparable transactions; the price deck is documented and held constant across similar reviews.

## How Comparable Transactions Drive the Valuation

The comparable-transactions method looks at recent sales of similar interests in the same county and formation. The methodology does not use a national average; it uses county-level data where available, basin-level data where county-level data is sparse, and a documented fallback when neither is available.

The output is a market-based price per net mineral acre. The price per acre is then multiplied by the net mineral acres in the subject interest to produce a comparable-transaction value range.

## How the Royalty Income Multiple Check Works

The royalty income multiple is the simplest of the three methods and the most overlooked. It is also the most useful sanity check.

For a producing royalty interest, the methodology divides the trailing monthly average royalty income into the offer or the valuation result. The result is the implied multiple. The published methodology uses the multiple as a cross-check that the DCF and comparable-transactions methods are producing a market-consistent answer; the methodology does not state a fixed market range.

The multiple check is not a substitute for DCF or comps. It is a cross-check that confirms the other two methods are producing a market-consistent answer.

## How the Three Methods Converge

The deliverable shows the three methods side-by-side.

- DCF range: $X-$Y (price per net mineral acre)
- Comparable-transactions range: $X-$Y
- Royalty multiple check: implied multiple of Zx

If the three ranges overlap, the deliverable reports the overlapping range as the defensible value range. If they diverge, the deliverable documents the divergence, explains which method is most applicable for the subject interest, and identifies the inputs that produced the divergence.

The owner sees the math, not just the answer.

## What the Methodology Does Not Claim

The methodology is precise about what it does and does not promise.

- **It does not promise a specific sale price.** The output is a range, and the actual transaction price depends on counterparty negotiation, due diligence, and title.
- **It is not a third-party professional appraisal report prepared under applicable standards.** For court, tax, or estate contexts where such a report is required, the methodology is not a substitute. Engage a state-licensed appraiser.
- **It is not legal or tax guidance.** For title, probate, contract, or tax questions, the next step is a qualified attorney or CPA.
- **It does not project a specific closing date.** Closing depends on title, operator consent, and buyer financing.

These boundaries are documented in the deliverable and on the [MRX methodology page](/methodology/).

## Why the Methodology Is Built on Public Data

Public data is verifiable. A buyer who claims a different value for the same interest has to defend the claim against the same public production numbers. The methodology is designed so that an owner can take the deliverable to any qualified buyer and ask the buyer to point to the specific input that produces a different number.

This is the difference between an opinion and a market analysis. The methodology produces a market analysis.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is discounted cash flow (DCF) in mineral rights valuation?

DCF is a standard finance technique that forecasts the future royalty income from a well, discounts each year's income back to present value at a market-clearing discount rate, and sums the discounted cash flows to produce a present value. It is one of three methods used in the [MRX methodology](/methodology/), and it is the most detailed of the three because it builds the forecast from public production data.

### What is a royalty income multiple?

A royalty income multiple is the ratio of the value of a royalty interest to its trailing monthly average royalty income. The [MRX published methodology](/methodology/) uses the multiple as one of three cross-checks to confirm the DCF and comparable-transactions methods are producing a market-consistent answer.

### What is the difference between royalty interest, working interest, and net mineral acres?

A royalty interest is the percentage of production revenue the owner receives free of operating costs. A working interest is the percentage of production revenue the owner receives net of operating costs. Net mineral acres are the underlying unit of ownership, calculated as the gross acres times the royalty or working interest percentage. Federal property law under [26 USC § 614](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/614) defines these interests for tax purposes.

### Where does the production data come from?

Production data is pulled from the [Texas Railroad Commission](https://www.rrc.texas.gov/) and the [EIA Texas state profile](https://www.eia.gov/state/?id=TX). The RRC maintains well-by-well production records; the EIA provides the basin-level production context used for the Texas basin productivity differential within the [MRX methodology](/methodology/).

### Is the valuation a third-party professional appraisal report prepared under applicable standards?

No. The valuation is a market analysis built on public data and the published methodology. It is not a third-party professional appraisal report prepared under applicable standards, and it is not a substitute for one in any legal, tax, or estate context where such a report is required.

### How accurate is the valuation?

The valuation is accurate to the extent the inputs are accurate. The methodology uses public production data and owner-supplied documents, both of which can be verified. The output is a range, not a single point estimate, and the range is documented with the inputs that produced it. The accuracy of any specific transaction depends on the negotiation, due diligence, and title that follow.

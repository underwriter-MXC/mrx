## Answer first

Texas mineral-rights valuation and offer review should begin with clear questions about the interest, records, assumptions, and written terms involved. Public RRC information can add reported operational context, but it does not prove ownership, establish title, or determine what an offer should be.

## Identify the property and interest

Ask what property and mineral interest a valuation discussion or offer covers. The Texas Comptroller's [Property Classification Guide](https://comptroller.texas.gov/taxes/property-tax/docs/96-313.pdf) distinguishes surface and oil-and-gas/mineral interests for classification purposes. It is not a title opinion, ownership determination, or appraisal for a particular owner.

## Understand what public RRC records can and cannot show

The Texas RRC's [Oil and Gas Production Data](https://www.rrc.texas.gov/oil-and-gas/research-and-statistics/production-data/) and [Well Information](https://www.rrc.texas.gov/oil-and-gas/research-and-statistics/well-information/) resources provide reported production and public well-information context. They can help you ask which wells, dates, or reported production records were considered in a discussion.

That context does not resolve title or ownership questions, and it does not establish a required transaction value. Treat it as a starting point for questions, not a conclusion about an offer.

## Compare records, assumptions, and written terms

When reviewing more than one offer or valuation discussion, compare:

- The property and interest identified in each document.
- The records and reporting periods considered.
- Assumptions, missing information, and potential changes.
- Written contingencies, adjustments, and deadlines.
- The boundaries of the discussion and any questions for outside professionals.

This approach avoids labels and unsupported claims about a buyer's motives. It focuses instead on information an owner can request and review.

## Keep professional boundaries clear

An offer review is not a legal opinion, tax conclusion, title opinion, or an appraisal. If the decision depends on any of those matters, seek the appropriate qualified professional with the records relevant to your situation.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can RRC production data confirm ownership?

No. It provides operator-reported production context, not title evidence or an ownership opinion.

### Can public well information determine a sale price?

No. It can provide well-status context, but it does not establish a dollar value or a required price.

### Does the Comptroller guide decide whether an interest is mineral or surface?

No. It describes classification categories and does not determine the facts or ownership of a particular property.

### What should I review in an offer?

Review the property and interest described, the stated assumptions, written terms, contingencies, adjustments, and questions that require outside advice.

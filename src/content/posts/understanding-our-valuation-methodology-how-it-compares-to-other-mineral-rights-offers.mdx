## Understanding Our Valuation Methodology: How It Compares to Other Mineral Rights Offers

> Comparing mineral rights offers requires a common framework. The [MRX published methodology](/methodology/) is one such framework; every other buyer's offer is a competing framework. The right move is to put every offer on the same basis, ask each buyer to disclose the inputs, and compare the offers against an independent valuation built on public production data.

### Key Takeaways

- **A methodology is a framework, not a number**: The [MRX published methodology](/methodology/) describes the inputs, the assumptions, and the cross-checks that produce a value range. Any buyer can be compared against the same framework.
- **Three methods, one conclusion**: Discounted cash flow, comparable transactions, and a royalty income multiple check. The methodology uses all three; most buyers use only one.
- **Public production data is the floor**: Well-by-well production from the [Texas Railroad Commission](https://www.rrc.texas.gov/) and the [EIA Texas state profile](https://www.eia.gov/state/?id=TX) is verifiable by any qualified buyer.
- **The comparison framework is the owner's defense**: Every offer is converted into the same three numbers and compared against the independent valuation. The owner sees the math, not just the answer.
- **No comparative superiority claim**: The methodology is described as a framework, not as a ranking. The owner chooses the offer that best fits the decision context.

## What a Mineral Rights Valuation Methodology Is

A mineral rights valuation methodology is a published set of inputs, assumptions, and cross-checks that produces a defensible value range for an interest. The methodology is the framework; the value range is the output.

The [MRX published methodology](/methodology/) describes three independent methods that converge on the same answer.

- **Discounted cash flow (DCF).** Forecasted production is multiplied by a price deck and discounted back to present value at a market-clearing discount rate.
- **Comparable transactions.** Recent sales of similar interests in the same county and formation provide a market-based price per net mineral acre.
- **Royalty income multiple.** The trailing monthly royalty income is divided into the value range to produce the implied multiple. The published methodology uses the multiple as one of three cross-checks to confirm the DCF and comparable-transactions methods are producing a market-consistent answer.

The convergence is the defensible value range. The divergence, when it occurs, is documented and explained.

## How Different Buyer Methodologies Compare

Not every buyer uses the same methodology. The differences are useful to understand because they explain why two buyers can look at the same interest and produce different numbers.

- **DCF-only buyers.** Some buyers use a DCF driven by their internal price deck and decline curve. The valuation is precise but depends on the assumptions. Ask for the price deck and the decline curve.
- **Multiple-only buyers.** Some buyers use a multiple of the trailing monthly royalty income. The valuation is fast but ignores the production decline. Ask for the multiple and the comparable transactions.
- **Comparable-only buyers.** Some buyers use a price per net mineral acre from recent comparable sales. The valuation is market-anchored but ignores the specific decline characteristics of the subject interest. Ask for the comparable transactions.
- **Three-method buyers.** Some buyers, including the [MRX methodology](/methodology/), use all three methods and report the convergence. The valuation is the most defensible because it cross-checks itself.

The owner does not need to choose one methodology; the owner needs to put every offer on the same basis.

## Putting Every Offer on the Same Basis

The comparison framework is the same one used in the [MRX offer review pillar](/offer-review/). Every offer is converted into three numbers.

| Number | How to calculate |
|---|---|
| Total net dollar amount | The offer after all deductions, fees, and post-closing adjustments |
| Price per net mineral acre | The total divided by your net mineral acres |
| Implied royalty income multiple | The total divided by your trailing monthly royalty income |

Once every offer is in the same three numbers, the comparison is mechanical.

- The offer with the highest price per net mineral acre is not necessarily the best offer; the clawback, the closing timeline, and the operator consent matter too.
- The offer with the highest implied multiple is not necessarily the best offer; an unusually high multiple can signal a buyer who is overpaying and recovering through a clawback.
- The offer that falls inside the independent valuation range is competitive on price; the offer that falls below is not.

The role of the methodology is to surface the comparison, not to make the decision.

## The Independent Valuation as the Anchor

Anchoring the comparison to an independent valuation is the most useful step. The valuation should be built on:

- Public production data from the [RRC](https://www.rrc.texas.gov/) and the [EIA Texas profile](https://www.eia.gov/state/?id=TX).
- The [MRX published methodology](/methodology/) for DCF construction, comparable-transactions selection, and royalty multiple review.
- A defensible value range, not a single point estimate.

Once the range is in hand, every offer either falls inside, above, or below it. The framework makes the comparison objective.

## What the Methodology Does Not Claim

The methodology is precise about what it does and does not promise.

- **It does not promise a specific sale price.** The valuation is a range; the actual transaction depends on counterparty negotiation, due diligence, and title.
- **It is not a comparative ranking.** The methodology is described as a framework, not as a ranking of one buyer over another. The owner chooses the offer that best fits the decision context.
- **It is not a third-party professional appraisal report prepared under applicable standards.** For court, tax, or estate contexts where such a report is required, the methodology is not a substitute. Engage a state-licensed appraiser.
- **It is not legal or tax guidance.** For title, probate, contract, or tax questions, the next step is a qualified attorney or CPA.

These boundaries are documented on the [MRX methodology page](/methodology/) and on the [MRX offer review pillar](/offer-review/).

## How to Ask Each Buyer to Disclose the Methodology

The comparison framework is useful only if every buyer discloses the inputs. Five questions surface the most information.

1. What royalty income multiple does your offer imply?
2. What long-term oil and gas price deck did you assume?
3. What decline curve and remaining reserve figure did you use?
4. What is your due diligence clawback, and what triggers it?
5. What is your funding source, and is it committed?

A buyer who answers all five with documented assumptions is a buyer who can be compared. A buyer who answers one or two and refuses the rest is not. ## Why the Comparison Framework Is Built on Public Data

Public data is verifiable. The [Texas Railroad Commission](https://www.rrc.texas.gov/) maintains well-by-well production records. The [EIA Texas state profile](https://www.eia.gov/state/?id=TX) provides the basin-level production context. Both are free to access and free to use.

A buyer who claims a different valuation for the same interest has to defend the claim against the same public production numbers. The owner can take the deliverable to any qualified buyer and ask the buyer to point to the specific input that produces a different number. This is the difference between an opinion and a market analysis.

## The Texas Basin Context

Texas basin productivity varies by formation and by operator. The [EIA Texas state profile](https://www.eia.gov/state/?id=TX) provides the basin-level production context for Permian, Eagle Ford, Haynesville, and Barnett acreage. The basin context is documented in the deliverable and is part of the comparable-transactions selection. An offer on Permian acreage is not directly comparable to an offer on Barnett acreage without a basin adjustment.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How do you compare mineral rights offers?

Convert every offer into the same three numbers: total net dollars to seller, price per net mineral acre, and implied royalty income multiple. Then compare all three against an independent valuation built on the [MRX methodology](/methodology/) and public production data. The offer that is closest to the middle of the valuation range, with the cleanest clawback terms and the shortest closing timeline, is the most competitive.

### What is the MRX valuation methodology?

The [MRX valuation methodology](/methodology/) is a published set of inputs, assumptions, and cross-checks that produces a defensible value range for a mineral rights interest. It uses three independent methods: discounted cash flow, comparable transactions, and a royalty income multiple check. The convergence is the defensible value range; the divergence is documented and explained.

### Why do different buyers give different valuation numbers?

Different buyers use different methodologies. Some use a DCF driven by internal assumptions; some use a multiple of the trailing monthly royalty income; some use a price per net mineral acre from recent comparable sales. The differences are useful to understand because they explain why two buyers can look at the same interest and produce different numbers. The owner does not need to choose one methodology; the owner needs to put every offer on the same basis.

### What is the most useful comparison number?

The implied royalty income multiple is a useful comparison number. The multiple is calculated by dividing the offer by the trailing monthly royalty income. The [MRX published methodology](/methodology/) uses the multiple as one of three cross-checks to confirm the DCF and comparable-transactions methods are producing a market-consistent answer. The published methodology does not state a fixed market range; the multiple is a comparison tool, not a threshold.

### Is the methodology a third-party professional appraisal report prepared under applicable standards?

No. The methodology is a market analysis built on public data and the published methodology. It is not a third-party professional appraisal report prepared under applicable standards, and it is not a substitute for one in any legal, tax, or estate context where such a report is required.

### Can I use the methodology to negotiate a better offer?

Yes. The most common use is comparing an existing offer to an independent value range. Sellers who enter negotiations with a documented range and a written comparison framework routinely achieve better outcomes than sellers who anchor to the buyer's opening number.

## The Bottom Line

A methodology is a framework, not a number. The right comparison framework is built on public production data, a published methodology, and a written deliverable that documents the inputs and the math. The same framework lets an owner put every offer on the same basis and choose the offer that best fits the decision context.

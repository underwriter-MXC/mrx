> **Public-record retrieval boundary.** This article records one Texas RRC Field Search attempt. It does not interpret a field rule, field number, field name, search result, identifier, well, operator, lease, county, map, filing, or record. It does not establish a field identity or boundary, acreage, well or property connection, title, ownership, lease effect, production, reserves, regulatory or compliance status, legal effect, value, offer quality, or a transaction result. It does not provide legal, title, land, tax, accounting, appraisal, engineering, geological, regulatory, or financial advice. MRX may have an economic interest in a later transaction; when that applies, the buyer relationship is disclosed in writing before an agreement is signed.

A **Texas RRC Field Search retrieval** worksheet answers one narrow question: exactly how was one official Field Search attempt performed? Record the route, exact source label, access timestamp and timezone, selected search mode, criterion actually entered, source-displayed minimum-character rule, displayed result/navigation or error state, a controlled retained reference, and one bounded retrieval status.

Use only **located**, **not located**, or **unverified**. Those terms describe the retrieval attempt. They do not decide what a field label or rule means, whether a displayed reference applies to a private property interest, or whether any field, well, lease, owner, regulator, or transaction has a particular condition.

## Preserve the two official labels without merging them

The Railroad Commission of Texas [Research Queries](https://www.rrc.texas.gov/resource-center/research/research-queries/) directory lists a link labeled **Field Rules Query** in its Oil & Gas table. The official page reached from that link is visibly labeled [Field Search](https://webapps.rrc.texas.gov/DP/initializeFieldSearchAction.do). Record both labels with their respective URLs and access times.

Do not rewrite the two labels as if the sources used one name. Do not infer that the index label and application label establish an identical record class, and do not use the route relationship to interpret field rules. The provenance record may say only that the official index displayed one label and launched an official application displaying the other.

This worksheet owns the launched Field Search attempt. It does not own field-rule interpretation, field-data downloads, well-record retrieval, production analysis, GIS mapping, property matching, title review, or valuation. For broader source orientation, use [Texas Railroad Commission: How to Use Public Records to Understand Your Mineral Rights](/blog/texas-railroad-commission-how-to-use-public-records-to-understand-your-mineral-rights/).

## Create an attempt header before searching

Give the attempt a neutral ID such as `TX-FIELD-RET-01`. The ID labels a retrieval session, not a field, rule, well, property, mineral interest, finding, or transaction conclusion. Record the purpose, official index route, launched application route, both displayed labels, access date and time, timezone, preparer role, worksheet version, and every source-stated access, display, search, or result limitation observed.

Create a new attempt record for a rerun. Do not overwrite an earlier criterion or displayed state because the interface, entry, or result changed. Separate attempt records allow an authorized reviewer to compare exactly what was queried without treating either attempt as a substantive field or property conclusion.

## Capture one search mode and criterion exactly

The current [Field Search](https://webapps.rrc.texas.gov/DP/initializeFieldSearchAction.do) page displays three ways to search:

- **Beginning with these characters**
- **Containing these characters**
- **Matching this Number Exactly**

The page also instructs the user to enter at least three characters. Record the exact mode selected, the criterion entered, the source-shown minimum, whether the attempt was submitted, and any displayed validation, error, loading, or result state. Do not publish a real field name, field number, result, or property-specific criterion in a reusable example.

Each entry is a locator criterion, not a conclusion. A displayed field reference remains source-displayed retrieval evidence. It does not establish the field's boundary, governing rules, connection to a well or property, current regulatory treatment, production, reserves, value, or legal effect.

## Record only the navigation that occurred

Preserve the path as a sequence:

**official Research Queries index → Field Rules Query link → launched Field Search page → selected mode → criterion entered → displayed result, validation, error, or navigation state**

For each step, record the displayed label, controlled route reference, action taken, access time and timezone, whether the page loaded or changed, and the next step selected. If the session ended, exposed no requested reference, reached a visible constraint, or did not establish the narrow retrieval fact, use **not located** or **unverified**. Do not extend that status to a field, rule, record, well, operator, property, or mineral interest.

If a result offers another official route, preserve that destination as a separate retrieval step. Record its source label, destination route, access time, retained reference, and scope before opening it. A shared field label or number does not by itself establish that separate sources have the same coverage, meaning, currency, or private-property relationship.

## Keep neighboring record channels separate

The RRC [Data Sets Available for Download](https://www.rrc.texas.gov/resource-center/research/data-sets-available-for-download/) page separately lists **Oil & Gas Field Name & Numbers**, **Oil & Gas Field Rules**, annual report field tables, and statewide field data. Those downloadable data sets are not the same retrieval as one Field Search attempt. If an authorized workflow uses one, assign a separate attempt ID and preserve its exact data-set label, source route, format, update label, access time, file reference, and limitations.

The RRC [Well Records FAQs](https://www.rrc.texas.gov/about-us/faqs/oil-gas-faq/well-records-faqs/) describes record types that may appear in Oil and Gas Well Records and offers separate search troubleshooting. A well-record search is also a separate route. Do not treat a matching word, field label, lease label, county, API number, or other source-displayed value as proof that a Field Search reference and a well record describe the same private interest.

The official [Website Queries training slides](https://www.rrc.texas.gov/media/ilapc3ix/website-queries-how-to-stay-in-compliance-powerpoint-slides.pdf) are retained here only as agency-authored query-navigation context. This worksheet does not use the slides to establish a current requirement, interpret a field rule, decide compliance, or replace the live application's displayed labels and limits.

## Preserve the source limitation

The Research Queries page states that its online data sets are continually updated, provided for informational purposes, not intended as authoritative public records, and have no legal force or effect. It places responsibility on users to check accuracy, completeness, currency, and suitability. Preserve that limitation with the attempt.

A **not located** result in one Field Search cannot prove that no field, rule, record, well, filing, map feature, production record, or related source exists. A **located** reference cannot prove that a label is complete, current, accurate, applicable to a private interest, or sufficient for a downstream decision.

## Use only bounded retrieval statuses

- **Located:** the recorded official Field Search attempt exposed the identified field reference.
- **Not located:** the recorded attempt did not expose the sought field reference.
- **Unverified:** a route, label, number, result, or next step appeared, but the narrow retrieval fact or relationship was not established.

Located does not mean identified as a legal boundary, governed by a particular rule, connected to a well or property, owned, leased, producing, compliant, valuable, or legally effective. Not located does not mean nonexistent. Unverified preserves uncertainty without alleging an error, omission, defect, violation, or false record.

## Privacy, disclosure, and handoff controls

The reusable worksheet should remain blank. Keep real field names, field numbers, search criteria, result exports, screenshots, maps, well identifiers, property details, title documents, payment details, and private correspondence in an access-controlled location. Share only records you are authorized to provide, and disclose why each record is being requested.

MRX may act as an educator, evaluator, marketplace operator, or possible buyer. If MRX or an affiliate could acquire an interest, that economic relationship should be stated clearly before a transaction agreement is signed. A provenance worksheet improves reproducibility; it does not make MRX a neutral public agency, title examiner, attorney, engineer, geologist, accountant, appraiser, or regulator.

## Final retrieval check

Before closing the attempt, confirm that the worksheet preserves the complete official route, both exact source labels, access time and timezone, selected mode, controlled criterion, source-displayed minimum, displayed state, navigation that actually occurred, retained reference, source limitation, bounded status, and a neutral next question. If one of those items is missing, use **unverified** until the retrieval record is complete.

Stop there. The next step may be another official-source retrieval or an appropriately scoped professional review. It is not a conclusion about field identity or boundary, field-rule meaning, acreage, a well or property connection, title, ownership, lease effect, production, reserves, compliance, legal effect, value, offer quality, or a transaction.

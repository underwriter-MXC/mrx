> This article describes the current MRX first-party booking and review process as of August 6, 2026. The review is free, educational, directional, and no-obligation. It does not provide owner-specific legal or tax guidance, a title opinion, an audit, a formal credentialed valuation, or a guarantee of eligibility, value, an offer, payment, or closing.

## Answer first

**Start at the [MRX booking page](/book/), give the scheduling assistant your timezone and general timing preference, select one of the available phone times, and confirm the contact information and communication choices for the appointment.** Before the call, identify the property and the question you want to discuss, then organize the relevant records you already have.

You do not need to solve the ownership or valuation question before asking for the review. State what you know, preserve what you do not know, and avoid guessing at acreage, title, production, or tax facts.

## Step 1: State the decision question

A focused question gives the review a useful starting point. Examples include:

- I inherited an interest and do not know which records connect the estate to the payor.
- I received a written offer and want its property description and adjustments organized.
- My royalty payments changed and I want to compare statements with public production context.
- I own a nonproducing interest and want to identify which facts are verified and which are only scenarios.
- I am considering a partial or full sale and want the alternatives framed without a required decision.

Avoid beginning with a demanded outcome, such as a guaranteed price or closing date. The review depends on the available evidence and stated assumptions.

## Step 2: Identify the property

Write down as much of the following as is known:

- state and county;
- legal description, survey, abstract, block, section, tract, or township and range;
- operator, lease, unit, field, and well names or identifiers;
- producing, nonproducing, leased, unleased, inherited, or in-suspense status;
- the owner’s understanding of the interest type and fraction; and
- whether an offer, royalty statement, division order, or lease is available.

An unknown is an acceptable answer. A royalty check can help locate an owner or property number, but it does not prove the complete title record.

## Step 3: Use the current booking flow

The [MRX booking page](https://mineralrightsxchange.com/book/) currently opens a private scheduling conversation with Angela, MRX’s AI scheduling guide. The flow asks for:

1. timezone and general timing preference;
2. selection from available phone times;
3. name, email, and phone number for the appointment; and
4. separate choices for email, SMS, marketing texts, and calls.

Declining optional text-message permission does not prevent booking. Review each communication choice rather than treating consent as one combined checkbox.

The booking page says phone appointments are free and there is no obligation to sell. Availability can change, so rely on the choices presented in the live flow rather than an old screenshot or email.

## Step 4: Organize documents by purpose

Use four folders:

| Folder | Examples | What it may help establish |
| -- | -- | -- |
| Ownership | Deeds, reservations, assignments, probate, trust funding records | Claimed interest, tract, transfer chain, unresolved gaps |
| Lease and payment | Lease, amendments, pooling records, division orders, statements | Property scope, payor inputs, payment history |
| Operations | Well, unit, operator, and public production records | Operator-reported context and property matching |
| Transaction | Offer letter, purchase agreement, proposed deed, correspondence | Rights covered, price terms, adjustments, timing, open questions |

The [MRX FAQ](https://mineralrightsxchange.com/faq/) says owners can begin with a recent royalty statement, offer, lease, or division order if available. No single document proves every issue.

## Step 5: Share only what is relevant through the intended channel

Use the first-party account or secure upload flow when document sharing is requested and available. Confirm the domain before signing in or uploading.

Before sharing, remove or redact unrelated:

- bank account and routing numbers;
- full taxpayer identification numbers;
- passwords, one-time codes, and security answers;
- unrelated medical, family, or financial records;
- signatures when an image is not needed for the review question; and
- pages that do not concern the property or transaction.

Keep an unmodified original in your own records. If secure document processing is unavailable, do not improvise by sending files to an unknown address or third-party link.

## Step 6: Prepare an evidence and unknowns list

The [MRX methodology](https://mineralrightsxchange.com/methodology/) describes a directional analysis that states its assumptions. Help preserve that boundary by marking each item:

- **Known and sourced:** supported by an identified record.
- **Owner-reported:** remembered or stated but not yet verified.
- **Calculated:** derived from disclosed inputs.
- **Scenario:** a possible future price, decline, timing, or development case.
- **Unknown:** missing, conflicting, or unresolved.

For example, a statement can show what the payor reported for a month. It does not establish that the decimal is legally correct. Public production data can show what an operator reported. It does not establish private title or future production.

## Step 7: Use the call to map the review

At the start of the phone conversation, confirm:

1. the property and interest being discussed;
2. the owner’s decision question;
3. the records used and their dates;
4. the important assumptions and unknowns;
5. what the directional review can address; and
6. which questions require another professional.

The current [MRX How It Works page](https://mineralrightsxchange.com/how-it-works/) describes review areas that may include production history, royalty income, offer terms, operator and basin context, and title or acreage confidence. Actual scope depends on the records and situation.

## Step 8: Confirm the boundaries before relying on the result

The review does not provide:

- a legal or title opinion;
- probate, trust, or contract advice;
- tax-return, basis, or Section 1031 advice;
- a royalty audit or payor accounting certification;
- a formal credentialed valuation;
- a guaranteed price, offer, payment, production result, or closing; or
- an obligation to sell or proceed.

The [MRX FAQ](https://mineralrightsxchange.com/faq/) states that MRX may become a buyer in some transactions. If that commercial role applies, it is disclosed before an agreement is signed. The owner can independently review any later offer and contract.

## Step 9: Leave with a written next-step list

A useful review should identify:

- the property and records reviewed;
- facts, calculations, scenarios, and unknowns;
- the assumptions behind any directional range;
- missing documents and who may hold them;
- focused questions for the operator, payor, buyer, county office, or professional; and
- owner-controlled choices to hold, investigate, compare, or consider a transaction.

No single next step fits every mineral owner.

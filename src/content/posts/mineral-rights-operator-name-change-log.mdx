> **Displayed-name boundary.** This article explains how to record the operator or payor name displayed in identified public and owner records. It does not determine whether two names identify the same organization, a successor, affiliate, agent, responsible operator, responsible payor, or party connected to a property. It is not a title opinion, legal opinion, regulatory determination, accounting conclusion, appraisal, development forecast, or transaction recommendation. MRX may have an economic interest in a later transaction; when that applies, the buyer relationship is disclosed in writing before an agreement is signed.

A **mineral rights operator name change log** is a source-preserving comparison, not a corporate family tree. Create one row for each identified record. Copy the operator or payor name exactly as displayed, retain the source, record identifier, retrieval date, and displayed date or status, and label the comparison only `same displayed name`, `different displayed name`, or `unavailable`.

Every different or unavailable result should produce a neutral clarification question. The log stops there. It does not decide that a name variation reflects a merger, assignment, acquisition, affiliate, clerical change, operator transfer, payment obligation, or property connection.

## Start with one identified record per row

Do not begin with a conclusion such as “the operator changed.” That statement may be true, but a name difference alone does not establish why it changed or whether the records describe the same role, asset, or organization.

Build the row from the source outward:

| Log field                    | What to preserve                                                                                   |
| ---------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Source system or record type | Exact public query, statement, division order, correspondence, or other owner record               |
| Record identifier            | Displayed API, permit, operator number, lease or account reference when shown                      |
| Retrieval date               | Date the record was opened, downloaded, or received                                                |
| Record date or status        | The date, period, current/historical label, or status displayed by that source                     |
| Role label                   | Operator, permitted operator, payor, purchaser, or another role exactly as the record describes it |
| Displayed name               | Exact spelling, punctuation, suffix, spacing, and capitalization shown                             |
| Comparison result            | Same displayed name, different displayed name, or unavailable                                      |
| Neutral question             | Required when the result is different or unavailable                                               |

Keep a permitted URL, export, PDF, screenshot, or copy of the underlying record when practical. A reviewer should be able to retrieve the same source and understand which field you observed.

## Keep each source system in its own lane

The Railroad Commission of Texas maintains separate [Online Research Queries](https://www.rrc.texas.gov/resource-center/research/research-queries/) for drilling permits, well records, production, organizations, and other subjects. The agency also states that these continually updated data sets are informational, are not authoritative public records, and carry no legal force or effect. Preserve the query system and retrieval date instead of treating every RRC screen as one interchangeable record.

The Commission's [Wellbore Query Help](https://webapps2.rrc.texas.gov/EWA/help/WellboreGeneralHowTo.html) lists operator as a search field and describes result fields that include API number, operator name, lease name and number, well number, county, field, and current or historical status. Those identifiers help preserve which wellbore result displayed the name. They do not establish a private-property or payment connection.

The [P-5 Organization Query](https://webapps2.rrc.texas.gov/EWA/help/P5Organization_About.html) answers a different record question. Its results include an operator number, operator name, organization status, and organization type; detail pages can display filing and expiration dates and other organization information. Copy the operator number and displayed name into the same row. Do not use a similar address, officer, name fragment, or organization type to decide that two entities are successors or affiliates.

The Commission's [Drilling Permit Query Results Help](https://webapps2.rrc.texas.gov/EWA/help/DPQuery_results_howto.htm) describes a permitted-operator field and the operator number assigned by the RRC. It also notes that an operator name can change at completion. A permit result and a later wellbore or completion result can therefore display different names. Record both observations with their identifiers and dates; do not silently replace one with the other.

## Preserve exact text before comparing it

Create an `exact displayed name` column that is never normalized. If one source shows punctuation, spacing, an abbreviation, or an entity suffix differently, retain the difference.

You may add a separate `comparison note` for ordinary formatting observations, such as “one record includes a period after an abbreviation.” That note must not become a corporate-identity conclusion. Similar words do not prove the same entity, and different words do not prove a transfer, merger, or error.

For example, a private statement may show a payor while a public wellbore result shows an operator. Even if the names match, the role labels differ. Preserve both roles. Do not convert “same displayed name” into “same responsibility.”

## Use only three comparison results

### Same displayed name

Use `same displayed name` only when the selected name fields agree as displayed, apart from a separately noted ordinary formatting difference. The result says nothing about whether the records refer to the same property, time period, role, company relationship, payment account, or obligation.

### Different displayed name

Use `different displayed name` when both identified records display a name and the names differ. Preserve both strings and ask a neutral question such as:

- “Which dated filing or owner record explains the displayed-name difference?”
- “Do these records describe the same role and time period?”
- “Which identifier should be used to retrieve the controlling record?”
- “What record supports any claimed relationship between these organizations?”

Avoid questions that assume an answer, such as “Why did the company sell this well?” or “Why is the payor using the wrong name?”

### Unavailable

Use `unavailable` when a source cannot be opened, the selected field is blank, the record identifier is missing, or the record does not display the role being compared. Unavailable is not a weak match and not evidence of a transfer. Record what is missing and ask what source or identifier would resolve the gap.

## Keep public records and owner records separate

An owner record may include a royalty statement, division order, check detail, tax document, lease correspondence, or another document the owner is authorized to review. Name the document type and preserve its date or period. Redact unrelated personal information before sharing it when appropriate.

Do not treat an owner-record payor field as though it were an RRC operator field. Do not assume that the company sending correspondence operates a well, owns a leasehold interest, acquired another organization, or owes a specific payment. The log records only what the identified document displays.

The live [offset-activity property-connection guide](/blog/offset-activity-property-connection-cross-check/) owns the separate task of comparing one identified offset record with owner property records field by field. This article does not repeat that matrix. A name-log row never establishes that a well, permit, lease, unit, tract, depth, production stream, or payment account belongs to the owner's interest.

## Never erase an earlier observation

If a current query later displays a different name, add a new dated row. Do not overwrite the older row. Preserve the earlier source copy, retrieval date, identifier, role label, and displayed status. The sequence should show what each record displayed when observed without claiming why it changed.

If a source is corrected or becomes unavailable, add that event as another row or note. A transparent log distinguishes the underlying record date from the date you retrieved it and from the date you noticed a difference.

## Stop before interpretation

The displayed-name log is complete when every selected record has a source, identifier, retrieval date, displayed name or unavailable result, comparison label, and neutral question where needed. Stop if the next step would require you to:

- decide whether organizations are the same, related, merged, affiliated, or successors;
- identify the responsible operator, payor, purchaser, agent, or contracting party;
- interpret a deed, lease, assignment, pooling document, division order, or title chain;
- connect a public record to a specific tract, depth, owner, or payment account;
- grade operator quality, compliance, finances, strategy, or development risk;
- predict drilling, completions, production, timing, or payment;
- calculate reserves, cash flow, DCF, market demand, an offer, or value; or
- recommend whether to sell, hold, sign, negotiate, or transact.

Those questions require different evidence and may require qualified land, legal, accounting, engineering, regulatory, appraisal, or tax review.

## Review the completed log

Before sharing the log, confirm that it:

- uses one identified record per row;
- preserves the source system or owner-record type;
- includes the exact displayed identifier and role label when available;
- separates record date, retrieval date, and displayed status;
- keeps the exact displayed name unchanged;
- uses only same displayed name, different displayed name, or unavailable;
- includes a neutral question for every different or unavailable result;
- retains earlier observations rather than overwriting them;
- excludes unnecessary personal information; and
- contains no corporate-identity, responsibility, property, title, payment, development, value, offer, or transaction conclusion.

A careful log can make a displayed-name question reproducible. It cannot answer the legal, regulatory, contractual, title, accounting, or economic question behind the difference.

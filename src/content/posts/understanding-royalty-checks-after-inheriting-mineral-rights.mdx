## Answer first

After inheriting mineral rights, an heir usually needs to connect two separate records: **the legal or estate path supporting the inherited interest and each operator or payor's owner account.** Royalty checks should not be expected to transfer automatically.

Identify the property and interest, complete the applicable estate or conveyance steps with qualified help, contact every payor through a verified owner-relations channel, submit the requested documents, review the resulting division order, and reconcile any suspense balance and new statements.

## Build the inherited-interest file

Start with the documents that explain how the interest moved from the prior owner. Depending on the facts, the file may include:

- the prior owner's deed or acquisition instrument;
- a will, probate order, inventory, distribution, or personal-representative deed;
- an affidavit or other instrument used in the title record;
- trust documents or trustee certification;
- transfer-on-death deed records;
- death certificate or identity records requested through an authorized process;
- leases, amendments, unit records, and division orders; and
- the prior owner's royalty statements and tax forms.

Texas Estates Code Chapter 358 expressly addresses mineral and royalty interests in estate property and administration. Which estate procedure applies is a legal question; this checklist is not a substitute for probate or title advice.

## Inventory every payor and property

One inherited portfolio may involve several operators, purchasers, or payors. Build a table with the prior owner number, payor, property name and number, operator, county, last statement date, current status, and verified owner-relations contact.

Do not send Social Security numbers, banking details, or sensitive estate documents to an unverified email address. Navigate from the operator's official website or use contact information independently confirmed from prior records.

## Ask for the ownership-transfer requirements

Request the payor's written transfer checklist and the reason for each property hold or suspense code. Submit a complete package and preserve proof of delivery.

Clarify:

1. which property or owner accounts are covered;
2. which document establishes the transfer in the payor's records;
3. whether certified or recorded copies are required;
4. whether separate tax and payment forms are needed;
5. the effective date the payor will use;
6. whether prior proceeds are in suspense; and
7. how the payor will report adjustments or prior-period payments.

A payor's acceptance for payment setup does not itself establish complete marketable title for a later sale.

## Review the new division order

Compare the new payee name, property, decimal, effective date, and terms with the prior owner's records and the inherited-interest documents. A changed decimal can reflect a partial inheritance, co-heirs, a different property, a unit change, or an error; it should be explained rather than guessed.

The guide to [division orders](/blog/what-is-a-division-order-and-why-does-it-matter-for-mineral-rights-owners/) provides a field-by-field checklist.

## Reconcile suspense and first payments

For every suspense or adjustment period, ask the payor for:

- the property and product;
- the production or sales months;
- the amount held;
- the reason for the hold;
- the documents required for release;
- the effective ownership date used; and
- the statement detail accompanying payment.

Texas Natural Resources Code Chapter 91 supplies general payment and division-order context. It should not be used here to promise a particular payment date or outcome without owner-specific review.

Use [the royalty-statement guide](/blog/how-to-decode-your-royalty-check-statement/) to trace volume, price, decimal, deductions, taxes, adjustments, and net payment. Compare the statement with prior records and the [Railroad Commission's operator-reported production data](https://www.rrc.texas.gov/oil-and-gas/research-and-statistics/production-data/), while preserving the limits of that public data.

## Keep sale readiness separate

Payor setup answers who the payor currently recognizes for distribution. A mineral-rights sale raises broader questions about authority, record title, the exact interest, co-owners, liens, basis records, and the proposed conveyance.

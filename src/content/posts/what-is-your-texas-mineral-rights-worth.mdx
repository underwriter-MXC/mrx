## What Is Your Texas Mineral Rights Worth? Comprehensive Valuation and Selling Insights

Understanding the value of your Texas mineral rights is crucial for landowners looking to maximize their assets. Mineral rights represent the ownership of the minerals beneath the land, which can include oil, gas, and other valuable resources. This article will guide you through the valuation process, the steps to sell your mineral rights, and how to effectively use mineral rights value calculators. Many landowners are unaware of the potential worth of their mineral rights, which can lead to missed opportunities for profit. ### Key Takeaways

- **Mineral rights and surface rights are legally separate in Texas**: Texas law allows a landowner to sell the surface while retaining the minerals, or vice versa. Once severed, the mineral estate is dominant, giving the mineral owner the right to use the surface for production activities.
- **Royalty interest is the most common mineral right ownership type**: A royalty interest entitles the owner to a percentage of gross production revenue without bearing drilling or operating costs. Working interests share in both revenue and expenses.
- **You can own mineral rights without owning the surface**: Millions of Texans inherited or purchased mineral rights that are completely separate from any surface deed. Ownership is proven through recorded instruments in the county deed records where the land lies.
- **Producing vs. non-producing status dramatically affects value**: Minerals with active royalty production are worth 3-10x more than equivalent acreage with no current production. An undeveloped position near active drilling still commands premium pricing over truly dormant acreage.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What are mineral rights and do I own them?

Mineral rights are the legal right to extract and profit from oil, gas, coal, and other subsurface resources beneath a parcel of land. In Texas, mineral rights can be separated from surface ownership, meaning you might own the minerals under land you don't own on the surface, or vice versa. To find out if you own mineral rights, search the deed records in the county where the land sits, starting with the instrument by which you or your ancestor acquired the property.

### What is the difference between surface rights and mineral rights?

Surface rights govern what happens on top of the land, farming, building, grazing. Mineral rights govern what's below, oil, gas, coal, and other subsurface resources. In Texas, these can be severed, meaning different people can own the surface and the minerals simultaneously. The mineral estate is legally 'dominant,' meaning mineral owners have the right to use the surface as reasonably necessary for production, even without the surface owner's permission.

### What is a royalty interest in mineral rights?

A royalty interest entitles the owner to receive a percentage of gross production revenue from oil and gas wells, without bearing any drilling, completion, or operating costs. Royalty rates are set in the oil and gas lease and typically range from 1/8 (12.5%) to 1/4 (25%) of gross production. A working interest, by contrast, shares in production revenue but also bears proportionate drilling and operating costs.

### How do I find out if my land has oil or gas production?

Search the Texas Railroad Commission's online records at rrc.texas.gov using the county and operator name. You can find well permits, production reports, and lease information. Also check your county's deed records for any oil and gas leases recorded against your property. If you're receiving royalty checks, the operator is required to send a check stub detailing production volumes and prices.

### What is a division order and why does it matter?

A division order is a document prepared by the oil company that specifies each owner's fractional interest in production from a well. You must sign and return the division order before the operator will pay you royalties. Review it carefully, errors in the decimal interest listed can underpay you for years. If the division order contradicts your deed or lease, contact an oil and gas attorney immediately before signing.

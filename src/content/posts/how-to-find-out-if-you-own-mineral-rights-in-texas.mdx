## Answer first

To find out whether you own mineral rights in Texas, start with the county deed and probate records for the tract, then confirm whether later conveyances, reservations, or affidavits changed the ownership chain. Texas agency production tools can add lease and well context, but they do not replace the county source of record for ownership proof.

## Start with the county source of record

Mineral-rights ownership is not usually verified by a single online search result. It is built from the recorded chain: deeds, reservations, probate filings, affidavits, and any later transfers touching the tract.
Texas recording rules matter because they explain why filed instruments carry so much weight when owners or later purchasers are trying to understand what was actually conveyed.

## Use public agency research as a second layer, not as proof of title

Once the owner has a tract, county, and legal description, Texas Railroad Commission resources can help connect the property to wells, lease names, operators, and production history. That can be useful when the owner is trying to understand whether a producing interest may exist.
But an agency production page is not the same thing as an ownership determination. The production file adds context after the title file is started.

## What documents usually show up in a real ownership file

Owners often need more than one document: an acquisition deed, probate documents if the property passed through an estate, later mineral deeds or reservations, and any current lease or division-order materials that explain how a producing interest has been handled.
For next-step reading, owners can compare this article with [Net Mineral Acres vs. Royalty Acres: What Texas Mineral Rights Owners Need to Know](/blog/net-mineral-acres-vs-royalty-acres-what-texas-mineral-rights-owners-need-to-know/) and the ownership hub at [Title, lease, and ownership resources](/learning-center/title-lease-ownership/).

## When to slow down and get help

If the tract appears to have severed minerals, multiple heirs, missing probate, conflicting deeds, or a gap in the chain, that is usually the moment to stop treating the problem as a simple self-serve search. Educational organization helps, but tract-specific title conclusions still belong to qualified professionals.

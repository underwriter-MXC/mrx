> **Document-extraction boundary.** This article organizes private lease-clause evidence. It does not find that a well is shut in; decide whether a clause was triggered, satisfied, breached, waived, cured, or extended; determine lease validity, HBP status, a savings-clause effect, payment entitlement, title, ownership, acreage, depth, unit coverage, production, cash flow, value, or a transaction result; or provide legal, title, land, tax, accounting, appraisal, engineering, investment, or financial advice. Regulatory data and public state-lease examples do not supply the terms or legal effect of a private lease. MRX may have an economic interest in a later transaction; when that applies, the buyer relationship is disclosed in writing before an agreement is signed.

A **shut-in clause conditions valuation review** worksheet should preserve contract text before anyone debates what it means. Start with the controlling signed lease stack. Copy the shut-in provision and every linked section exactly, preserve the version and source locator, and separate each stated condition into its own field. Turn uncertainty into a neutral question.

That output is a document map, not a lease opinion or valuation adjustment. It helps another reviewer find the exact words and supporting records without treating a clause label, an operator notice, a payment record, or a regulatory result as proof.

## Start with the controlling document stack

Do not begin with a web article, a neighbor's lease, or a generic form. Begin with the documents that may control the specific interest:

- the fully executed lease and all signature pages;
- addenda, riders, exhibits, amendments, extensions, and ratifications;
- recorded memoranda, pooled-unit documents, releases, and partial releases;
- assignments or ownership records needed only to identify the relevant document version;
- authorized operator or payor notices that expressly reference the clause; and
- payment correspondence, receipts, or owner-relations responses that may belong in a separate evidence row.

Give each document a neutral ID such as `LEASE-01` or `AMENDMENT-02`. Record the full document name, execution date, recording reference if available, page count, version relationship, and where the retained copy came from. Do not publish owner names, signatures, addresses, payment instructions, bank information, private figures, or complete private instrument images in a shared worksheet.

If two copies differ, preserve both and mark `version conflict`. Do not silently select the cleaner copy. The conflict itself is a fact that the next qualified reviewer needs to resolve.

## Preserve the clause before extracting fields

Capture the entire provision, not one sentence containing the words `shut in`. Include the section heading, paragraph boundaries, defined terms, internal references, and the first and last words of the retained excerpt. Record:

- **Document ID and version:** the exact lease, addendum, or amendment.
- **Section and page locator:** printed page, PDF page, paragraph, exhibit, and recording reference when available.
- **Exact excerpt:** the full retained language in a private file.
- **Beginning and ending anchors:** short text anchors that help a reviewer find the same passage.
- **Linked sections:** every definition, payment, notice, operations, force-majeure, cessation, habendum, pooling, or other provision expressly referenced.
- **Extraction date and extractor:** when the text was copied and by whom.
- **Image or OCR check:** whether the text came from a legible original, a scan, or OCR that was compared back to the image.

Never let OCR become the source of record. Hyphens, decimal points, dates, section numbers, and words such as `and`, `or`, `before`, or `after` can change the apparent structure of a clause. Compare the extracted text against the retained image and mark unreadable language as `illegible` instead of guessing.

## Split the provision into condition fields

After the source text is preserved, create one row per condition. Keep the original words beside any plain-language label.

- **Defined trigger wording:** copy what the provision says must exist or occur. Do not write that it actually occurred.
- **Covered subject:** record whether the text refers to a well, lease, premises, land, pooled unit, particular product, or another defined subject.
- **Capability or status wording:** preserve the clause's exact wording without classifying the actual well.
- **Stated reason or circumstance:** copy any condition stated in the provision; do not decide whether the facts meet it.
- **Payment amount or formula:** preserve the private text and locator without calculating, normalizing, or publishing a real owner's figure.
- **Due timing:** copy the stated deadline, recurrence, anniversary, or measurement rule without deciding timeliness.
- **Recipient or tender route:** record the named recipient, depository, address reference, or other route as written, redacted in shared examples.
- **Notice, affidavit, or certification:** identify each stated accompanying record and its source locator.
- **Duration or renewal wording:** preserve the period and any repeat-payment language without deciding the resulting term.
- **Cross-reference:** create a separate row for every linked section or document.
- **Unresolved term:** record an exact neutral question when a definition, date, version, fact, or connection is missing.

Use `not stated in extracted provision`, `not located`, `illegible`, `version conflict`, or `needs qualified review` instead of leaving a blank. Those labels distinguish an incomplete record from a conclusion.

## Follow every cross-reference as a separate evidence object

A shut-in provision may use defined terms or point to other clauses. A later amendment may replace only part of the original text. A memorandum may identify a lease but omit the operative language. Build a cross-reference table rather than merging everything into a paraphrase.

- **Definition:** preserve the exact term, section, version, and text anchor; do not decide whether facts satisfy the definition.
- **Payment provision:** preserve the exact formula or wording, timing, and recipient; do not decide whether payment was due or effective.
- **Notice or cure:** preserve the required text, method, and stated period; do not decide whether notice or cure occurred legally.
- **Amendment:** preserve replacement language plus execution and recording locators; do not decide whether it controls without full document review.
- **Savings clause:** preserve the exact link and source text; do not decide lease continuation or legal effect.
- **Unit or exhibit:** preserve the referenced unit, land, depth, or exhibit locator; do not decide property coverage, title, or acreage.

If a referenced document is missing, write the request narrowly: `Obtain the executed amendment identified in section 12` is better than `confirm the lease is valid`. The first request seeks evidence. The second assumes a conclusion.

## Keep public examples in their own lane

The Texas General Land Office [Lease Maintenance](https://www.glo.texas.gov/energy/mineral-leasing/lease-maintenance) page identifies state-lease administration categories such as shut-in payments and affidavits. The GLO [Leasing](https://www.glo.texas.gov/energy/mineral-leasing/leasing) page supplies the official route to state-leasing guidance and forms.

The public [GLO Relinquishment Act Lease Form](https://www.glo.texas.gov/sites/default/files/2025-02/Form_Relinquishment_Act_Lease.pdf) is useful only as a field-design example. It shows why an extraction worksheet may need separate rows for stated trigger language, covered subject, payment wording, timing, accompanying records, duration, and cross-references. It does not provide the terms of a private lease, and its administration rules cannot be imported into an owner's document.

Label every public example `state-lease example only; not controlling private text`. Do not copy its deadlines, formulas, filing requirements, or legal consequences into a private working row unless the owner's controlling document independently contains that language.

## Keep RRC context separate from contract text

The Railroad Commission [Research Queries](https://www.rrc.texas.gov/resource-center/research/research-queries/) page routes users to production, permit, well-record, and other regulatory systems. The Commission also cautions that online datasets are informational and do not have legal force. Create a separate row for the exact query used, field entered, result identifier, access date, and source limitation.

The RRC [Production Data](https://www.rrc.texas.gov/oil-and-gas/research-and-statistics/production-data/) page leads to operator-reported production compilations. The [Production Data Query FAQs](https://www.rrc.texas.gov/about-us/faqs/oil-gas-faq/production-data-query-system-faqs/) explain important scope limits: Texas oil production can be reported at the lease level, reports arrive after the production period, results can change with revised or delinquent reports, and the online display is a snapshot.

Those limits matter. A blank month, a zero, a late report, or a changed row does not by itself prove why production did or did not occur. It does not prove that a private clause trigger existed or that a payment, notice, or filing condition was met.

The RRC [Oil and Gas Well Records](https://www.rrc.texas.gov/oil-and-gas/research-and-statistics/obtaining-commission-records/oil-and-gas-well-records/) page identifies useful file locators such as county, operator, field, lease or well name and number, and Commission identifiers. It also explains that the Commission does not decide private lease, royalty, or property-right matters. Preserve a well-file locator as regulatory context only. Do not bridge it to the private lease without separate property-connection evidence and qualified review.

## Keep four evidence lanes separate

1. **Controlling contract lane:** signed lease, addenda, amendments, ratifications, and exhibits with exact source locators.
2. **Performance-record lane:** authorized notices, correspondence, tenders, receipts, or responses, each preserved without deciding sufficiency or effect.
3. **Regulatory-context lane:** exact RRC query, date, identifier, and source limitations without private-contract inference.
4. **Professional-conclusion lane:** any later legal, land, title, engineering, accounting, appraisal, or valuation conclusion with its own author, date, scope, and supporting record list.

Do not collapse these lanes into one timeline that implies causation. A clause may exist without a supported trigger. A payment record may exist without proving legal sufficiency. An RRC record may display a status or volume without establishing private lease effect. A later valuation analysis may use qualified conclusions, but this extraction worksheet does not create them.

## Turn unknowns into neutral questions

Useful questions identify the missing evidence and the person qualified to address it:

- Which executed amendment supplies the current wording for this section?
- Which definition controls the terms used in the extracted paragraph?
- Which complete document contains the referenced notice or payment provision?
- Which source identifies the covered well, lease, premises, or unit?
- Which records show the date and content of a notice or tender without assuming legal effect?
- Which qualified reviewer should evaluate clause meaning, factual satisfaction, and legal consequence?
- If a valuation professional later uses a conclusion, what evidence, effective date, and uncertainty should accompany it?

Avoid questions such as `Is my lease still valid?` or `How much did the shut-in reduce my minerals?` in the extraction sheet. Those are downstream professional questions, not missing-field labels.

## Final quality check

The worksheet is ready for the next reviewer when:

- the controlling signed document stack and every version are inventoried;
- the full provision is retained before fields are summarized;
- each row has a document, page or section, text anchor, extraction date, and extractor;
- OCR was checked against the source image and illegible text was not guessed;
- every definition and cross-reference has its own source row;
- clause text, performance records, correspondence, and regulatory context remain separate;
- public GLO examples are labeled as state-lease examples rather than private terms;
- RRC results preserve their reporting, revision, system, and legal-force limitations;
- unknowns are neutral questions rather than findings; and
- no row decides status, clause satisfaction, payment sufficiency, lease effect, ownership, property connection, production treatment, valuation inputs, value, or a transaction result.

A disciplined shut-in clause worksheet does something modest but useful: it makes the controlling words and missing evidence traceable. That reduces the risk that a later reviewer bases a legal or economic conclusion on a generic form, an incomplete excerpt, an unverified OCR line, or a regulatory record that answers a different question.

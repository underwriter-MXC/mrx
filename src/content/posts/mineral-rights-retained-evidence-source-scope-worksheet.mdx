> **Evidence-boundary stop.** This blank worksheet describes one already-retained artifact and one capture event. It does not search, retrieve, open, download, authenticate, interpret, compare, submit, purchase, or decide anything. Keep real artifacts, captured values, and integrity digests in an authorized controlled record. MRX may have an economic interest in a later transaction; when that applies, the buyer relationship is disclosed in writing before an agreement is signed.

**Mineral rights retained evidence source scope** is a narrow recordkeeping job. It preserves what one retained artifact says about its own scope and limitations, together with a controlled pointer and a limited byte-change reference when one already exists. It does not determine what the artifact proves.

That boundary matters because a filename, storage location, familiar source, or unchanged digest can look more conclusive than it is. The worksheet keeps those observations separate from authenticity, identity, authority, completeness, relevance, ownership, legal effect, valuation, offer quality, and transaction decisions.

## Direct answer

Create one worksheet ID for one already-retained artifact and one capture event. Record the source title and URL, capture timestamp and timezone, source type, retained filename and format, already-available integrity reference, controlled evidence pointer, source-stated scope, source-stated limitation, reviewer role, unresolved fields, and authorized-human handoff.

Copy source-stated scope and limitations exactly as displayed. If the artifact does not state one, use `not stated`. If the wording is unreadable or its boundary needs qualified review, use `unresolved`. Do not replace a gap with an assumption.

Stop there. Do not retrieve another copy, open an account, search for a related file, compare a sender, infer what a property or mineral interest is, or decide whether anyone should rely on the artifact.

## Start with one already-retained artifact

The worksheet begins after retention. An authorized reviewer must already have the artifact and the permitted descriptive information. This public template does not provide a route to fetch, download, order, scrape, or request anything.

Give the record a neutral identifier such as `[source-scope worksheet ID]`. Tie it to exactly one artifact and one capture event. Start a separate worksheet or version when any of these changes:

- the retained file or physical item;
- the capture timestamp or timezone;
- the source title or URL;
- the retained filename or format;
- the source-stated scope or limitation;
- the controlled evidence pointer; or
- the integrity reference being recorded.

Do not combine several files because they arrived together, have similar names, or appear in the same correspondence. Relationship analysis belongs in a separate authorized review.

## Use the federal-records material only as an analogy

The National Archives [Records Basics](https://www.archives.gov/records-mgmt/scheduling/basics) page distinguishes several categories in the federal-government context. It discusses source records, working files, information-system inputs, system content, and outputs. Those distinctions illustrate why a record should identify what kind of retained artifact it describes instead of treating every copy, input, or output as interchangeable.

That page governs federal records-management concepts. This worksheet does not import a federal record definition, preservation duty, disposition schedule, legal status, or agency rule into a private mineral-rights file. The source-type field is descriptive. If the retained artifact does not state a type, record `unresolved` rather than applying a federal label to it.

Likewise, do not relabel `source-stated scope` as mineral scope, property scope, title scope, ownership scope, valuation scope, offer scope, deal scope, or transaction scope. Preserve the artifact's wording without expanding it.

## Treat an integrity reference as byte-change evidence only

NIST's [FIPS 180-4 Secure Hash Standard](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/fips/180-4/upd1/final) specifies hash algorithms that generate message digests. NIST describes those digests as a way to detect whether messages changed after the digests were generated.

That is the permitted boundary here. If an authorized controlled record already contains a SHA-256 digest, the worksheet may identify its type and controlled location. It may support a limited comparison of bytes against that reference in a separate authorized process.

A digest does not prove:

- who created or sent the artifact;
- where it originated;
- whether it is authentic or authoritative;
- whether its contents are accurate or complete;
- whether it concerns a particular person, property, interest, or transaction;
- whether it has legal effect;
- whether it is relevant to a decision; or
- whether anyone owns or may act on anything it describes.

Do not generate a new digest through this public worksheet. Do not publish a real digest. Record only an already-available reference in the authorized controlled record.

## Copy stated scope and limitations without interpretation

Preserve exact displayed wording, including qualifiers, dates, page labels, and limiting phrases. Do not normalize a narrow statement into a broader one. Do not use surrounding correspondence, a likely filename, a neighboring page, a search snippet, or memory to fill a missing field.

Use one of three bounded entries:

- `exact displayed wording` when the source states the field clearly;
- `not stated` when the artifact does not display it; or
- `unresolved` when readability or meaning requires an authorized qualified reviewer.

The worksheet may record that a field exists, is not stated, or is unresolved. It may not decide whether the source is sufficient, trustworthy, complete, current, official, relevant, compliant, or suitable for reliance.

The [offer version-control and deadline register](/blog/mineral-rights-offer-version-control-and-deadline-register/) separates written versions and deadlines. This worksheet covers only one artifact and does not compare versions, track a deadline, or establish which copy controls.

The [offer correspondence index](/blog/mineral-rights-offer-correspondence-index/) organizes communication items. This worksheet does not identify a correspondent, connect messages, or compare a sender, signer, caller, buyer, owner, operator, or transaction party.

## Preserve the controlled evidence boundary

The public template contains labels and citations only. Real artifacts and completed values belong in an authorized system with appropriate access controls. The controlled record may retain the worksheet ID, artifact pointer, captured fields, integrity-reference location, unresolved questions, reviewer role, and handoff state.

Do not put credentials, account details, private contact data, title instruments, offers, tax information, legal communications, completed digests, or transaction documents into a public example. Do not create fictional values that could be mistaken for evidence.

## Stop at an authorized-human handoff

Close the worksheet with one neutral state: `recorded for authorized review`, `held for source clarification`, or `not used`. Name the unresolved field and the next authorized role. Do not use `authentic`, `verified`, `complete`, `owner confirmed`, `safe to rely on`, `safe to proceed`, or another conclusion.

An authorized human may decide that retrieval, source verification, legal review, title review, identity comparison, valuation work, or transaction review is needed. Each later job requires its own purpose, authority, sources, privacy controls, and evidence. It is outside this worksheet.

This source-scope worksheet records bounded descriptive evidence for one already-retained artifact. It does not provide legal, tax, title, land, records-management, appraisal, valuation, engineering, compliance, investment, offer, or transaction advice.

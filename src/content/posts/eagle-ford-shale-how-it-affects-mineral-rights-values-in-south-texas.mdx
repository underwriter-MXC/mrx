## What the Eagle Ford Shale is, and where its public records come from

The Eagle Ford Shale is a geologic formation in South Texas that produces oil, natural gas, and natural gas liquids. Public information about the formation and the wells in it comes from several authoritative sources:

- The U.S. Geological Survey (USGS) Eagle Ford Group assessment describes the geology and estimated continuous oil and gas resources for the formation.
- The Railroad Commission of Texas (RRC) publishes operator-reported production, permit, and well records for Eagle Ford wells.
- The U.S. Energy Information Administration (EIA) publishes Texas and U.S. crude oil and natural gas production and price series, which provide the price context behind a directional cash-flow model.

These public sources describe the formation as a whole. They do not, by themselves, determine who owns a specific interest or what that interest is worth.

## How Eagle Ford production and price data are organized

Operator-reported Eagle Ford production is published by the RRC through its production-data pages and the online research query portal. A user can search by lease name, operator, well number, or county. The EIA Texas profile summarizes state-level crude oil production, natural gas production, and prices, and the EIA spot-price series are commonly used as references in cash-flow models.

For any production or price figure cited in an article, the RRC query export, the EIA series identifier, the publication date, and the access date should be attached so the reader can reproduce the figure.

## What Eagle Ford public data can tell an owner

Eagle Ford public data is useful for several owner tasks:

- Verifying that the production shown on a royalty statement is consistent with the volumes reported to the RRC for the same lease and period.
- Identifying whether Eagle Ford activity in a county is increasing, stable, or declining relative to prior periods.
- Putting a specific interest in context against state-level Eagle Ford production and against the EIA price series.
- Supporting an MRX directional review that needs dated production and price inputs and an operator name to look up lease and well records.

## What Eagle Ford public data cannot tell an owner

Eagle Ford geology and production data, taken together, do not:

- Determine ownership. County deed, probate or estate, and other chain-of-title records must be reviewed separately; leases and division orders answer related contract and payment questions.
- Establish a royalty fraction. The royalty share is set in the lease and shown on the division order.
- Substitute for a valuation. A valuation requires dated production, dated prices, the royalty fraction, division-order decimals, and the lease and ownership documents.
- Predict future production. Wells are added, recompleted, and plugged over time; past Eagle Ford output is not, by itself, a forecast.

For any of these questions, public Eagle Ford data is one input among several, not an answer.

## How an owner can pair Eagle Ford public data with tract-level records

A useful Eagle Ford file moves from regional context to the exact wells and documents affecting the tract:

1. Locate the county, survey, abstract, or other legal-description reference in the owner's records.
2. Use the cited USGS assessment to understand the regional Eagle Ford formation context without treating its map as an ownership record.
3. Search the RRC records for the leases, wells, permits, and completions tied to that location and record the operator and relevant dates.
4. Match the reported production periods with the division order and recent royalty statements for the same property.
5. Investigate the recorded chain of title separately and involve a qualified title professional when the conveyance history is complex or incomplete.

## How MRX uses Eagle Ford data in its reviews

MRX's published methodology describes how it assembles a directional mineral-rights review from public data and owner-provided documents. Among other inputs, MRX uses RRC production data as a dated record of what is being produced on or near an interest, EIA price series as the price context behind a directional cash-flow model, and the cited USGS Eagle Ford assessment as the formation context. Those public inputs are combined with the lease, the division order, royalty statements, and the owner's other documents to support a directional range. The MRX methodology page describes the inputs and assumptions behind that process.

## When to bring in a qualified professional

Public Eagle Ford data can frame a question, but several related questions are outside public data:

- Ownership disputes, severance questions, and chain-of-title review typically require a title opinion from a qualified Texas oil and gas attorney or land professional.
- Royalty-fraction interpretation and division-order review can be reviewed by a division-order analyst or oil and gas attorney.
- Federal and state royalty-tax treatment is specific to each owner's facts and is outside an Eagle Ford data review. A CPA or tax attorney can address an owner's individual situation.

## Records an owner can request during a review

For a directional MRX review on an Eagle Ford interest, the following records are commonly useful:

- The current royalty statement(s) for the interest.
- The lease and any amendments.
- The division order(s) issued by the operator.
- Recent (typically 12 months) RRC production for the relevant leases.
- County deed records establishing the chain of ownership for the interest.

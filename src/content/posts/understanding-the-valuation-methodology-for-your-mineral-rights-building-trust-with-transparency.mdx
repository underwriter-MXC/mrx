## Answer first

A mineral-rights valuation methodology is most useful when it states what information was reviewed, what assumptions were used, and what the result does not establish. Public Texas records can help an owner ask informed questions about reported production and well-status context, but they do not prove ownership, resolve title, or establish a dollar value.

## Start with the scope of the review

Before relying on a valuation discussion or an offer, ask what interest and property are being considered, which documents were available, and which facts remain unconfirmed. A clear scope separates reported operational information from ownership, legal, tax, and appraisal questions that may require an appropriate outside professional.

That distinction matters because Texas materials recognize that oil-and-gas or mineral interests can be separate from surface interests. The [Texas Comptroller's Property Classification Guide](https://comptroller.texas.gov/taxes/property-tax/docs/96-313.pdf) provides classification context; it is not a title opinion or an appraisal of a particular interest.

## Use public records as question prompts

The Texas Railroad Commission (RRC) makes operator-reported production resources available through its [Oil and Gas Production Data](https://www.rrc.texas.gov/oil-and-gas/research-and-statistics/production-data/) page. The RRC also provides [Well Information](https://www.rrc.texas.gov/oil-and-gas/research-and-statistics/well-information/) query context for public well and status information.

Those resources can support practical questions such as:

- Which reported wells or production records were considered?
- What period of reported production was reviewed?
- Are any assumptions based on information that has not been confirmed?
- Which questions about ownership, title, lease terms, or taxes should be reviewed separately?

RRC information is useful operational context. It should not be treated as proof of who owns an interest or what that interest must be worth.

## Ask for assumptions in writing

Transparent methodology does not mean a result is guaranteed or that every uncertainty has been removed. It means an owner can identify the records, dates, assumptions, exclusions, and next questions behind a discussion. Written terms and a clear explanation of what could change are often more useful than a headline number alone.

## A practical review checklist

Use this checklist when evaluating a valuation discussion:

1. Confirm the property and interest being discussed.
2. Request a list of the documents and public records reviewed.
3. Ask which inputs are reported facts and which are assumptions.
4. Ask what is outside the review's scope.
5. Read proposed transaction terms carefully and seek legal, tax, title, or appraisal guidance when your facts call for it.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can RRC production data establish my ownership?

No. RRC production resources provide reported production context, not a title determination or ownership opinion.

### Does a valuation methodology establish a required sale price?

No. A methodology can explain inputs and assumptions, but it does not guarantee a transaction outcome or establish a required price.

### Why distinguish surface and mineral interests?

Texas classification materials recognize that these interests can be separate. That general context does not determine the ownership or value of a specific property.

### What should I bring to a review conversation?

Bring the records and offer documents you want to understand, then ask which facts are confirmed, which are assumptions, and which questions require outside advice.

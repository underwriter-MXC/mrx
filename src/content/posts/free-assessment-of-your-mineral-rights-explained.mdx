## Free Assessment of Your Mineral Rights Explained

> A free mineral rights assessment is a no-cost, no-obligation evaluation of your royalty or mineral interest by an independent underwriter using published DCF methodology on public production data. It is designed to give you an objective market-based benchmark before you accept, reject, or negotiate any offer.

### Key Takeaways

- **A free assessment is information, not a sales commitment**: It is a review of your royalty interest, working interest, or net mineral acres against public production data and basin-specific decline assumptions, not a contract to sell or a guarantee of any specific outcome.
- **You get a market-based value range, not a single price**: The output is a defensible valuation range built on discounted cash flow (DCF), comparable transactions, and royalty income multiple checks, the same components a sophisticated buyer uses internally.
- **Public production data is the foundation**: Reviews pull from the Texas Railroad Commission ([RRC](https://www.rrc.texas.gov/)) and the U.S. Energy Information Administration ([EIA Texas profile](https://www.eia.gov/state/?id=TX)) so the inputs are verifiable rather than buyer-controlled.
- **Use it before you sign anything**: The highest-value use of a free assessment is to compare an existing offer against an independent benchmark, before any contract, deed, or letter of intent is on the table.
- **The published methodology documents, not hides, the inputs**: The [MRX published methodology](/methodology/) lists every input the review weighs, production history, decline-curve context, royalty terms, risk-adjusted discount rate, and offer clauses, and shows how each one moves the resulting range.

## What a Free Mineral Rights Assessment Is

A free mineral rights assessment is an independent review of your interest, royalty, overriding royalty, working interest, or net mineral acres, that produces an objective value range using the same inputs a buyer uses, but without the buyer’s pricing incentives. It is not a third-party prepared professional appraisal report, not legal guidance, and not tax guidance. It is a structured market analysis built on:

- Public production data (RRC, EIA).
- Your lease terms and division orders.
- Commodity pricing context for the relevant Texas basin.
- Comparable transactions in your county and formation.

The output is a written valuation range and a clear list of the inputs that drove it. You keep the analysis whether or not you ever sell. See the [MRX offer review pillar](/offer-review/) for how the assessment fits into a broader decision workflow.

## What Is Reviewed in a Free Mineral Rights Assessment

The review covers the five components that determine what your interest is actually worth on a given day. The same five appear on the [MRX methodology page](/methodology/) and are derived from public sources.

1. **Production history and decline curve.** Well-by-well output from the [RRC public records](https://www.rrc.texas.gov/resource-center/research/data-sets-available-for-download/) and the [EIA petroleum datasets](https://www.eia.gov/petroleum/) is used to forecast remaining recoverable volume.
2. **Lease terms and royalty percentage.** Your division order and lease define the share of production revenue you receive. These documents are required to translate gross production into your net income.
3. **Operator and well control.** Operator quality, working-interest ownership, and existing permits affect how a buyer prices your interest.
4. **Basin and formation context.** Permian, Eagle Ford, Haynesville, and Barnett acreage each carry different development assumptions. The [EIA Texas state profile](https://www.eia.gov/state/?id=TX) provides the basin-level production context used in the review.
5. **Comparable transactions.** Recent sales of similar interests in your county establish a floor and ceiling for the valuation range.

## What You Receive From the Assessment

The deliverable is a written review that includes:

- A defensible value range expressed as dollars per net mineral acre and as a total offer range.
- A discounted cash flow (DCF) summary showing the forecasted production, price deck, and discount rate used.
- A royalty income multiple check, calculated by dividing the value range by the trailing monthly royalty income, used as a cross-check that the DCF and comparable-transactions methods are producing a market-consistent answer.
- A list of every input document you provided, so the valuation is auditable.
- A clear statement that the assessment is not a third-party prepared professional appraisal report, not legal guidance, and not tax guidance.

You do not give up any rights by requesting the assessment, and there is no obligation to act on the output.

## When to Use a Free Mineral Rights Assessment

The highest-value use cases are the ones where an independent benchmark changes the negotiation economics.

- **Before responding to an unsolicited offer.** Landmen and acquisition companies send unsolicited letters precisely because they know owners rarely verify the offer independently. A free assessment establishes an objective number to compare against before any response.
- **Before listing or marketing the interest.** Knowing the defensible value range lets you set a credible minimum acceptable offer and reject lowball bids at the door.
- **During estate or probate planning.** Multiple heirs often need to agree on a valuation. An independent written assessment gives the family a shared starting point. The federal stepped-up basis (26 USC § 1014) and [IRS Publication 551](https://www.irs.gov/forms-pubs/about-publication-551) govern the tax mechanics, but a market-based value range is the prerequisite.
- **Before a 1031 exchange decision.** If you are considering a like-kind exchange under [26 USC § 1031](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/1031), you need a defensible value to identify the replacement property basis. See [How to Navigate a 1031 Exchange for Mineral Rights](/blog/how-to-navigate-a-1031-exchange-for-mineral-rights/) for the timeline rules.

## How the Assessment Differs From a third-party prepared professional appraisal report

A free underwriter review is not a USPAP-third-party prepared professional appraisal report. It is a market analysis built on the same public data sources, but it does not carry the legal standing of a third-party prepared professional appraisal report and is not a substitute for one in a court, tax, or estate context where a third-party prepared professional appraisal report is required. For those contexts, engage a state-licensed appraiser. For day-to-day owner decisions, the free assessment is the appropriate level of review.

## What the Assessment Does Not Do

The assessment is conservative about what it promises. It does not:

- Guarantee a specific sale price or that any sale will close.
- Project a specific closing date or time-to-close.
- Provide legal guidance on title, probate, or contract enforcement.
- Provide tax guidance on basis, capital gains, or exchange treatment.
- Replace a third-party prepared professional appraisal report where one is required.

For any of those questions, the next step is a qualified attorney, CPA, or state-licensed appraiser.

## How to Prepare for the Free Assessment

Gather these documents before you request the review:

- Your most recent royalty check stub or division order.
- The lease or lease assignments that establish your royalty percentage.
- Any recent offer letters or marketing materials you have received.
- The county, section, and survey (or abstract) for the property.
- Any prior valuation reports or production records you already have.

If you do not have a document, the assessment can still proceed using public production data from the RRC and EIA; it just won’t have the precision of a fully documented review.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is a free mineral rights assessment really free?

Yes. A free mineral rights assessment is a no-cost, no-obligation review. You do not sign a listing agreement, grant an option, or give up any rights to receive the analysis. The output is yours whether or not you ever transact with the reviewing firm.

### What is the difference between a free assessment and an offer?

An offer is a specific dollar amount a buyer will pay, contingent on due diligence, title, and closing. A free assessment is the analytical foundation for an offer, a defensible market value range built on public production data, basin context, and comparable transactions. The assessment is the input; an offer is the output.

### How long does a free mineral rights assessment take?

Most assessments are completed within a few business days once the supporting documents are in hand. If the interest is in a basin with limited recent comparable transactions, the review may take longer because the comparable-transactions component requires more outreach.

### What information do I need to provide for a free assessment?

The minimum inputs are your county, section, royalty percentage, and the operator on the well. Royalty check stubs, division orders, and the underlying lease sharpen the precision. Anything you do not have can be reconstructed from RRC and EIA public records.

### Is a free assessment the same as a third-party prepared professional appraisal report?

No. A free assessment is a market analysis built on public data and the methodology published on the MRX site. A third-party prepared professional appraisal report is a report prepared under applicable professional appraisal standards prepared by a licensed appraiser and is a legal document for court, tax, and estate contexts. The free assessment is appropriate for owner decision-making; the third-party prepared professional appraisal report is appropriate for legal proceedings.

### Can I use a free assessment to negotiate a better offer?

Yes. The most common use is comparing any existing unsolicited offer to an independent value range. A documented range can help an owner compare assumptions and ask focused questions. It does not guarantee a higher offer or transaction outcome.

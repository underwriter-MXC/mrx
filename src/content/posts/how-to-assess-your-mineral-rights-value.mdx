## Answer first

Assessing a mineral-rights value discussion starts with identifying the interest and property, reviewing the records and assumptions involved, and understanding the limits of public data. The process is educational and does not determine title, ownership, taxes, legal consequences, or a certified appraisal value.

![MRX valuation landscape visual labeled “how to assess mineral rights value”.](/assets/articles/inline/how-to-assess-your-mineral-rights-value/how-to-assess-mineral-rights-value.webp)

## Identify the interest before discussing value

Ask which mineral interest and property are being considered and what documents support that discussion. The [Texas Comptroller's Property Classification Guide](https://comptroller.texas.gov/taxes/property-tax/docs/96-313.pdf) distinguishes surface interests from oil-and-gas/mineral interests for classification purposes. That general distinction is not proof of ownership or a determination of value for a specific interest.

## Review reported production and well context

The Texas RRC makes [Oil and Gas Production Data](https://www.rrc.texas.gov/oil-and-gas/research-and-statistics/production-data/) available and provides [Well Information](https://www.rrc.texas.gov/oil-and-gas/research-and-statistics/well-information/) resources. These public resources can help an owner ask which reported production or well-status information a review considered.

They do not provide title evidence and do not establish what an interest must be worth. Treat them as context for questions rather than a complete answer.

## Ask for the assumptions behind a discussion

Ask which inputs are reported facts, what dates apply, and what has not been verified. You can also ask what could change if further documents, title information, or written transaction terms become available. This makes it easier to distinguish the limits of a review from conclusions that require a qualified professional.

## Use a record-and-questions checklist

1. Identify the property and interest under discussion.
2. Collect the documents and offer materials you want to understand.
3. Ask which reported production or well information was used as context.
4. Ask which inputs are assumptions or unresolved.
5. Seek legal, tax, title, or certified-appraisal advice when the decision requires it.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can RRC production information determine my mineral-rights value?

No. It provides operator-reported production context that can inform questions; it does not determine title, ownership, or a dollar value.

### Does a separate mineral interest mean I own it?

No. The Comptroller guide provides classification context only. Ownership and title require the relevant records and, where appropriate, qualified advice.

### Should I rely on a review as an appraisal?

No. A review may explain records and assumptions, but it is not a certified appraisal unless separately identified and performed by an appropriately qualified professional.

### What should I do before accepting terms?

Read the written terms, ask what facts and assumptions underlie them, and seek outside advice when your transaction facts call for it.

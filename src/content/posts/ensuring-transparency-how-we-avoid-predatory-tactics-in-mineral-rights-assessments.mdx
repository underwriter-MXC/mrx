## Ensuring Transparency: How We Avoid Predatory Tactics in Mineral Rights Assessments

> Transparency in a mineral rights assessment means the owner can see every input, every assumption, and every number that drives the value range. The same framework that lets an underwriter avoid predatory tactics lets an owner ask any buyer the questions that surface whether an offer is fair. The framework is built on public production data, the [MRX published methodology](/methodology/), and a written comparison table.

### Key Takeaways

- **Transparency is a framework, not a promise**: It is the set of inputs, assumptions, and written outputs that an owner can verify against public data and the [MRX published methodology](/methodology/).
- **Public production data is the foundation**: Well-by-well production from the [Texas Railroad Commission](https://www.rrc.texas.gov/) and the [EIA Texas state profile](https://www.eia.gov/state/?id=TX) is verifiable by any qualified buyer.
- **Predatory tactics share identifiable patterns**: Missing methodology, clawback clauses, pressure to decide within a short deadline without allowing time for an independent review, and a headline number that does not match the underlying math are all signals.
- **The owner-protection framework is portable**: The same questions work with any buyer, not just one specific firm. The framework is the owner's defense regardless of who is on the other side.
- **The comparison framework is the deliverable**: A written valuation range with documented inputs is the output of a transparent assessment. A single dollar number without the inputs is not.

## What Transparency Means in a Mineral Rights Assessment

Transparency in mineral rights assessments is the property that lets an owner verify every number that drives the value range. It is not the same as friendliness, responsiveness, or a willingness to "explain the offer." It is the structural property that the inputs are public, the assumptions are documented, and the math is reproducible.

A transparent assessment answers six questions:

1. Where did the production data come from?
2. What decline curve and remaining reserve forecast were used?
3. What long-term price deck was assumed?
4. What discount rate was applied?
5. What comparable transactions were selected, and why?
6. What royalty income multiple was used to cross-check the DCF and comps?

A buyer who answers all six with documented inputs is operating transparently. A buyer who answers one or two and refuses the rest is not.

## How a Transparent Assessment Avoids Predatory Tactics

Predatory tactics share identifiable patterns. The transparent assessment is designed to remove the leverage those patterns depend on.

- **No missing methodology.** The buyer is asked for the multiple, the discount rate, the assumed decline, and the price deck. A buyer who refuses to disclose the methodology has revealed the structure of the offer.
- **No undisclosed clawback.** The contract is reviewed for due diligence adjustment rights, environmental contingency, and title contingency. Any provision that allows the buyer to reduce the price after signing is identified and quantified.
- **No pressure to decide quickly.** The owner is given the time and the documents to verify the offer against an independent valuation. A buyer who refuses to allow that time is signaling that the price is not defensible on the merits.
- **No anchoring to a teaser number.** The headline number is replaced by the post-clawback number, and the post-clawback number is compared against an independent valuation range built on the [MRX methodology](/methodology/) and public production data.

The defense is the same in every case: the comparison framework published on the [MRX offer review pillar](/offer-review/) and the questions that any owner can ask any buyer.

## The Owner-Protection Framework

The owner-protection framework is a set of questions that any mineral rights owner can ask any buyer. The questions are not specific to one firm; they are the questions that surface predatory tactics regardless of who is on the other side.

### Question 1: What Is the Implied Royalty Income Multiple?

The implied multiple is the offer divided by the trailing monthly royalty income. The [MRX published methodology](/methodology/) uses the multiple as one of three cross-checks to confirm the DCF and comparable-transactions methods are producing a market-consistent answer. The published methodology does not state a fixed market range; the multiple is a comparison tool, not a threshold. A buyer who will not disclose the multiple is asking you to take the price on faith.

### Question 2: What Is the Long-Term Price Deck?

The price deck is the long-term oil and gas price assumption that drives the DCF. A buyer who assumes a much lower price deck than the public consensus is producing a lower valuation. Ask for the price deck and compare it against the [EIA Annual Energy Outlook](https://www.eia.gov/outlooks/aeo/) and current strip prices.

### Question 3: What Decline Curve and Remaining Reserve Figure Did You Use?

The decline curve determines how fast production falls off and how much volume is left to recover. A buyer who assumes a more aggressive decline is producing a lower valuation. Ask for the decline curve and the remaining reserve figure, then compare against the [RRC public records](https://www.rrc.texas.gov/) for the relevant wells.

### Question 4: What Is the Due Diligence Clawback?

A clawback clause allows the buyer to reduce the price after the contract is signed. Ask for the clawback trigger, the maximum reduction, and the cure period. Refuse uncapped clawbacks; cap them at a fixed dollar amount with a clear definition of what triggers the adjustment.

### Question 5: What Is the Funding Source?

A buyer whose funds are not committed is not a buyer whose offer is firm. Ask whether the buyer's funds are in escrow, subject to financing, or subject to investor approval. If the funds are subject to financing, the offer is contingent on the buyer's financing, not on your performance.

### Question 6: How Many Comparable Transactions in My County and Formation Were Used?

A buyer who cannot point to recent comparable sales in your county and formation is not anchored to the market. Ask for the comparable transactions, the price per net mineral acre, and the implied multiple. The comparable-transactions selection should be documented.

## What a Buyer Who Operates Transparently Looks Like

A buyer who operates transparently:

- Shares the methodology. The multiple, the discount rate, the assumed decline, and the price deck are documented in the offer.
- Walks the math. The buyer is willing to explain how the offer was derived, in language the owner can verify.
- Welcomes the comparison. The buyer is comfortable with the owner sharing the offer with an independent underwriter for a second opinion.
- Puts everything in writing. There are no verbal-only commitments, no implied terms, and no "we'll work that out at closing" provisions.
- Allows time. The owner is given the time to consult with advisors, run an independent valuation, and ask follow-up questions.

The presence of all five behaviors is a useful indicator that the offer is being made in good faith.

## The Independent Valuation Is the Anchor

The owner-protection framework is most effective when the owner has an independent valuation to compare against. The valuation should be built on:

- Public production data from the [RRC](https://www.rrc.texas.gov/) and the [EIA Texas profile](https://www.eia.gov/state/?id=TX).
- The [MRX published methodology](/methodology/) for DCF construction, comparable-transactions selection, and royalty multiple review.
- A defensible value range, not a single point estimate.

Once the range is in hand, every offer either falls inside, above, or below it. The framework makes the comparison objective.

## Why the Framework Is Built on Public Data

Public data is verifiable. The [Texas Railroad Commission](https://www.rrc.texas.gov/) maintains well-by-well production records. The [EIA Texas state profile](https://www.eia.gov/state/?id=TX) provides the basin-level production context. Both are free to access and free to use.

A buyer who claims a different valuation for the same interest has to defend the claim against the same public production numbers. The owner can take the deliverable to any qualified buyer and ask the buyer to point to the specific input that produces a different number. This is the difference between an opinion and a market analysis.

## What the Framework Does Not Replace

The framework is precise about what it does and does not replace.

- **It does not replace a third-party prepared professional appraisal report.** For court, tax, or estate contexts where a third-party prepared professional appraisal report is required, the framework is not a substitute. Engage a state-licensed appraiser.
- **It does not replace legal guidance.** For title, probate, contract, or estate questions, the next step is a qualified attorney.
- **It does not replace tax guidance.** For basis, capital gains, or exchange treatment, the next step is a qualified CPA.
- **It does not guarantee a specific sale price.** The valuation is a market-based range; the actual transaction depends on negotiation, due diligence, and title.

These boundaries are documented in the [MRX methodology page](/methodology/) and on the [MRX offer review pillar](/offer-review/).

> This article is general educational information, not tax advice, legal advice, or a determination that any mineral or royalty interest qualifies for Section 1031 treatment. Consult qualified tax and legal advisors about your own transaction before taking action.

## Answer first

IRS materials limit Section 1031 exchanges to real property. A mineral or royalty interest should not be assumed to qualify automatically: eligibility depends on the interest and the facts of the transaction. A qualified tax and legal advisor should evaluate those facts before an owner relies on Section 1031.

## Start with the real-property limitation

The IRS explains Section 1031 rules in [Publication 544](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p544) and the [Instructions for Form 8824](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i8824). The Form 8824 instructions describe the real-property limitation. These resources are useful for general process education, but they do not decide whether a particular mineral or royalty interest qualifies.

Ask your advisors to review the nature of the interest, how it is held, the intended transaction, and any other facts relevant to the real-property requirement. This article does not provide a qualification conclusion.

## Know the deferred-exchange timeline

IRS materials describe two key deadlines for a deferred exchange: replacement property is generally identified within 45 days after the transfer of the relinquished property, and replacement property is generally received within 180 days. The 180-day period is subject to the earlier return-due-date rule described by the IRS.

Because the deadlines and documentation are consequential, involve qualified advisors early. Do not treat a general timeline summary as a substitute for advice about the dates that apply to you.

## Understand the qualified-intermediary boundary

The IRS materials also explain that use of a qualified intermediary does not cure a missed requirement. A qualified intermediary may have a role in a deferred exchange, but the taxpayer and their advisors still need to satisfy the applicable rules. Where applicable, IRS reporting includes Form 8824.

## Questions to take to your advisors

- Does my specific mineral or royalty interest meet the real-property requirement?
- What dates govern my 45-day identification and 180-day receipt periods?
- Does the earlier return-due-date rule affect my timeline?
- How should the transaction be structured and documented?
- Is Form 8824 required for my return, and what records should I retain?

## Frequently asked questions

### Do mineral rights automatically qualify for a Section 1031 exchange?

No. IRS materials limit Section 1031 exchanges to real property. A qualified tax and legal advisor must evaluate your specific interest and transaction facts.

### What are the general 45-day and 180-day rules?

In a deferred exchange, replacement property is generally identified within 45 days, and receipt is generally within 180 days, subject to the earlier return-due-date rule. See the IRS instructions and get advice on your dates.

### Does a qualified intermediary make every exchange valid?

No. IRS materials explain that a qualified intermediary does not cure a missed requirement.

### Is Form 8824 part of the process?

IRS materials describe Form 8824 reporting where applicable. Ask your tax advisor how it applies to your return.

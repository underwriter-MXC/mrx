> **Tax-office-directory-only boundary.** This blank worksheet documents one authorized manual attempt through the official Texas Comptroller county directory and one selected county page. Preserve only the source-displayed `Tax Assessor/Collector` section. A displayed entry does not establish identity, authenticity, completeness, currency, certification, collecting responsibility, jurisdiction, service scope, property connection, tax status, title, ownership, mineral connection, value, or legal effect. Keep completed worksheets, screenshots, contact values, and retained evidence in an authorized controlled system. MRX may have an economic interest in a later mineral transaction; when that applies, the buyer relationship is disclosed in writing before an agreement is signed.

A **Texas county tax assessor collector directory retrieval** can preserve where a county tax-office contact entry came from without turning it into a tax or property record. The worksheet records one official route, one selected county page, the labels displayed during one access event, and what prevented a bounded result.

This is not the appraisal-district worksheet. The same Comptroller county page can contain both sections, but each has a different heading, field set, and stated role. Use the [appraisal-district directory retrieval worksheet](/blog/texas-comptroller-county-appraisal-district-directory-retrieval-provenance-worksheet/) for that separate section. Do not merge the two records.

## Direct answer

Create one worksheet ID for one authorized manual attempt. Open the Texas Comptroller [Local Property Appraisal and Tax Information directory](https://comptroller.texas.gov/taxes/property-tax/county-directory/), select one source-displayed county code and name, and preserve the official county-page route.

Within that page, locate only the `Tax Assessor/Collector` section. Record the access timestamp and timezone; exact section and `Last Updated` labels; displayed tax-assessor/collector, phone, fax, email, website, mailing-address, and street-address labels; the exact `Collecting Unit` statement; `Consolidated Taxing Units Served` codes and names when shown; a controlled retained-evidence pointer; a source limitation; and one outcome: `located`, `not located`, or `unverified`.

Stop there. Do not copy the `Appraisal District` section, follow the local website into a record search, contact an office, submit a form, sign in, purchase, bypass, scrape, or interpret tax, title, ownership, mineral, value, or transaction questions.

## Keep the two directory sections separate

The Comptroller index distinguishes local appraisal questions from county tax-office questions. It says county tax assessor-collector offices can answer questions for the taxing units they serve about payment options, tax bills, tax certificates, tax rates, tax receipts, and other payment-related information.

That statement describes office-question categories. It does not decide which office serves a particular property or whether the directory entry is current. This worksheet preserves the directory trail only; it does not contact the office or ask any of those questions.

Do not copy these Article 217 fields into this worksheet:

- chief appraiser;
- taxpayer liaison;
- appraisal-district contact values;
- appraisal-district active-taxing-unit list; or
- any appraisal-district conclusion.

Do not copy the tax-assessor/collector section into Article 217. Shared county-page provenance does not make the two source sections interchangeable.

## Preserve collecting-unit language exactly

The official [Montgomery County directory page](https://comptroller.texas.gov/taxes/property-tax/county-directory/montgomery.php) demonstrates a `Tax Assessor/Collector` section with contact and address fields, a `Collecting Unit` label, and `Consolidated Taxing Units Served` codes and names. Those source-displayed fields validate the worksheet structure.

Another official page can display a different collecting-unit statement. The [Austin County directory page](https://comptroller.texas.gov/taxes/property-tax/county-directory/austin.php), for example, states that the tax office does not collect property taxes and points users toward the appraisal district for possible collection information.

Preserve either statement exactly. Do not convert a county name into `collects all county taxes`, treat a consolidated list as complete for every later purpose, or infer collecting responsibility from a link, address, office title, or the appraisal-district section.

## Preserve three controlled outcomes

Use only these outcomes:

- **Located:** the official selected-county page displayed the bounded `Tax Assessor/Collector` section during the documented attempt.
- **Not located:** the recorded official page did not display that bounded section during the attempt.
- **Unverified:** route identity, essential labels, access state, evidence retention, or another limitation prevented either bounded observation.

Do not convert `located` into `current officeholder`, `correct collector`, `serves this tract`, `collects this tax`, `tax is due`, `tax is paid`, or `tax record is valid`.

Do not convert `not located` into `no tax office`, `no collector`, `no taxing unit`, `no tax record`, `no property`, or `no mineral interest`. One page, section label, route, or access event may be incomplete or unavailable.

## Stop before local records or contact

The directory can display a local website, email, phone number, and office address. Those are source fields, not an instruction to act. Preserve them in the controlled record without opening a local search or initiating contact under this workflow.

Do not use this worksheet to:

- search by owner, heir, estate, address, parcel, account, legal description, or tax identifier;
- retrieve a bill, certificate, rate, receipt, delinquency, payment, protest, exemption, or appraisal record;
- decide which office collects for a property or taxing unit;
- call, email, mail, visit, or submit a form;
- create an account, sign in, or verify identity;
- pay a tax, fee, document charge, or access charge;
- bypass a CAPTCHA, rate limit, access control, or terms;
- scrape or bulk collect directory or local records; or
- infer title, ownership, heirship, mineral connection, value, offer quality, or transaction readiness.

If the task changes from preserving the directory entry to searching records or contacting an office, close this worksheet and create a separately authorized workflow with its own purpose, access, privacy, evidence, and human-review rules.

## Keep contact values and evidence controlled

The reusable public template needs no completed person name, office contact, phone, email, website, address, collecting unit, taxing-unit list, screenshot, tax record, property identifier, estate detail, mineral description, amount, offer, signature, or result.

Use one controlled retained-evidence pointer for the official page capture or note. Do not publish a directory capture as proof that a person, office, property, tax status, or mineral interest has been verified.

If an authorized reviewer later needs to confirm a contact, determine collecting responsibility, or search a local tax system, that action requires a separate record. This worksheet ends before contact, submission, or local-system access.

## Keep tax and mineral conclusions separate

A tax-assessor/collector directory entry does not identify a mineral owner, explain a tax value, prove a payment, establish a tax status, allocate title, connect an estate to a tract, model production, calculate present value, or evaluate an offer.

## Stop at authorized-human review

Close the worksheet with the controlled outcome, limitation, unresolved question, and next authorized role. Do not answer the later question inside this retrieval record.

Questions about collecting responsibility, local tax records, bills, certificates, rates, receipts, payments, exemptions, protests, office contact, tax interpretation, title, ownership, heirship, mineral connection, valuation, or transaction conduct require the applicable local office, qualified Texas tax or legal counsel, a title professional, or another appropriately qualified reviewer.

This worksheet documents one manual official-directory retrieval attempt. It does not provide legal, probate, tax, title, appraisal, valuation, investment, financial, identity-verification, offer, closing, or transaction advice.

## Frequently asked questions

### Does the directory prove that the county office collects a specific tax?

No. Preserve the collecting-unit statement and displayed taxing-unit labels exactly. Determining responsibility for a particular property or tax requires separate source evidence and qualified review.

### Can a displayed taxing-unit code be connected to my mineral interest?

Not in this workflow. Preserve the code and name only as directory fields. A property or mineral connection requires separate records and review.

### What if a contact field is blank?

Record it as blank or not stated. Do not fill the gap from memory, a search result, a local site, the appraisal-district section, or another county page.

### What if the source page changes later?

Use a new worksheet version or ID. Preserve both access times, routes, displayed labels, retained pointers, and limitations without declaring which version is controlling.

### Can I use the local website linked from the tax-office section?

The link can be preserved as a displayed field. Opening it for a record search, payment, contact, login, or submission is outside this worksheet and requires a separate authorized workflow.

> **Tax-record boundary.** This page explains how to use current official IRS channels to retrieve available tax transcripts and preserve a controlled handoff to a qualified tax professional. It does not choose a transcript, interpret an entry, determine completeness, establish mineral ownership or basis, calculate gain or tax, validate an offer, recommend a sale or exchange, complete a form, file a return, or set a retention period. Keep taxpayer information and transcript files inside an authorized controlled system. MRX may have an economic interest in a later mineral transaction; when that applies, the buyer relationship is disclosed in writing before an agreement is signed.

**Cash for mineral rights tax transcripts** can support a CPA's source review only when their limited IRS-defined scope remains clear. Retrieve the record through an official IRS channel, preserve the unaltered copy, record how and when it was obtained, disclose what is unavailable or unresolved, and stop before interpretation.

An IRS transcript is not a mineral deed, lease, division order, title opinion, closing statement, appraisal, basis schedule, complete return copy, or complete transaction history. It can show federal tax information within the scope of the requested transcript type. The CPA and attorney must decide whether that information is relevant, complete enough for their work, and consistent with the other controlled records.

## Direct answer

Use this bounded sequence:

1. Ask the CPA which transcript type, tax form, and years fit the professional engagement.
2. Open a current official IRS retrieval route rather than a search result, email link, or third-party form mirror.
3. Retrieve only your own available record through the authorized route.
4. Preserve the original downloaded or mailed record without editing, highlighting, renaming fields, or merging it with another source.
5. Record the official route, transcript type, tax years requested and obtained, retrieval date and timezone, controlled-copy location, and any availability or scope limitation.
6. Hand the controlled record to the CPA through the method selected for that engagement.

This sequence organizes source evidence. It does not decide which record is required, what a transcript entry means, whether a payment was reported correctly, how basis should be reconstructed, what tax treatment applies, or whether a cash offer should be accepted.

## Start with the current official IRS routes

The IRS [Get your tax records and transcripts](https://www.irs.gov/individuals/get-transcript) page currently identifies several routes:

- **Individual Online Account:** an authenticated taxpayer can view, print, or download the transcript types offered through the account.
- **Get transcript by mail:** the IRS offers tax return or tax account transcripts by mail to the address it has on file. The IRS currently states a five-to-ten-calendar-day delivery estimate.
- **Automated phone service:** the IRS currently identifies `800-908-9946` for mailed return or account transcript requests.
- **Form 4506-T:** the IRS identifies the current paper form as a route for the transcript types listed on the form.

These routes are not interchangeable in scope or availability. Do not choose a route from this article alone. Let the CPA identify the record and years needed, then confirm the current IRS page and form before acting. Delivery and processing statements are estimates, not deadlines or guarantees.

Avoid links in unsolicited email, text messages, or social posts. The IRS retrieval page cautions that the agency does not initiate those contacts to request personal or financial information. Navigate to the official `irs.gov` page independently.

## Keep the transcript types separate

The current IRS [transcript-types page](https://www.irs.gov/individuals/transcript-types-for-individuals-and-ways-to-order-them) defines five individual record types. Preserve the IRS label and scope rather than treating every record as a generic tax transcript.

### Tax return transcript

The IRS says a tax return transcript shows most line items from the original Form 1040-series return as filed, along with forms and schedules. It does not show later changes. The IRS currently lists it for the current and three prior tax years.

That scope does not make it a complete return copy, a basis schedule, or proof that every source relevant to mineral ownership or a later sale is present.

### Tax account transcript

The IRS says a tax account transcript shows basic data such as filing status, taxable income, payment types, and changes made after the original return was filed. The current availability varies by route: generally the current and nine prior tax years in Individual Online Account, and the current and three prior tax years by mail or automated phone, subject to IRS limitations.

An account entry still requires professional interpretation. It does not identify mineral title, explain why an adjustment occurred, or reconstruct a transaction by itself.

### Record of account transcript

The IRS describes this as a combination of the return and account transcripts. It currently lists the current and three prior tax years.

Combined does not mean complete for a mineral rights decision. It does not add deeds, leases, division orders, acquisition records, closing files, or professional workpapers that are outside the IRS transcript.

### Wage and income transcript

The IRS says this transcript shows data from information returns it receives, including Forms W-2, 1098, 1099, and 5498. The current page warns that it shows only information-return documents filed with the IRS, may not reflect every document issued, and is limited to about 85 income documents in the online account. It currently lists the current and nine prior tax years, with current-processing-year information generally appearing in early February.

A missing entry is not proof that a payment, mineral interest, information return, or transaction did not exist. Record the limitation and send the question to the CPA. If an expected information return is missing, the current Form 4506-T also tells taxpayers to contact the payer first when a copy of a Form W-2 or Form 1099 is needed.

### Verification of non-filing letter

The IRS says this letter states that it has no record of a processed Form 1040-series return as of the request date. The IRS expressly says that the letter does not indicate whether the taxpayer was required to file. Current-year letters are available after June 15, with different routes for older years.

Do not turn a non-filing letter into a conclusion about filing obligations, mineral ownership, income, a sale, or tax compliance. Those are professional questions outside this retrieval record.

## Use a source-scope handoff matrix

The in-body matrix is a routing aid, not a transcript-selection tool.

| Transcript class | IRS-stated contents | Bounded handoff context | What it cannot establish |
| ---- | ---- | ---- | ---- |
| Tax return transcript | Most original Form 1040-series line items, forms, and schedules | Preserve the original-filing view supplied by the IRS | Later changes, complete return copy, title, basis, or complete mineral history |
| Tax account transcript | Basic account data, payments, and later account changes | Preserve the account-activity view for CPA review | Why an entry changed, ownership, title, basis, or transaction treatment |
| Record of account | Return transcript plus account transcript | Keep the combined IRS record as one unaltered source | Deeds, leases, acquisition evidence, valuation, or complete transaction files |
| Wage and income transcript | Information-return data received by the IRS | Identify only the reported source and year shown in the controlled record | Every document issued, complete reporting, ownership, basis, or tax character |
| Verification of non-filing | No processed Form 1040-series return on record as of the request date | Preserve the exact letter and date for CPA review | Whether filing was required, whether income or property existed, or tax compliance |

## Preserve the unaltered copy

Download or retain the transcript exactly as delivered. Do not edit the PDF, cover entries, combine pages from different transcript types, paste a transcript into a worksheet, or treat a screenshot as the controlled source.

Use a separate handoff record for administrative notes:

- **Preparation ID:** `[controlled matter pointer]`
- **Professional request pointer:** `[CPA-provided record request]`
- **IRS route:** `[Individual Online Account, mail, phone, or current Form 4506-T]`
- **Transcript label:** `[exact IRS record type shown]`
- **Tax years requested:** `[controlled request pointer]`
- **Tax years obtained:** `[controlled source pointer]`
- **Retrieved or received at:** `[controlled timestamp and timezone]`
- **Original controlled copy:** `[authorized repository pointer]`
- **Availability or scope limitation:** `[unavailable year, unpopulated data, route limit, legibility issue, or unresolved]`

- **Handoff event:** `[controlled transmission record]`
- **Routing state:** `[retrieved, retrieval-pending, or professional-review-required]`

The bracketed phrases are instructions, not sample taxpayer facts. Do not enter names, Social Security numbers, addresses, account numbers, tax years, dollar amounts, properties, payers, offers, or transcript entries in a public template, URL, analytics event, screenshot, or image.

`Retrieved` means only that the controlled source was obtained through the recorded route. `Retrieval-pending` means the requested source has not yet been obtained. `Professional-review-required` means selection, interpretation, reconciliation, or another professional decision remains open. None of these states establishes accuracy, completeness, basis, tax treatment, title, offer quality, or filing readiness.

## Respect transcript masking and visible financial data

The IRS [About tax transcripts](https://www.irs.gov/individuals/about-tax-transcripts) page explains that most personal identifiers are partly masked, but all financial entries remain visible. It also explains that unmasked wage-and-income transcripts may be provided for return preparation and can display full personal information.

Masked does not mean safe for public sharing. Treat every transcript as protected taxpayer information. Keep it out of ordinary email threads, public drives, project-management comments, screenshots, image assets, analytics, and the reusable worksheet. Ask the CPA which secure exchange method fits the engagement rather than treating one product or channel as universally sufficient.

The IRS also states that it does not fax transcripts and does not mail Form 4506-T transcripts to third parties. Preserve the taxpayer-to-IRS retrieval event, then record the separate authorized professional handoff.

## Keep Form 4506-T revision-controlled

The IRS [About Form 4506-T](https://www.irs.gov/forms-pubs/about-form-4506-t) page links to the current form. As reviewed on August 26, 2026, the official [Form 4506-T PDF](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-pdf/f4506t.pdf) is revision April 2025.

If a CPA directs use of the paper route, open the current IRS landing page again. Record the revision shown and preserve the request through the controlled matter file. Do not use a cached form, a third-party copy, or an address copied from an old article. The IRS maintains a separate current where-to-file page and warns that web addresses may be newer than the printed form.

This article does not walk through form fields. The professional must identify the tax form number, transcript type, years, signer, authority, current destination, and any other request detail for the actual taxpayer.

## Distinguish a transcript from a complete return copy

[IRS Topic 156](https://www.irs.gov/taxtopics/tc156) distinguishes free transcript products from a complete copy of a filed return. A complete copy, when relevant, uses Form 4506 rather than Form 4506-T and follows a separate process.

Do not order a complete copy merely because this distinction exists. Let the CPA determine whether the controlled file needs a transcript, a complete return copy, payer records, acquisition evidence, or another source. This page does not state a current copy fee or recommend a paid request.

## Reconcile the handoff, not the tax result

An administrative reviewer can verify the handoff without interpreting tax data:

- Does the source come from an official IRS route?
- Does the record retain the exact transcript label shown by the IRS?
- Are requested and obtained years recorded separately?
- Is the downloaded or mailed source preserved unaltered?
- Is the retrieval or receipt date and timezone captured?
- Is the controlled location accessible to the authorized professional?
- Is any unavailable, delayed, unpopulated, truncated, or inconsistent information disclosed as a limitation?
- Is the separate CPA handoff event recorded?

Stop there. Do not mark the record complete for tax work, reconcile a transcript to a sale, infer basis, classify income, calculate gain, verify an offer, or reach a filing conclusion.

## Stop at professional review

Hand off immediately when the work requires:

- choosing a transcript type, tax form, or year;
- interpreting a transcript code, amount, payer entry, adjustment, payment, penalty, or status;
- deciding whether an expected record is missing or whether reporting is complete;
- reconstructing acquisition history, adjusted basis, depletion, depreciation, recapture, proceeds, gain, loss, or tax;
- classifying a payment, royalty, lease bonus, rent, sale, or other transaction;
- deciding which return, schedule, statement, correction, or amendment is required;
- completing Form 4506-T, Form 4506, a return, or another tax submission;
- setting a retention or destruction date;
- authenticating a deed, lease, assignment, division order, title record, closing file, or contract;
- valuing a mineral interest, validating an offer, comparing buyers, or recommending a transaction; or
- reaching an owner-specific tax, accounting, title, legal, valuation, investment, offer, or transaction conclusion.

The CPA owns federal tax-record selection, interpretation, reconciliation, calculation, filing, and retention decisions. An attorney owns title, ownership, contract, and legal questions. A clean retrieval trail helps them find the controlled source; it does not replace their work.

## Frequently asked questions

### Does an IRS transcript prove my mineral rights tax basis?

No. A transcript displays federal tax information within the scope of the requested transcript type. It does not establish mineral title, acquisition history, adjusted basis, or the treatment of a specific sale. A CPA must reconcile the controlled records for the actual facts.

### Which transcript should I request before reviewing a cash offer?

This article does not choose a transcript. Ask the CPA handling the engagement which IRS record type, tax form, and years are relevant. Then use a current official IRS route and preserve what was actually obtained.

### Can the IRS mail my Form 4506-T transcript directly to a buyer or CPA?

The IRS states that it no longer mails Form 4506-T transcripts to third parties. The taxpayer receives the record and should coordinate an appropriate secure handoff with the authorized professional.

### Does a missing Form 1099 entry prove that no royalty payment or sale occurred?

No. The IRS states that a wage-and-income transcript displays information returns filed with the IRS and may not reflect every document issued. Current-period information can also take time to populate. Missing data is a limitation to route to the CPA, not negative proof.

### Is a tax transcript the same as a complete copy of my filed return?

No. IRS Topic 156 distinguishes transcripts from complete return copies. A CPA should decide whether a transcript, a complete copy, or another controlled source is relevant to the engagement.

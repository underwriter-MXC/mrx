> **Public-record retrieval boundary.** This article records one Texas RRC Online Inspection Lookup attempt. It does not interpret a criterion, tab, operator, lease, well, facility, API, permit, complaint, inspection date, statewide rule, result, compliance field, violation, enforcement field, export, placeholder, or blank value. It does not establish property connection, title, ownership, acreage, lease effect, production, reserves, cash flow, condition, liability, value, offer quality, legal effect, or a transaction result. It does not provide legal, title, land, tax, accounting, appraisal, engineering, geological, environmental, regulatory, or financial advice. MRX may have an economic interest in a later transaction; when that applies, the buyer relationship is disclosed in writing before an agreement is signed.

A **Texas RRC Online Inspection Lookup retrieval** worksheet answers one narrow question: exactly how was one official retrieval attempt performed? Record the official entry and application routes, exact source-displayed labels, access timestamp and timezone, controlled chosen criteria, navigation or displayed state, authorized retained reference, source limitations, and one bounded retrieval status.

Use only **located**, **not located**, or **unverified**. Those words describe the retrieval attempt. They do not decide whether a displayed row relates to a mineral interest, what an inspection or violation means, whether a person complied with a rule, whether a site has a particular condition, or whether any transaction action is appropriate.

## Start with the exact RRC OIL route

The Railroad Commission of Texas [Research Queries](https://www.rrc.texas.gov/resource-center/research/research-queries/) directory lists **RRC Online Inspection Lookup (RRC OIL)** as a separate research tool. Preserve the exact directory label, directory URL, launch URL, access time, and any source-displayed access or data limitation. Do not substitute a search-engine result, an old bookmark, or a neighboring RRC application for the current official route.

The official [About RRC Online Inspection Lookup](https://www.rrc.texas.gov/resource-center/research/research-queries/about-rrc-online-inspection-lookup/) page says RRC OIL searches inspection and violation data recorded by Oil and Gas Division inspectors for leases, wells, facilities, and other sites. It also says the application retrieves completed inspections, while inspections in progress or under review are not displayed. Its stated online coverage begins in August 2015, and the page identifies a separate open-records path for earlier information.

Those are source boundaries, not findings about a particular operator, site, well, or mineral interest. The same page states that the data is updated nightly and describes the online query as informational rather than an authoritative public record with legal force. Preserve those statements with the attempt. Do not turn a current access timestamp into a claim that every underlying record is current, complete, accurate, or suitable for another purpose.

The official application route is [RRC Online Inspection Lookup](https://webapps2.rrc.texas.gov/PDA/ice/pdaIceHome.xhtml). It currently presents separate **Inspections** and **Violations** tabs. A retrieval worksheet should identify which tab was used. Opening one tab does not document a search of the other.

For broader orientation, use [Texas Railroad Commission: How to Use Public Records to Understand Your Mineral Rights](/blog/texas-railroad-commission-how-to-use-public-records-to-understand-your-mineral-rights/). For a different route-specific job, use the [Texas RRC Orphan Well Query Retrieval Provenance Worksheet](/blog/texas-rrc-orphan-well-query-retrieval-provenance-worksheet/). Keep attempt records separate even when the same operator, county, lease, district, API, or well label appears across systems.

## Create an attempt header before entering criteria

Give the attempt a neutral ID such as `TX-RRC-OIL-RET-01`. That identifier labels a retrieval session, not an inspection, violation, operator, site, mineral interest, finding, condition, obligation, or transaction. Record:

- the Research Queries directory route and exact directory label;
- the about-page route and exact page label;
- the launched application route, HTML title, visible application name, and selected tab;
- access date, time, and timezone;
- preparer role and worksheet version;
- stated purpose, limited to one RRC OIL retrieval;
- source-stated coverage, update, display, export, and informational-use limitations; and
- the controlled location where real criteria and retained evidence will be stored.

Create a new attempt ID for every rerun. Do not replace an earlier record because a label, criterion, table, export control, result state, error, or access condition changed. A sequence of preserved attempts shows what was actually retrievable at each recorded time.

## Record only the criteria the source displays

The current Inspections tab displays criteria for Operator Name, Operator No, County, Lease Name, Lease No, Oil & Gas District, API No, Complaint No, Compliance, Inspection Date From, Inspection Date To, Drilling Permit No, and Statewide Rule. The source also displays Search and Reset controls.

The official 18-page [RRC OIL User Guide](https://webapps2.rrc.texas.gov/PDA/resources/docs/RRC_OIL_User_Guide.pdf) describes those criteria and the search sequence. It notes, for example, that some name searches use matching or beginning text and that the date fields use a stated format. Record the source-displayed label, the actual chosen search mode, whether a default appeared, whether the preparer changed it, and the controlled evidence reference for the value used.

Do not copy real values into a reusable public worksheet. Use placeholders such as `[controlled county value]`, `[controlled date range]`, or `[controlled API reference]`. A criterion is an input to one attempt. It does not validate the entity named, prove a property relationship, or establish the meaning of a returned row.

## Preserve the selected tab and displayed state

An attempt may stop at the initial application, a validation message, an empty result grid, a populated result grid, a detail view, an export control, an error, or an access boundary. Record the displayed state in neutral language:

- Inspections tab displayed with no search submitted;
- Violations tab displayed with no search submitted;
- controlled criteria entered and Search selected;
- source validation or constraint displayed;
- result grid displayed with zero visible rows;
- result grid displayed with one or more visible rows;
- sought controlled reference displayed or not displayed;
- pagination, sorting, filtering, detail, or Excel control displayed;
- error, timeout, session, or access state displayed; or
- source state could not be verified.

The current application can display column headings and controls even when no result rows are visible. Preserve the difference between an empty table structure, a submitted query with no visible match, and an attempt for which the submission state was not captured. Do not collapse all three into “no record.”

If the source displays a substantive label, value, row, detail, or notice, preserve the exact source wording in controlled evidence. In the reusable worksheet, record only that the named field or state was captured. Avoid paraphrases that strengthen, simplify, or broaden what the source displayed.

## Treat blanks and placeholders as source state

The official user guide warns that some records may have blank fields or placeholder values because applicable information was not available to the inspector for the inspected location. The guide names examples including operator, lease, API, well, permit, and field information.

A blank or placeholder is not permission to fill the field from memory or another system. Record the exact field label, the fact that the source displayed a blank or placeholder, the attempt timestamp, and the controlled reference. If a different official source is later consulted, create a separate retrieval record and link the two references without merging their meanings.

The guide also separates data by the type of regulated location. That is another reason to preserve the source interface and exact displayed state. It is not a basis for MRX to infer why a field is present, absent, blank, or populated for a particular row.

## Record navigation, filtering, and export mechanics

The user guide describes result-page navigation, column sorting, column filtering, and an Excel control. It says an exported file contains the same data columns as the onscreen result grid and states a maximum of 10,000 records for that export path. It separately describes full completed-inspection and violation data-set downloads for the state and Oil and Gas districts.

If the attempt uses one of those controls, record:

- the selected tab and criteria state before the action;
- the exact visible control or column label;
- the sort, filter, page, detail, or export action taken;
- the displayed state immediately after the action;
- the capture or file timestamp and timezone;
- the controlled filename and SHA-256 when a file is retained; and
- whether the file came from the query-result Excel path or a separate full-data-set route.

Do not publish the export merely because it came from a public site. The file may contain identifiers or facts that should remain in an authorized case location. A file hash proves byte identity for the retained artifact; it does not prove accuracy, completeness, ownership, legal effect, or relevance to a mineral interest.

## Retain evidence in a controlled location

The reusable worksheet should contain a controlled reference, not the underlying sensitive evidence. A retained-evidence record can identify:

- attempt ID and worksheet version;
- authorized repository, case system, or evidence-vault location;
- capture, note, or export filename;
- capture timestamp and timezone;
- file SHA-256 or other integrity reference;
- source route, selected tab, and displayed page label;
- access role and retention rule; and
- whether a later reviewer independently repeated the retrieval.

Do not publish credentials, session tokens, cookies, private correspondence, personal contact details, actual operator or property identifiers, complaint numbers, result rows, screenshots, exports, inspection details, violation details, enforcement information, or internal case notes. If evidence must be shared with an authorized professional, provide the controlled reference and the minimum necessary underlying record through an approved channel.

## Assign only a bounded retrieval status

Choose one status after the attempt:

1. **Located:** the recorded route exposed the specific controlled reference sought.
2. **Not located:** the recorded attempt did not expose that reference using the stated criteria.
3. **Unverified:** the route, selected tab, criteria, displayed state, retained reference, or source limitation was not captured well enough to reproduce the attempt.

`Located` does not authenticate a record, establish that it applies to a mineral interest, or interpret any field. `Not located` does not prove absence. `Unverified` is the correct status when the evidence trail is incomplete.

End with one neutral next question. For example: should the attempt be rerun with a corrected controlled criterion, should a separately named official route be searched, or should the relevant agency office be asked a source-specific question? Do not end with a conclusion about an inspection, violation, compliance, operator, site condition, complaint, enforcement, liability, ownership, value, offer, or transaction.

## Keep contact routing neutral

The RRC [Inspections and Violations](https://www.rrc.texas.gov/resource-center/inspections-and-violations/) page provides agency-level context for inspection and violation information. The agency's [Locations](https://www.rrc.texas.gov/about-us/locations/) page provides district-office routing information. If a retrieval question requires contact, record the page used to identify the office, the office label, the date and time, the narrow question asked, and the controlled correspondence reference.

A contact attempt, referral, acknowledgment, delay, or nonresponse is not confirmation of a record. Preserve it as communication provenance only. Do not imply that an agency office has endorsed MRX's interpretation or connected a public record to a private interest unless an authorized source expressly supplies that statement and the separate review scope permits it.

## Protect the mineral owner's information

Store real operator, API, lease, well, facility, permit, complaint, district, county, criterion, result, violation, compliance value, inspection date, enforcement action, screenshot, export, property, title, payment, and correspondence details in an access-controlled location. Share only records you are authorized to provide and disclose why each record is requested.

MRX may act as an educator, evaluator, marketplace operator, or possible buyer. If MRX or an affiliate could acquire an interest, that economic relationship should be stated clearly before a transaction agreement is signed. A provenance worksheet improves reproducibility; it does not make MRX a public agency, title examiner, attorney, engineer, geologist, environmental consultant, accountant, appraiser, regulator, or neutral fiduciary.

## Final retrieval check

Before closing the attempt, confirm that the worksheet preserves the complete official route, exact source labels, selected tab, access time and timezone, controlled criteria, navigation, displayed result or error state, retained reference, source limitations, bounded status, and one neutral next question. If one item is missing, use **unverified** until the retrieval record is complete.

Stop there. The next step may be another official-source retrieval or an appropriately scoped professional review. It is not a conclusion about an inspection, violation, compliance, operator, lease, facility, well, complaint, enforcement, liability, regulatory significance, environmental condition, property connection, title, ownership, acreage, legal effect, value, offer quality, or transaction.

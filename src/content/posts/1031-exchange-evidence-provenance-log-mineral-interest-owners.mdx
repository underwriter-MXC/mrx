> **Record-organization boundary.** This blank log records provenance for records that already exist in one controlled exchange file. It does not decide what records are required, whether a mineral-related interest or transaction qualifies, whether a deadline was met, what a record proves, how long a particular owner must retain it, or how a form, return, or transaction should be completed. Keep names, TINs, addresses, property descriptions, dates, amounts, agreements, statements, notices, and workpapers in an authorized controlled system. MRX may have an economic interest in a later mineral transaction; when that applies, the buyer relationship is disclosed in writing before an agreement is signed.

**1031 exchange recordkeeping for mineral interests** is easier to review when each existing record has a traceable source, custodian, retrieval event, controlled location, limitation, and professional owner. A provenance log preserves that trail without deciding whether the evidence is sufficient or what the tax result should be.

This is not a document checklist. It does not tell an owner which items must exist. It is also not a 1031 process guide, eligibility test, deadline tracker, qualified-intermediary evaluation, Form 8824 map, tax calculator, or filing worksheet. Use it only to organize records already supplied or retrieved for an exchange file that the professional team is handling.

## Direct answer

Create one controlled log ID for one exchange file. Give each existing source a separate row. Record the source class, origin or supplying party through a controlled pointer, current custodian, date shown by the source, retrieval timestamp and method, controlled-copy location, integrity note, limitation, and professional handoff state.

Use one routing state:

- **Retrievable:** an authorized reviewer can locate the controlled source through the recorded pointer.
- **Gap-open:** the source, custody step, retrieval path, controlled copy, or limitation remains unresolved.
- **Professional-review-required:** interpretation, characterization, reconciliation, retention, calculation, selection, or advice is required.

These are routing states only. None establishes authenticity, completeness, relevance, qualification, timing, tax treatment, legal effect, or filing readiness.

## Use a blank provenance row

- **Log ID:** `[controlled preparation ID]`
- **Record class:** `[bounded category supplied by the professional team]`
- **Origin or supplying party:** `[controlled contact or system pointer]`
- **Original custodian:** `[controlled role pointer]`
- **Current custodian:** `[controlled role pointer]`
- **Source date as displayed:** `[controlled source field pointer]`
- **Retrieved at and timezone:** `[controlled access-event pointer]`
- **Retrieval method:** `[authorized portal, secure delivery, record room, or other controlled route]`
- **Controlled copy location:** `[authorized repository pointer]`
- **Integrity note:** `[original, certified copy, downloaded copy, scan, or unresolved]`
- **Source limitation:** `[missing page, illegible field, conflicting version, incomplete custody, or not reviewed]`
- **Professional owner:** `[qualified intermediary, CPA, attorney, or other authorized role]`
- **Routing state:** `[retrievable, gap-open, or professional-review-required]`

The bracketed phrases are instructions, not sample facts. Do not invent a taxpayer, property, legal description, party, agreement, date, amount, notice, transfer, filing, deadline result, or completed outcome.

## Keep provenance separate from meaning

A provenance log answers a narrow question: **Where did this existing record come from, and how can the authorized reviewer find the controlled source?** It does not answer whether the record is legally effective, factually complete, relevant to a tax position, or sufficient for a transaction.

For example, a retrieved agreement can have a clear portal path and an intact controlled copy while still requiring professional review. A source date can be captured exactly as displayed without deciding whether that date controls identification, transfer, receipt, return, or filing timing. A custody chain can be complete without establishing that the underlying transaction qualifies.

## Preserve the source origin

For each item already in the exchange file, identify the closest available origin without copying protected facts into the reusable template:

- the office, portal, counterparty, professional, record room, or controlled system that supplied it;
- whether the item was viewed in an original system, received through secure delivery, or copied from another controlled file;
- the source's displayed title or category through a controlled pointer;
- the displayed revision or source date through a controlled pointer; and
- any visible limitation, such as a missing page, partial scan, unreadable field, or inconsistent version.

Do not label a source `official`, `controlling`, `complete`, or `correct` merely because it came from a familiar portal or professional. Record the origin and limitation. Let the authorized reviewer decide what weight the source deserves.

## Record custody without exposing identities

Custody shows who controlled the record at each bounded step. In the reusable log, use role or system pointers rather than names, email addresses, account numbers, or signatures.

- **Original custody pointer:** who or what controlled the source when it was supplied.
- **Transfer pointer:** the controlled delivery or access event.
- **Current custody pointer:** the authorized repository and access class.
- **Reviewer pointer:** the professional role expected to review it.
- **Custody gap:** an unexplained handoff, duplicate version, missing original, or uncertain repository.

A complete custody trail does not authenticate a deed, assignment, exchange agreement, notice, closing record, or workpaper. It makes the source path inspectable for the person who must perform that analysis.

## Capture the retrieval event

Record retrieval as an access event rather than as proof of the transaction:

- retrieval timestamp and timezone;
- authorized portal, secure-delivery route, record room, or controlled repository;
- query, folder, or request pointer stored inside the authorized system;
- source revision or date exactly as displayed;
- format received, such as original electronic record, certified copy, downloaded PDF, or scan;
- controlled-copy location; and
- any access, legibility, version, or completeness limitation.

Do not put login details, personal data, legal descriptions, transaction identifiers, or source copies in the public article, URL, analytics, image, or general-purpose log.

## Use record classes supplied by the professional team

The professional team may already have categories for the controlled file. The provenance log can use those supplied categories without deciding whether any one is required. Examples of bounded classes may include:

- property acquisition or ownership source;
- relinquished-property transfer source;
- replacement-property acquisition source;
- exchange agreement or assignment source;
- written-notice source;
- intermediary communication source;
- closing or settlement source;
- basis or adjustment source supplied for professional review;
- liability or payment source supplied for professional review; or
- professional workpaper or filing-copy source.

Those labels are filing-system categories, not a universal documents-needed list. Do not add, remove, or treat a category as sufficient without direction from the qualified intermediary, CPA, and attorney.

## Respect the statutory and regulatory boundary

[Section 1031](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?req=granuleid:USC-prelim-title26-section1031&num=0&edition=prelim) supplies the federal statutory context for exchanges of real property held for productive use or investment. This provenance log does not decide whether a mineral-related interest is real property for this purpose, whether it is like kind, why it was held, whether a transfer occurred, or whether any statutory period applies to the owner's facts.

The official 2025 annual-edition text of [26 C.F.R. § 1.1031(k)-1](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CFR-2025-title26-vol13/pdf/CFR-2025-title26-vol13-sec1-1031k-1.pdf) describes deferred-exchange rules and includes written-agreement, assignment-notice, intermediary, and property-transfer context. A log may preserve pointers to records already associated with those topics. It may not decide that an agreement, assignment, notice, intermediary, transfer, restriction, or safe harbor is valid or effective.

Use the [general 1031 eligibility and process guide](/blog/1031-exchange-for-mineral-rights-does-it-qualify-and-how-does-it-work/) for general education. Send owner-specific questions to the qualified intermediary, CPA, and attorney before acting.

## Keep retention decisions with the authorized professional

The IRS explains that the retention period depends on the action, expense, or event a document records. Its [record-retention page](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/how-long-should-i-keep-records) also states that, after a nontaxable exchange, records on both the old and new property generally remain relevant until the limitations period expires for the year in which the new property is disposed of.

That general statement does not set a retention date for this owner. The log should capture the professional retention-policy pointer and any litigation-hold, title, contract, insurance, lender, privacy, or state-law instruction supplied by the authorized team. Do not calculate a destruction date or discard a record from this article.

## Validate the trail, not the tax result

An administrative reviewer can check whether the provenance row is internally usable without deciding the transaction:

- Does the row point to one existing source rather than merge several items?
- Can an authorized reviewer follow the controlled location?
- Is the displayed source date preserved without interpretation?
- Are origin, current custody, retrieval route, and format recorded?
- Is a missing page, conflicting version, illegible field, or custody break disclosed?
- Is the professional owner identified by role?
- Is the routing state limited to retrievable, gap-open, or professional-review-required?

Do not convert that quality check into an opinion about eligibility, like-kind status, holding purpose, timing, constructive receipt, intermediary status, basis, gain, reporting, or legal effect.

## Handle versions without choosing a winner

If two controlled sources appear to cover the same item, create separate provenance rows. Preserve each origin, displayed date, retrieval event, controlled location, and limitation. Link both rows to one controlled conflict record.

Do not silently replace an older version, merge conflicting fields, infer which copy controls, or mark the conflict resolved because one source is newer. The authorized professional decides whether a version is authoritative, superseded, incomplete, or irrelevant.

## Build a bounded professional handoff

The handoff can contain:

- the controlled log ID;
- pointers to retrievable sources;
- a list of gap-open entries;
- a list of professional-review-required entries;
- version or custody conflicts;
- access limitations; and
- the authorized repository and retention-policy pointers.

The handoff should not state that the file is complete, the transaction qualifies, a deadline was met, the intermediary is qualified, a tax result is correct, or a return is ready. The professional team must review the actual controlled records and complete the work within its role.

## Stop at professional review

Close the organizational log and hand off immediately when the work requires:

- deciding which records the transaction or filing requires;
- classifying a mineral-related interest or replacement property;
- determining holding purpose, like-kind status, eligibility, or legal effect;
- evaluating an exchange agreement, assignment, notice, intermediary, transfer, fund restriction, or safe harbor;
- applying identification, transfer, receipt, return, disaster-relief, extension, or filing periods;
- authenticating or interpreting a deed, assignment, closing record, notice, statement, agreement, or workpaper;
- calculating basis, consideration, liability, expense, depreciation, recapture, gain, tax, or a form field;
- completing Form 8824 or another return, schedule, election, statement, correction, or amendment;
- designing, changing, validating, or attempting to cure a transaction;
- setting a record-retention or destruction date; or
- reaching an owner-specific tax, legal, accounting, title, investment, valuation, or transaction conclusion.

Use the professional appropriate to the issue. A provenance log improves traceability. It does not replace the qualified intermediary, CPA, or attorney.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is this the same as a documents-needed checklist?

No. A checklist states or suggests what should be collected. This log records where an existing item came from, who controls it, how it was retrieved, where the controlled copy is stored, and what limitation remains. The professional team identifies actual requirements.

### Does `retrievable` mean the source is authentic or sufficient?

No. It means only that an authorized reviewer can follow the controlled pointer to the source. Authenticity, relevance, completeness, legal effect, and sufficiency require professional review.

### Can I use the source date to calculate an exchange deadline?

No. Preserve the date exactly as displayed and route the record to the professional team. Do not choose a triggering date, count days, apply an extension or relief rule, or reach a deadline conclusion in this log.

### What if the original record is missing but a scan exists?

Record the scan as its own source, identify its origin and custody as far as known, disclose the missing original as a limitation, and use `gap-open` or `professional-review-required`. Do not label the scan equivalent to an original.

### Does a complete log mean the return is ready?

No. Provenance completeness is not transaction validation or return preparation. The authorized professional must review the actual evidence, resolve conflicts, determine the tax and legal treatment, and prepare any filing.

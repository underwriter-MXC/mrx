> This article is general educational information, not tax advice, legal advice, or a conclusion that any mineral or royalty interest qualifies for a Section 1031 exchange. Consult qualified tax and legal advisors about your own facts, deadlines, and documents.

## Answer first

The mineral-rights Section 1031 exchange process begins with an eligibility question, not an assumption. IRS materials limit Section 1031 exchanges to real property, so a qualified tax and legal advisor should assess whether a specific mineral or royalty interest and transaction meet the applicable requirements.

## Review the applicable IRS materials

[IRS Publication 544](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p544) provides general information on Section 1031 exchanges. The [Instructions for Form 8824](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i8824) describe reporting and the real-property limitation. They are primary educational resources, but they do not determine eligibility for your particular interest.

Use them to frame questions for your advisors rather than to reach a qualification conclusion on your own.

## Understand the general deferred-exchange sequence

For a deferred exchange, IRS materials describe a general sequence:

1. Transfer the relinquished property.
2. Identify replacement property within 45 days.
3. Receive replacement property within 180 days, subject to the earlier return-due-date rule.
4. Complete applicable reporting, including Form 8824 where required.

The specific dates, documents, and tax consequences depend on the transaction. Missing a requirement can affect the result, so have qualified advisors confirm the timeline before proceeding.

## Do not treat a qualified intermediary as a cure-all

IRS materials explain that using a qualified intermediary does not cure a missed requirement. A qualified intermediary may be part of a deferred-exchange arrangement, but it does not replace a review of eligibility, timing, documentation, or reporting requirements with qualified advisors.

## Keep the mineral-interest question open until reviewed

Texas and federal property concepts can be fact-specific. This article does not state that mineral interests, royalty interests, or any other interest automatically qualify. Ask your advisors to evaluate the exact interest, how it is held, the relinquished property, the replacement property, and the transaction structure.

## Advisor checklist

- Is this interest real property for the Section 1031 analysis?
- What is the 45-day identification deadline?
- What is the 180-day receipt deadline, and does the earlier return-due-date rule apply?
- What role, if any, should a qualified intermediary have?
- What reporting, including Form 8824 where applicable, is required?

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I assume a mineral interest qualifies for Section 1031?

No. Section 1031 is limited to real property, and eligibility depends on your specific interest and transaction facts. Consult an appropriately qualified professional for your own circumstances.

### When do I identify replacement property?

IRS materials generally describe a 45-day identification period after the transfer of relinquished property. Ask your advisor to confirm the date for your exchange.

### When must I receive replacement property?

The general period is 180 days after the transfer, subject to the earlier return-due-date rule. See the IRS materials and obtain advice on your facts.

### Does a qualified intermediary fix a missed deadline?

No. IRS materials explain that qualified-intermediary use does not cure a missed requirement.

### Will I need to file Form 8824?

The IRS instructions describe Form 8824 reporting where applicable. Your tax advisor can determine your reporting obligations.

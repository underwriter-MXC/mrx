## Answer first

Compare mineral-rights review services by examining their process, not by relying on broad claims that one provider is better than another. Ask what records were reviewed, what assumptions are stated, what terms are written, and which questions need outside legal, tax, title, or appraisal help.

## Use public Texas information within its limits

The Texas RRC provides [Oil and Gas Production Data](https://www.rrc.texas.gov/oil-and-gas/research-and-statistics/production-data/) and [Well Information](https://www.rrc.texas.gov/oil-and-gas/research-and-statistics/well-information/) resources. These resources can help an owner ask what reported production and well-status context was considered in a review.

They do not prove ownership, establish title, or determine a dollar value. Similarly, the Texas Comptroller's [Property Classification Guide](https://comptroller.texas.gov/taxes/property-tax/docs/96-313.pdf) distinguishes surface and oil-and-gas/mineral interests for classification purposes; it is not a title opinion or an appraisal for a particular property.

## Compare the information behind the discussion

Use the following questions with any service:

- What property and interest are being discussed?
- Which documents and public records were reviewed?
- Which points are reported facts, and which are assumptions?
- What information is incomplete or unverified?
- What could change before a transaction?

The answers help an owner understand the basis of a discussion without implying that any service has a superior outcome.

## Compare written terms and boundaries

Ask for proposed terms in writing and make time to understand contingencies, adjustments, and unresolved issues. Also ask the provider to state its boundaries. A review conversation should not be represented as a legal opinion, tax conclusion, title opinion, or an appraisal.

## Do not compare only a headline number

A headline number is incomplete without the records, assumptions, dates, and written terms behind it. An owner may wish to obtain advice from qualified professionals when a decision turns on title, legal consequences, taxes, or a certified appraisal.

> **Single-attempt record-search boundary.** This blank worksheet documents one authorized manual search through one official Texas court or county route. A displayed case, order, or letters label does not establish identity, authenticity, completeness, certification, currency, appointment, qualification, authority, title, ownership, mineral connection, or legal effect. Keep names, cause numbers, queries, retrieved records, screenshots, and completed worksheets in an authorized controlled system. MRX may have an economic interest in a later mineral transaction; when that applies, the buyer relationship is disclosed in writing before an agreement is signed.

A **Texas probate court appointment record retrieval** can preserve a useful source trail when every observation stays attached to one official route, one controlled criterion, and one access event. The worksheet records what the source displayed and what prevented a bounded result. It does not decide what the record means.

That separation matters when an inherited mineral-rights file refers to an executor, administrator, order, or letters. A family statement, buyer request, royalty account, or retained document is one source. A court or county search display is another. Neither source, by itself, authorizes this worksheet to identify a person, authenticate a record, or determine who may act for an estate.

## Direct answer

Create one worksheet ID for one authorized manual search attempt. Use the Texas Judicial Branch directory to identify the applicable official court information, then follow the official court or county route that actually provides the permitted record-search interface.

Record the exact starting and final HTTPS routes, source-displayed court and county labels, access timestamp and timezone, search mode, controlled criterion or cause reference, displayed case-style, cause, docket, and document labels when available, navigation and result state, retained-search pointer, source limitation, and one outcome: `located`, `not located`, or `unverified`.

Stop there. Do not interpret an appointment, order, letters, qualification, authority, fiduciary scope, title, ownership, mineral connection, or legal effect. Do not order, purchase, submit, contact, log in, create an account, or request a certified copy under this workflow.

## Use one official route and one attempt

Texas does not have one identical probate-record interface for every county. The Texas Judicial Branch [Trial Courts](https://www.txcourts.gov/about-texas-courts/trial-courts/) page explains that the state has several trial-court levels with some overlap. It also distinguishes constitutional county courts, county courts at law, district courts, and statutory probate courts.

That structure is a reason to preserve the route, not a reason to choose a court by inference. Use the current official directory and the actual court or county site. Record exactly what those sources call the court, county, search type, and destination.

Start a new worksheet ID or version when any of these changes:

- the official starting or final route;
- the court or county label;
- the search mode;
- the controlled criterion or cause reference;
- the access timestamp or timezone;
- the displayed case, docket, or document labels;
- the access or result state;
- the retained-search pointer; or
- the source limitation.

Do not merge searches across counties, courts, platforms, names, causes, dates, or document types. A later reviewer should be able to see which exact source state produced each observation.

## Start with the official Texas court directory

The Texas Judicial Branch [Judicial Directory](https://www.txcourts.gov/judicial-directory/) says its annual published information is current as of April 2026 unless otherwise noted and points to the live directory database for more current court information. The linked Texas Office of Court Administration [public directory search](https://card.txcourts.gov/DirectorySearch.aspx) identifies itself as the Court Activity Reporting and Directory System and exposes source-displayed court-search controls.

Use those sources only to preserve the official court-routing trail. The directory is not the probate case record. After identifying the court information, retain the official route that the relevant court or county actually provides for authorized public record access.

Do not substitute an advertisement, people-search site, document reseller, search snippet, social profile, buyer portal, or unofficial index for the official route. If the final route cannot be confirmed, record the attempt as `unverified` and stop.

## Preserve record labels without interpreting them

The official [Texas Estates Code PDF](https://tcss.legis.texas.gov/resources/SDocs/ESTATESCODE.pdf) separates probate jurisdiction in Chapter 32 from the granting and issuance of letters in Chapter 306. Chapter 306 also distinguishes an order granting letters, issuance of original letters, the form and content of letters, and the statute's stated effect of letters.

Those distinctions support exact label recording. If the official source displays `order`, `letters testamentary`, `letters of administration`, `case style`, `cause`, `docket`, or another document label, preserve the exact source wording. Do not collapse different document types into the generic word `appointment`.

This worksheet does not apply Chapter 32 to select the legally correct court. It does not apply Chapter 306 to decide whether an order or letters is effective, current, sufficient, certified, or related to a particular person or property. Those are separate legal and evidentiary questions.

## Preserve three controlled outcomes

Use only these outcomes:

- **Located:** the recorded official route displayed the controlled result or retained result reference during the documented attempt.
- **Not located:** the recorded route did not display the controlled result during that attempt.
- **Unverified:** access, route identity, court or county labels, search mode, result state, missing fields, private-data controls, or retained evidence prevented either bounded observation.

Do not convert `located` into `authentic`, `certified`, `current`, `same person`, `appointed`, `qualified`, `authorized`, `may lease`, `may sell`, `owns the minerals`, or `valid transaction party`.

Do not convert `not located` into `no probate`, `no order`, `no letters`, `no representative`, `no estate`, `no authority`, or `record does not exist`. One route, search mode, spelling, date range, index, or access state may be incomplete.

## Stop when access changes the task

An authorized public search can reach a boundary that requires a login, account, clerk contact, order form, payment, certified-copy request, identity verification, subscription, or another submission. Record the route, access time, displayed requirement, and limitation. Use `unverified` and stop.

This workflow does not authorize:

- creating an account or signing in;
- paying a search, access, document, or certification fee;
- submitting a request or order;
- contacting a clerk, court, vendor, party, or attorney;
- downloading restricted or bulk records;
- bypassing a CAPTCHA, rate limit, access control, or terms;
- scraping or automating a search; or
- representing that a non-certified display is a certified record.

A later authorized process can decide whether contact, purchase, certification, or legal review is appropriate. Keep that decision outside the retrieval worksheet.

## Keep private case data controlled

The reusable public template needs no completed name, cause number, docket number, filing date, document number, address, attorney, court staff contact, estate detail, family relationship, will term, property description, mineral interest, lease, royalty amount, offer, signature, screenshot, or result.

Use one controlled input pointer for the reason the search was performed. Use a separate controlled retained-search pointer for the evidence preserved from the official source. Do not merge the two into an identity profile or publish either pointer's private contents.

If an authorized reviewer needs to compare names, dates, or record details, create a separate purpose, access rule, evidence standard, and human review. This worksheet is not an identity-matching or record-authentication process.

## Exclude title, mineral, and transaction conclusions

A probate search display does not connect an estate to a tract, survey, abstract, section, block, acreage, depth, formation, lease, unit, well, royalty account, payor, or offer. Do not add those fields to this worksheet.

Use the broader [probate process for mineral interests](/blog/understanding-the-probate-process-for-mineral-interests/) article for educational context about estate, title, inventory, and mineral administration. Use the [retained evidence source-scope worksheet](/blog/mineral-rights-retained-evidence-source-scope-worksheet/) to document the source scope of an artifact that has already been retained. This article owns the earlier, narrower job of documenting one official probate record-search attempt.

Do not use the outcome to decide who owns mineral rights, who may sign, whether title is clear, whether a lease or sale is valid, whether an offer is fair, or whether a transaction should proceed.

## Stop at authorized-human review

Close the worksheet with the controlled outcome, limitation, unresolved question, and next authorized role. Do not answer the later question inside this retrieval record.

Questions about the correct court, record certification, document meaning, appointment, qualification, present authority, fiduciary scope, legal effect, title, ownership, heirship, mineral connection, lease or sale power, or transaction conduct require the court or clerk, qualified Texas probate or oil-and-gas counsel, or another appropriately qualified professional.

This worksheet documents one manual official-route search attempt. It does not provide legal, probate, tax, title, appraisal, valuation, investment, financial, identity-verification, record-authentication, offer, closing, or transaction advice.

## Frequently asked questions

### Does a directory listing prove where a probate record is filed?

No. The directory helps preserve official court information and routing. It does not establish venue, jurisdiction for a particular matter, filing location, case existence, record completeness, or legal effect.

### Can I record a screenshot instead of the route and search state?

A controlled screenshot may be part of retained evidence when authorized, but it does not replace the route, access time, search mode, exact displayed labels, limitation, and outcome. Keep the screenshot out of the public template.

### Does this worksheet verify an executor or administrator?

No. It documents a source retrieval attempt only. Identity, appointment, qualification, current authority, fiduciary scope, and power to act require separate evidence and qualified review.

### What if the official site is unavailable?

Record the attempted route, access time, unavailable or error state, and limitation. Use `unverified`. Do not switch silently to an unofficial source or treat the outage as evidence about the case or record.

### Can the same worksheet be reused after the source changes?

Use a new version or worksheet ID. A changed route, court label, search mode, criterion, date range, displayed fields, access state, or result state is a new source event.

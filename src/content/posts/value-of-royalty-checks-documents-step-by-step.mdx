## Value Of Royalty Checks: Documents Step By Step

The value of a royalty check is the last number on a stack of documents that runs in one direction: from the owner’s chain of title and the lease that defines the royalty terms, through the payor’s payment-setup records, into the monthly statement and the IRS information return, and finally into the federal tax documents that report the income and claim the deduction. Each layer supports a tax-planning action; the walk-through below names each document and ties it to the decision it backs.

## 1. Title and estate records - the ownership chain

The ownership chain is what proves the owner has a royalty interest. It is built from recorded deeds/conveyances, probate or court documents, and assignments, and it is the foundation for the lease, the division order, the 1099, and the depletion basis. MRX does not prepare title opinions; for owner-specific title questions, a qualified land professional or attorney is the right resource.

## 2. The lease and lease assignments - the granted royalty terms

The lease (and any lease assignment) is the document that defines the granted leasehold and the royalty terms the operator or lessee will pay. Royalty percentage, payment timing, deduction categories, and pooling or unit provisions are written into the lease, and the lease is the source of truth when a statement label disagrees with the math. Keep the original lease and every amendment; material lease-interpretation questions belong with qualified oil-and-gas counsel.

## 3. The division order - the payor’s payment-direction document

The division order (DOI) is the payee-signed agreement that directs the distribution of proceeds and authorizes the payor to pay in accordance with the order. Section 91.401(3) of the [Texas Natural Resources Code](https://tcss.legis.texas.gov/resources/NR/htm/NR.91.htm) defines the document; section 91.402(c)(2) states that a qualifying division order does not amend any lease, operating agreement, or other purchase contract for oil or gas; section 91.402(h) provides that a division-order provision in contradiction with the lease is invalid. The DOI records the payor’s decimal and payment setup: the fractional share, the property or well identifier, the effective date, and the payee name, address, and TIN. It is a payment-direction and decimal-setup document, not a title instrument, and should be reconciled against the title and lease records rather than treated as proof of title.

## 4. The owner statement and the IRS Form 1099-MISC - the actual payment

The monthly owner statement shows the property, production or sales month, product (oil, gas, NGLs, condensate), volume, price, owner decimal, deductions, taxes, adjustments, and net. The companion document is the [IRS Form 1099-MISC Box 2 (Royalties)](https://www.irs.gov/forms-pubs/about-form-1099-misc), the payor’s information return for the year. Per [IRS Publication 525](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p525), for a cash-method taxpayer, royalty income generally is reported when actually or constructively received, and the taxpayer’s accounting method and the specific facts can affect timing; Form 1099-MISC is an information return used to reconcile, and it does not alone govern return timing. This article is not tax advice. A useful reconciliation sequence is to compare the operator-reported sales volume and the realized unit price against the operator-reported gross proceeds, apply the owner decimal to estimate the owner’s gross share, and then trace the displayed deductions, taxes, and adjustments to the net figure; products, unit conversions, statement layouts, and lease terms differ, so this is a reconciliation framework, not a universal formula. The companion article explains [how to read a royalty statement line by line](/blog/how-to-decode-your-royalty-check-statement/).

## 5. Texas Railroad Commission public production data - the independent cross-check

The [Texas Railroad Commission Oil & Gas Division](https://www.rrc.texas.gov/oil-and-gas/) publishes monthly production and proration data organized by county, operator, and lease. The owner can use this data to cross-check the volumes reported on the statement and in the 1099-MISC for the same property and period. Three caveats matter: public production data does not establish private mineral ownership; RRC figures can differ from the operator’s owner statement due to measurement timing, marketing or severance treatment, and ownership-attribution conventions; and the Commission does not maintain an individual payee ledger. Treat RRC data as a volume cross-check, not as a regulator of the owner statement. The [downloadable RRC monthly production datasets](https://www.rrc.texas.gov/resource-center/research/data-sets-available-for-download/) and the [RRC research and statistics portal](https://www.rrc.texas.gov/oil-and-gas/research-and-statistics/) are the typical starting points.

## 6. The federal tax documents - Schedule E, Schedule C, and Form 1040

Federal tax placement depends on whether the interest is non-operating or operating. Per the [IRS Schedule E instructions](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i1040se), a non-operating mineral royalty interest for an individual is generally reported on Schedule E (Form 1040), Part I; an operating mineral interest can be reported on Schedule C; Form 1040 is the umbrella return, and IRS [Publication 17](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p17) is the umbrella citation. The depletion deduction is allowed under [26 USC § 611](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/611); percentage depletion is governed by [26 USC § 613](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/613); the deduction for oil and gas wells is subject to the 65% of taxable income cap under [26 USC § 613A](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/613A). [IRS Publication 535](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p535) describes the records the owner should retain. Owner-specific federal tax decisions (including the depletion election and the cost-vs-percentage choice) should go to a qualified tax adviser.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Which documents determine the value of a royalty check?

Seven record categories: title and estate records, the lease and lease assignments, the payor’s division order, the owner statement, the [IRS Form 1099-MISC Box 2](https://www.irs.gov/forms-pubs/about-form-1099-misc), the [RRC](https://www.rrc.texas.gov/oil-and-gas/) public production data, and the federal tax forms (Schedule E / C / 1040).

### What is the difference between the division order and the lease?

The lease grants the leasehold and defines the royalty terms. The division order, defined in [Texas Natural Resources Code § 91.401(3)](https://tcss.legis.texas.gov/resources/NR/htm/NR.91.htm), directs distribution of proceeds and authorizes payment; under § 91.402(c)(2) it does not amend the lease or operating agreement. The DOI is a payment-direction and decimal-setup document, not a title instrument.

### How does an owner reconcile a royalty check with the 1099-MISC?

Compare the check-stub net to [Form 1099-MISC Box 2](https://www.irs.gov/forms-pubs/about-form-1099-misc) for the same property and period. If they diverge, ask the payor for the calculation and the production month. Per [Publication 525](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p525), for a cash-method taxpayer, royalty income generally is reported when actually or constructively received; the accounting method and facts can affect timing, and Form 1099-MISC is an information return to reconcile, not a sole determinant of return timing.

### Why use the Texas Railroad Commission public production data?

The [RRC Oil & Gas Division](https://www.rrc.texas.gov/oil-and-gas/) publishes monthly production and proration data that an owner can use as a volume cross-check. Public production data does not establish private mineral ownership; treat RRC figures as a cross-check subject to timing, measurement, and ownership-attribution differences from the operator’s owner statement.

### Which federal tax form reports mineral royalty income?

Per the [Schedule E instructions](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i1040se), a non-operating mineral royalty interest for an individual is generally reported on Schedule E (Form 1040), Part I; an operating mineral interest can be reported on Schedule C. Depletion is allowed under [26 USC § 611](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/611) and computed under [26 USC § 613](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/613), subject to the 65% cap under [26 USC § 613A](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/613A) for oil and gas wells.

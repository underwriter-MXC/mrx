## Answer first

After a Texas mineral-rights sale closes, the work shifts from negotiation to documentation and administration. The parties should preserve the final agreement, conveyance, payment evidence, and settlement records; confirm recording; provide required information to affected payors; distinguish sold rights from any retained rights; and address tax and legal questions using the actual closing package.

The deed and agreement control the transaction. A marketing summary, email, royalty check, or public database should not be used to redefine what the final instrument conveyed.

MRX provides directional underwriter reviews and may become a buyer in some transactions. If MRX is the buyer, the same post-closing evidence and disclosure standards apply.

## 1. The final documents define what changed

Start with the executed purchase agreement, mineral or royalty deed, exhibits, settlement statement, and any written closing instructions. Confirm the county, legal description, interest type, fraction, depths, effective date, and any retained interests.

A full sale, partial sale, term interest, depth-limited conveyance, and royalty-only conveyance can produce different post-closing results. If the deed language does not match the seller's understanding, that is a legal-review issue, not something to solve by relying on an article or oral assurance.

## 2. Recording creates a public record trail

Texas Property Code chapter 12 addresses when an instrument concerning real property may be recorded. After closing, retain the county, recording date, document or instrument number, and a copy of the recorded image when available.

Recording is important, but it is not a guarantee that every title, authority, or contract issue has been resolved. A recorded instrument can still require interpretation in the context of prior documents and the parties' rights.

## 3. Signing, delivery, payment, and recording are distinct events

Do not assume that all four occurred at the same moment. The agreement may define conditions for delivery, funding, acceptance, or release of documents. Keep evidence of:

- the final signed version of each document;
- how signatures and acknowledgments were completed;
- when and how funds were sent and received;
- when the deed was delivered or released;
- which party submitted it for recording; and
- any unresolved holdback, adjustment, or curative item.

If payment or recording evidence conflicts with the closing instructions, contact the appropriate transaction professional promptly.

## 4. Royalty-payor records may change on a different schedule

Operators and purchasers may have their own transfer and division-order procedures. They may request the recorded conveyance, transfer order, tax form, contact information, or other title evidence before changing owner records.

That administrative timing does not itself determine the legal effect of the deed. It can also create a temporary mismatch between the closing date, reported ownership, suspense status, and the name on a royalty statement.

If the seller retained part of the interest, compare new division orders and payment decimals with the deed and the retained-rights description. Do not sign a replacement division order merely because it arrived after closing; verify that it reflects the intended interest.

## 5. Public production data remains contextual

The Railroad Commission explains that production reports are a snapshot and can change when operators submit revised, corrected, or delinquent reports. A change in reported production after closing does not rewrite the contract, guarantee future royalties, or determine who owned a payment period.

Use public production data to ask questions, then use the lease, division order, payor records, conveyance, and qualified advice to address the owner-specific answer.

## 6. Retained rights need their own file

For a partial sale, create a short retained-rights summary tied to the final deed. Include the tract, fraction, interest type, depths or formations, and any retained executive, leasing, royalty, or other rights described in the instrument.

This summary is an organizational tool, not a substitute for the deed. It can help an owner detect later inconsistencies in tax notices, division orders, royalty statements, or future offers.

## 7. The tax file begins with basis and the final transaction package

[IRS Publication 544](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p544) explains that gain or loss generally depends on the amount realized and adjusted basis, and that the classification and reporting of a disposition depend on the asset and the taxpayer's circumstances.

That general framework does not establish the treatment of a particular mineral-rights sale. The seller may need records showing acquisition by purchase, gift, inheritance, divorce, or another transfer; prior depletion or other basis adjustments; the interest sold; selling costs; installment terms; and any retained economic interest.

Give the qualified tax professional the actual agreement, deed, settlement statement, payment evidence, prior basis records, and relevant tax forms. Do not rely on the buyer's description of tax treatment as owner-specific tax guidance.

## 8. Close every written follow-up obligation

Review the agreement for curative duties, affidavits, tax forms, confidentiality, indemnity, cooperation, notice, or other provisions that survive closing. Record who owns each open item and the evidence that will close it.

Also verify that contact and banking changes came through a trusted channel. Post-closing documents and payment changes can be sensitive, so confirm instructions independently before sharing private information.

## Post-closing checklist

1. Save the final signed agreement, deed, exhibits, and settlement statement.
2. Preserve verified payment evidence and closing instructions.
3. Confirm the county recording details and retain the recorded image when available.
4. List sold and retained interests from the final deed.
5. Track payor, division-order, tax-form, and suspense updates.
6. Reconcile later statements with the retained interest, if any.
7. Provide the closing package and basis records to the qualified tax professional.
8. Close any surviving contractual or curative obligations.

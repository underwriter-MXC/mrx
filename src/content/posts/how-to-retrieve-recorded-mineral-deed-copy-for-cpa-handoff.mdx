> **Retrieval boundary.** This page explains how to locate a current official county-clerk route, retrieve a recorded real-property instrument copy, preserve the source and receipt, document limitations, and hand the evidence securely to a CPA. It does not determine ownership, title, deed validity, record priority, legal effect, reservations, conveyed acreage or interest, completeness, basis, tax treatment, value, offer quality, or transaction choice. Keep documents and transaction details inside an authorized controlled system. MRX may have an economic interest in a later mineral transaction; when that applies, the buyer relationship is disclosed in writing before an agreement is signed.

A **recorded mineral deed copy** can support a CPA's source review only when the retrieval trail and the document's limits remain clear. Use the current official county-clerk route, preserve the copy exactly as received, record how it was located, disclose what the search could not establish, and stop before interpreting the instrument.

“Recorded mineral deed” is a retrieval label in this article, not a conclusion about the instrument. A copy returned by a county index may be relevant to the engagement, but retrieval alone does not establish that the document is valid, complete, controlling, correctly indexed, or sufficient for title or tax work.

## Direct answer

Use this bounded sequence:

1. Ask the CPA what document or transaction the engagement is trying to source and whether a plain or certified copy is needed.
2. Identify the likely recording county from a controlled document already in the file. Do not guess from the owner's current address.
3. Open the current official county-clerk or county-designated real-property-records page.
4. Search with details already known: an instrument or document number first, then supported name, date, volume/page, or property-description fields when available.
5. Save a neutral retrieval log showing the source URL, access time and timezone, exact inputs, result, instrument identifier, image availability, and limitation.
6. Obtain the requested copy through the clerk's current route. Preserve the received file and receipt unaltered.
7. Hand the controlled source, receipt, and retrieval log to the CPA through the professional's approved secure method.

The sequence ends at retrieval. It does not authenticate the deed, read a mineral clause, identify a reservation, establish current ownership, choose a tax treatment, or validate a sale.

## Start with the recording county, not a broad web search

[Texas Property Code §11.001](https://statutes.capitol.texas.gov/Docs/PR/pdf/PR.11.pdf) states the recording-location rule for an eligible instrument concerning real property: it must be recorded in the county where a part of the property is located. That rule helps identify the office to check when the controlled file already identifies the property's county.

Do not infer the county from a mailing address, operator name, basin, nearby town, appraisal-district result, or a generic search-engine result. A mineral interest can involve land outside the owner's residence, and one transaction can involve more than one county. If the controlled records do not establish the relevant county, stop and ask the attorney, title professional, closing professional, or CPA handling the matter.

Use the county's current official page to reach the designated index. Avoid lookalike portals, scraped databases, cached forms, and advertisements. Capture the official page URL and the destination search URL because counties may use a designated vendor for the index.

## Prepare only known search inputs

Build a short search-input note from facts already in the controlled file:

- county;
- instrument or document number;
- volume and page;
- grantor or grantee name exactly as already recorded;
- approximate recording date or bounded date range;
- instrument type, if already identified by a responsible source; and
- legal description or subdivision fields only when already present in a controlled document.

Do not invent a middle initial, normalize a name, expand an abbreviation, choose a deed type, reconstruct a legal description, or decide that a person must be the grantor or grantee. If the official index supports variants, record each exact variant separately and state why it came from the existing file.

An instrument number is usually the least ambiguous starting point when it is already known. If it is not available, use the index fields the county actually provides. The [current Harris County real-property portal](https://www.cclerk.hctx.net/applications/websearch/RP.aspx/Registration/Home.aspx), for example, exposes file number, date, grantor, grantee, instrument type, volume/page, and several property-description fields. It also warns that its online database is not the official repository and may not reflect the complete or unaltered contents maintained in the official records.

That warning is the right evidence posture: an online index is a retrieval tool, not a title opinion or completeness certificate.

## Create a neutral retrieval log

For every search attempt, record these paired facts and boundaries:

- **Source:** Capture the official clerk page and designated index URL. Do not assume that a separate third-party result is official.
- **Access:** Capture the date, time, and timezone. Do not assume that the index was complete at another time.
- **Query:** Capture the exact fields and values entered. Do not assume that a spelling variant belongs to the same person.
- **Result:** Capture the exact result count or displayed limitation. Do not treat an empty result as proof that no record exists.
- **Identifier:** Capture the instrument number or volume/page shown. Do not conclude that it is the operative deed.
- **Image:** Record whether the image is available, restricted, delayed, or unavailable. Do not conclude that a visible image is complete or legally sufficient.
- **Copy:** Record whether the copy is plain or certified and its delivery route. Do not claim that certification answers ownership or legal-effect questions.
- **Receipt:** Capture the amount charged, date, and order reference. Do not claim that the fee or receipt validates the document's meaning.

Preserve the log as an administrative record. Do not paste confidential names, legal descriptions, account credentials, or purchased images into a public worksheet.

## Treat negative and partial results as limitations

A failed search can have many procedural causes: the wrong county, a name-index variation, a recording date outside the online range, a document not yet reflected online, a restricted image, an incorrect instrument number, or a system problem. The [Harris County portal](https://www.cclerk.hctx.net/applications/websearch/RP.aspx/Registration/Home.aspx) currently states that images are available from November 1, 1960 and that a newly processed recording may take one to two business days to appear in the search inquiry.

Those are county-specific current limitations, not universal timelines. Record what the source says on the access date. Never turn “not found,” “image unavailable,” or “not yet online” into “no deed,” “no minerals,” “no ownership,” or “no transaction.”

When a supported search remains unresolved:

1. save the exact query and result;
2. note the official source's stated coverage or availability limit;
3. contact the clerk for navigation or copy-request instructions; and
4. route identity, title, or legal questions to the responsible professional.

Do not ask clerk staff to provide a title opinion. Current Tarrant County guidance states that its staff do not conduct title or lien searches or determine restrictions, although they can help users navigate the online search tool.

## Let the CPA choose plain or certified

[Texas Local Government Code §191.004](https://statutes.capitol.texas.gov/Docs/LG/pdf/LG.191.pdf) provides for an attested copy of a recorded instrument on demand with the fee provided by law. Section 191.0041 specifies elements of a certified copy made from an original document on file with the county clerk.

That statutory context does not tell this article which format a particular CPA engagement needs. Ask the receiving CPA whether a free watermarked image, paid plain copy, electronic certified copy, paper certified copy, or raised-seal copy is required. If the CPA cannot answer a legal-sufficiency question, route it to the attorney or title professional.

County processes differ. As a current example, [Tarrant County's copy page](https://www.tarrantcountytx.gov/en/county-clerk/real-estate-records/copies-of-official-public-records--real-property-.html) lists four routes:

- in person;
- free online unofficial watermarked copies of most official public records;
- online certified copies; and
- a mail request that identifies the document number.

As of the August 26, 2026 source review, that page states an in-person or mailed copy fee of $1 per page plus $5 for certification. It says the online certified-copy system calculates its charge. These figures are a dated Tarrant County example, not a statewide quote or a promise. Verify the current official page before ordering.

The [Harris County public-records page](https://www.cclerk.hctx.net/applications/websearch/PublicRecords.aspx) publishes different copy formats, per-page or electronic charges, certification charges, ordering routes, and a search fee when a file number or film code is not supplied. Its [real-property portal](https://www.cclerk.hctx.net/applications/websearch/RP.aspx/Registration/Home.aspx) also notes that an online certified copy does not have a raised seal that some recipients may require.

Do not buy every available format. Let the receiving professional state the needed form, then use the current official route. A county copy fee paid by the reader is not an MRX publication charge, and MRX does not receive it.

## Preserve the source, receipt, and limitation note

When the copy arrives:

1. save the original download or scan in the authorized matter location without editing it;
2. preserve the county's filename when practical, or record the original filename before applying a controlled internal name;
3. calculate a file hash if the professional's evidence process uses one;
4. save the receipt or order confirmation separately;
5. record the copy type, page count, delivery route, received time, and any visible watermark or certification format;
6. note unreadable, missing, restricted, or truncated pages without repairing or interpreting them; and
7. create a separate working copy only if the receiving professional requests one.

Do not crop a recording stamp, remove a watermark, rewrite a legal description, combine pages from different downloads, add an interpretation to the source file, or replace the original with an annotated copy. If confidential data must be redacted for a working purpose, preserve the unaltered source and let the responsible professional define the working-copy procedure.

## Build the CPA handoff around provenance

The handoff should tell the CPA where the source came from and what remains unresolved:

- responsible county and official clerk page;
- designated search portal;
- access date, time, and timezone;
- exact query inputs;
- displayed instrument identifier;
- copy type and delivery route;
- original filename, page count, and controlled location;
- receipt or order reference;
- known coverage, image, legibility, or search limitations; and
- the date and method of the professional handoff.

Use the CPA's approved secure channel. Do not email sensitive deed images to an unverified address, place them in a public link, or upload them to an unrelated document-analysis service. This article does not select an encryption product, storage platform, or retention period.

The retrieval log may say “county index returned this identifier” or “certified electronic copy received.” It may not say “this deed proves ownership,” “the reservation is valid,” “the chain is complete,” “this establishes basis,” or “the sale qualifies for a tax result.”

## Stop at professional review

Hand off immediately when the work requires:

- deciding whether the correct county, person, entity, instrument type, or property was found;
- reading a grant, reservation, exception, warranty, acknowledgment, exhibit, or legal description;
- determining ownership, current title, chain of title, record priority, deed validity, legal effect, or instrument sufficiency;
- deciding whether another deed, probate record, court order, lease, assignment, release, affidavit, or title source is required;
- resolving records that are missing, conflicting, redacted, restricted, illegible, or differently indexed;
- reconstructing acquisition date, interest, acreage, allocation, adjusted basis, proceeds, gain, loss, character, or holding period;
- calculating tax, completing a return or form, choosing a filing position, or setting a retention date;
- valuing a mineral interest, reviewing an offer or contract, comparing buyers, or recommending a transaction; or
- reaching an owner-specific tax, accounting, title, legal, valuation, investment, offer, or transaction conclusion.

The CPA owns tax-record relevance, reconciliation, calculation, reporting, filing, and retention decisions. An attorney or qualified title professional owns ownership, deed, title, validity, priority, legal-effect, and sufficiency questions. A clean retrieval trail helps them locate the controlled source; it does not replace their work.

## Frequently asked questions

### Does a recorded mineral deed copy prove that I own the mineral rights?

No. A retrieved copy is evidence from a recording office. This workflow does not determine title, deed validity, the interest conveyed or reserved, record priority, or current ownership. Route those questions to an attorney or qualified title professional.

### Should I order a plain copy or a certified copy for my CPA?

Ask the CPA receiving the file which format the engagement requires. County certification options and delivery methods differ, and this article does not select a copy type for an owner.

### What if the county search returns no result?

Record the exact query, date, source, coverage limits, and error or empty result. Try only supported variants grounded in known records, then contact the clerk or responsible professional. A failed search is not proof that no instrument or interest exists.

### Can I use the deed copy to calculate tax basis?

Not by itself, and this article does not calculate basis. Preserve the source and give it to the CPA, who must decide whether it is relevant and reconcile it with acquisition, allocation, transaction, and other controlled records.

### Do all Texas counties provide free online deed copies?

No universal rule is stated here. Current county examples show different search coverage, copy formats, fees, certification methods, and in-person or mail options. Verify the current official clerk page for the relevant county before ordering.

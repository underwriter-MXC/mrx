## Answer first

No short checklist can determine whether a mineral-rights offer is fair. A more careful approach is to ask what property and interest are covered, what records and assumptions inform the discussion, what written terms apply, and which questions need qualified legal, tax, title, or appraisal help.

## Confirm what interest is being discussed

Start with the property description and the mineral interest covered by the proposed terms. Texas classification materials distinguish surface interests from oil-and-gas/mineral interests, as described in the [Texas Comptroller's Property Classification Guide](https://comptroller.texas.gov/taxes/property-tax/docs/96-313.pdf). That classification context is not a title opinion and does not establish ownership of a particular interest.

## Use public RRC data as context, not proof

The Texas RRC provides [Oil and Gas Production Data](https://www.rrc.texas.gov/oil-and-gas/research-and-statistics/production-data/) and [Well Information](https://www.rrc.texas.gov/oil-and-gas/research-and-statistics/well-information/) resources. They can help you ask what reported production or well-status context may be relevant to a discussion.

RRC records do not prove ownership or title, and they do not establish a required price. Do not rely on a single public-data point to resolve questions about an offer.

## Questions to ask before deciding

- What property and mineral interest does the offer cover?
- Which records and reporting periods informed the discussion?
- Which points are confirmed, and which are assumptions?
- What contingencies, adjustments, or deadlines appear in the written terms?
- What facts could change before a transaction?
- Which ownership, tax, title, or valuation questions call for an appropriately qualified professional for my own circumstances?

## Read the written terms carefully

The written terms may address matters that a high-level discussion does not. Ask for time to read them and to understand any provisions that affect your decision. A provider should not represent an offer review as a legal opinion, tax conclusion, title opinion, or an appraisal.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can reported production data establish the value of my offer?

No. RRC production data provides reported operational context. It does not establish title, ownership, or a required transaction value.

### Does the Comptroller guide confirm that I own mineral rights?

No. The guide provides classification context only; it is not evidence of title or ownership for a particular property.

### Can a review determine whether an offer is fair?

No. A review can help organize questions about records, assumptions, and terms, but it does not guarantee an outcome or determine what an owner should accept.

### When should I get outside advice?

Get the appropriate qualified advice when your decision depends on legal, tax, title, or certified-appraisal questions.

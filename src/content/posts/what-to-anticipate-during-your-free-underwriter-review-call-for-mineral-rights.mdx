## What to Anticipate During Your Free Underwriter Review Call for Mineral Rights

> A free underwriter review call for mineral rights is a structured 30-45 minute conversation in which an independent underwriter confirms your interest, gathers the inputs needed to value it, and walks you through the methodology before any offer is discussed. The call is informational, not a sales pitch, and you do not give up any rights by participating.

[Schedule Your Review](/book/)

### Key Takeaways

- **The call is informational, not transactional**: The underwriter's job on the call is to gather the inputs needed for a defensible valuation, not to pitch a sale or pressure a decision.
- **It runs 30-45 minutes**: The first 10 minutes cover the interest and the documents; the next 20 minutes walk through the methodology inputs; the final 5-10 minutes cover next steps if you want them.
- **You will be asked for the basics**: County, section, lease terms, royalty percentage, recent royalty checks, and any existing offers you have received.
- **The underwriter will walk the methodology**: You will see the [MRX published methodology](/methodology/) applied to your interest in real time, including the DCF inputs, the comparable-transactions selection, and the royalty income multiple check.
- **You will leave with a written value range**: Within 1-3 business days after the call, you receive a written valuation range and the inputs that produced it. The range is yours whether or not you ever transact.

## Who Is on the Call

The call is with an independent underwriter who is not the eventual buyer of your interest. The underwriter's role is to produce a market analysis on the [MRX methodology](/methodology/) so that you have an objective benchmark before any negotiation. The underwriter is not incentivized by the closing of a sale; the deliverable is the output of the conversation.

If you ever choose to transact, the underwriter's analysis is shared with the buyer at your direction. Until then, the analysis is yours and confidential.

## What to Prepare Before the Call

The cleaner your inputs, the more precise the valuation range. The minimum you should have ready:

- **County, section, and survey (or abstract) for the property.** Publicly available from the [Texas Railroad Commission](https://www.rrc.texas.gov/) and the county appraisal district.
- **Lease or lease assignment.** Establishes your royalty percentage and any deductions.
- **Division order.** Confirms the operator, the well, and the share of production you receive.
- **Most recent royalty check stub.** Shows the most recent months of income and any deductions.
- **Any existing offers you have received.** Lets the underwriter compare the offers against the methodology.

If you do not have a document, the call can still proceed. The underwriter will reconstruct the inputs from public production data, lease records from the [RRC data sets](https://www.rrc.texas.gov/resource-center/research/data-sets-available-for-download/), and the [EIA Texas profile](https://www.eia.gov/state/?id=TX) for basin context.

## What the Underwriter Will Ask

The conversation follows a predictable structure. Knowing the structure in advance lets you prepare the documents instead of scrambling for them on the call.

1. **Confirm the interest.** Royalty, working interest, or net mineral acres. Confirm the county, section, and operator.
2. **Confirm the documents.** Lease, division order, royalty checks. Note any gaps and how the underwriter will fill them from public data.
3. **Production history.** Pull from the [RRC public records](https://www.rrc.texas.gov/) and confirm against any royalty checks you have.
4. **Existing offers.** Any offers you have received are noted; the underwriter will compare them against the methodology at the end of the call.
5. **Decision timeline.** Are you responding to an unsolicited offer? Planning an estate? Considering a 1031 exchange? The decision context shapes which inputs matter most.
6. **Tax and estate context.** If the interest was inherited, the basis discussion under [26 USC § 1014](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/1014) and [IRS Publication 551](https://www.irs.gov/forms-pubs/about-publication-551) is referenced for your CPA; the underwriter does not provide tax guidance.

## What the Underwriter Will Walk You Through

The bulk of the call is a transparent walk-through of the valuation methodology applied to your interest. The underwriter will show:

- **The DCF inputs.** Production history, fitted decline curve, remaining reserve forecast, price deck, discount rate, and the resulting present value range.
- **The comparable-transactions selection.** Recent sales in your county and formation, with the price per net mineral acre and the implied royalty multiple.
- **The royalty income multiple check.** The trailing monthly royalty income is divided into the value range to produce the implied multiple. The [MRX published methodology](/methodology/) uses the multiple as a cross-check that the DCF and comparable-transactions methods are producing a market-consistent answer.
- **The convergence.** Where the three methods overlap, that range is the defensible value range. Where they diverge, the underwriter explains why and which method is most applicable for your interest.

You see the math. The deliverable is auditable.

## Common Questions During the Call

Owners ask the same handful of questions. The underwriter is prepared to answer each one.

- **Is this a sales call?** No. The deliverable is a written valuation range. You do not sign a listing agreement, grant an option, or give up any rights.
- **Will you share my information with buyers?** Only at your direction. The analysis is confidential until you decide otherwise.
- **How long until I have the written range?** Most reviews are delivered within 1-3 business days after the call. Reviews of interests with limited public comparable transactions may take longer.
- **What if I have multiple offers?** Bring them. The underwriter will compare each offer against the methodology and surface the assumptions each buyer is using.
- **What if the interest was inherited?** Bring the date-of-death documentation. The basis under [26 USC § 1014](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/1014) is a tax question for your CPA; the valuation is the same regardless of basis.
- **What if I have a 1031 exchange in mind?** Mention it on the call. The timing of the exchange under [26 USC § 1031](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/1031) interacts with the valuation timeline; the underwriter will structure the review accordingly.

## What You Receive After the Call

Within 1-3 business days, you receive a written deliverable that includes:

- A defensible value range expressed as dollars per net mineral acre and as a total offer range.
- A DCF summary showing the production, price deck, and discount rate used.
- A comparable-transactions summary showing the recent sales in your county and formation.
- A royalty income multiple check showing the implied multiple of your trailing monthly royalty income.
- A list of every input document you provided or that was pulled from public data.

The deliverable is yours. You can use it to negotiate, to plan an estate, to anchor a 1031 exchange, or to set a reserve price for a future listing.

## What the Review Is Not

The underwriter review is precise about what it does and does not promise.

- **It is not a third-party prepared professional appraisal report.** For court, tax, or estate contexts where a third-party prepared professional appraisal report is required, the review is not a substitute. Engage a state-licensed appraiser.
- **It is not legal guidance.** For title, probate, contract, or estate questions, the next step is a qualified attorney.
- **It is not tax guidance.** For basis, capital gains, or exchange treatment, the next step is a qualified CPA.
- **It is not a guarantee of any specific sale price or closing.** The deliverable is a market-based range; the actual transaction depends on counterparty negotiation, due diligence, and title.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How long is a free underwriter review call?

The call runs 30-45 minutes. The first 10 minutes cover the interest and the documents; the next 20 minutes walk through the methodology inputs; the final 5-10 minutes cover next steps if you want them.

### Do I need to prepare documents for the call?

The cleaner the inputs, the more precise the valuation. The minimum useful documents are your lease, division order, and most recent royalty check stub. If you do not have a document, the underwriter will reconstruct the inputs from public production data from the [Texas Railroad Commission](https://www.rrc.texas.gov/) and the [EIA Texas state profile](https://www.eia.gov/state/?id=TX).

### Is the call really free?

Yes. The underwriter review call is a no-cost, no-obligation conversation. You do not sign a listing agreement, grant an option, or give up any rights to participate. The deliverable is yours whether or not you ever transact with the reviewing firm.

### What happens after the call?

Within 1-3 business days, you receive a written valuation range and the inputs that produced it. The deliverable is auditable, so any qualified buyer can replicate the math and identify the specific input that produces a different number.

### Can I bring an attorney or CPA to the call?

Yes. You are welcome to bring your advisor to the call. The underwriter is comfortable walking the methodology with an attorney or CPA on the line, and the conversation often goes faster when the advisor is part of it.

### Will the underwriter pressure me to sell?

No. The underwriter's role is to produce a market analysis, not to close a sale. If you decide to transact, the analysis is shared with the buyer at your direction. Until then, the analysis is confidential and yours.

### What if my interest is in multiple counties or basins?

The valuation is run for each component interest and combined into a single deliverable. The comparable-transactions selection is county-specific where the data supports it and basin-level where it does not.

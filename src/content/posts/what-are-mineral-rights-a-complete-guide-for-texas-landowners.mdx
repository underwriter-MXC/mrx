## What Are Mineral Rights? A Complete Guide for Texas Landowners

Mineral rights are a crucial aspect of land ownership in Texas, representing the legal rights to extract and profit from the minerals beneath the surface of a property. Understanding mineral rights is essential for landowners, especially in a state rich in oil and gas resources. This guide will explore the definition, ownership, selling processes, leasing agreements, and tax implications associated with mineral rights in Texas. Many landowners may not realize the value of their mineral rights or how to navigate the complexities of selling or leasing them. By the end of this article, you will have a comprehensive understanding of mineral rights, including how to sell them effectively and the legal considerations involved.

### Key Takeaways

- **Mineral rights and surface rights are legally separate in Texas**: Texas law allows a landowner to sell the surface while retaining the minerals, or vice versa. Once severed, the mineral estate is dominant, giving the mineral owner the right to use the surface for production activities.
- **Royalty interest is the most common mineral right ownership type**: A royalty interest entitles the owner to a percentage of gross production revenue without bearing drilling or operating costs. Working interests share in both revenue and expenses.
- **You can own mineral rights without owning the surface**: Millions of Texans inherited or purchased mineral rights that are completely separate from any surface deed. Ownership is proven through recorded instruments in the county deed records where the land lies.
- **Producing vs. non-producing status dramatically affects value**: Minerals with active royalty production are worth 3-10x more than equivalent acreage with no current production. An undeveloped position near active drilling still commands premium pricing over truly dormant acreage.

## What Defines Texas Mineral Rights Ownership?

Texas mineral rights ownership is defined by the legal entitlements that allow landowners to extract and profit from the minerals located beneath their property. These rights can be separated from surface rights, meaning that one party can own the land’s surface while another owns the minerals below. This separation can lead to complex ownership structures and transactions, making it essential for landowners to understand their rights and responsibilities.

### How Does Texas Law Define Mineral Rights and Ownership?

Under Texas law, mineral rights are considered a type of property right that grants the owner the ability to explore, extract, and sell minerals such as oil, gas, and other natural resources. The Texas Property Code outlines the legal framework governing these rights, emphasizing that mineral rights can be sold, leased, or inherited independently of the surface rights. This legal distinction is crucial for landowners, as it affects how they can utilize their property and the potential income they can generate from mineral extraction.

### What Are the Differences Between Surface Rights and Mineral Rights in Texas?

In Texas, surface rights refer to the ownership of the land itself, while mineral rights pertain to the resources beneath the surface. This distinction is significant because it allows for different parties to own these rights. For example, a landowner may sell their mineral rights while retaining ownership of the surface land. This separation can lead to conflicts, especially if mineral extraction activities disrupt the surface land’s use. Understanding these differences is vital for landowners to navigate their rights effectively and make informed decisions regarding their property.

## What Are the Key Aspects of Leasing Mineral Rights in Texas?

Leasing mineral rights is another option for landowners who wish to retain ownership while allowing companies to extract resources. This arrangement can provide a steady income stream through royalty payments. Understanding the key aspects of leasing agreements is essential for landowners to protect their interests.

### How Do Mineral Rights Lease Agreements Work in Texas?

Mineral rights lease agreements in Texas typically involve a landowner granting a company the right to explore and extract minerals in exchange for a signing bonus and ongoing royalty payments. These agreements should specify the duration of the lease, the percentage of royalties, and any conditions for termination. Landowners should carefully review lease terms to ensure they are favorable and protect their rights.

### What Are Typical Royalty Structures and Payment Terms in Texas Mineral Leases?

Royalty structures in Texas mineral leases can vary widely, but they generally include:

1. **Percentage of Production**: Landowners typically receive a percentage of the revenue generated from the sale of extracted minerals, often ranging from 12.5% to 25%.
2. **Signing Bonuses**: Many leases include an upfront signing bonus, which can provide immediate financial benefits to landowners.
3. **Payment Terms**: Payments are usually made on a monthly or quarterly basis, depending on the lease agreement.

Understanding these structures helps landowners negotiate better terms and maximize their income from mineral leases.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What are mineral rights and do I own them?

Mineral rights are the legal right to extract and profit from oil, gas, coal, and other subsurface resources beneath a parcel of land. In Texas, mineral rights can be separated from surface ownership, meaning you might own the minerals under land you don't own on the surface, or vice versa. To find out if you own mineral rights, search the deed records in the county where the land sits, starting with the instrument by which you or your ancestor acquired the property.

### What is the difference between surface rights and mineral rights?

Surface rights govern what happens on top of the land, farming, building, grazing. Mineral rights govern what's below, oil, gas, coal, and other subsurface resources. In Texas, these can be severed, meaning different people can own the surface and the minerals simultaneously. The mineral estate is legally 'dominant,' meaning mineral owners have the right to use the surface as reasonably necessary for production, even without the surface owner's permission.

### What is a royalty interest in mineral rights?

A royalty interest entitles the owner to receive a percentage of gross production revenue from oil and gas wells, without bearing any drilling, completion, or operating costs. Royalty rates are set in the oil and gas lease and typically range from 1/8 (12.5%) to 1/4 (25%) of gross production. A working interest, by contrast, shares in production revenue but also bears proportionate drilling and operating costs.

### How do I find out if my land has oil or gas production?

Search the Texas Railroad Commission's online records at rrc.texas.gov using the county and operator name. You can find well permits, production reports, and lease information. Also check your county's deed records for any oil and gas leases recorded against your property. If you're receiving royalty checks, the operator is required to send a check stub detailing production volumes and prices.

### What is a division order and why does it matter?

A division order is a document prepared by the oil company that specifies each owner's fractional interest in production from a well. You must sign and return the division order before the operator will pay you royalties. Review it carefully, errors in the decimal interest listed can underpay you for years. If the division order contradicts your deed or lease, contact an oil and gas attorney immediately before signing.

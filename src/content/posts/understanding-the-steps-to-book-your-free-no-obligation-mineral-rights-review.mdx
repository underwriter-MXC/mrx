> This article describes the current MRX first-party booking flow as accessed August 12, 2026. Interface steps and available times can change. The phone review is free, educational, directional, and no-obligation. It is not legal or tax guidance, a title opinion, a reserve report, engineering work, a formal valuation engagement, or a guarantee of eligibility, value, an offer, payment, or closing. Angela is a fictional MRX AI scheduling guide, not a human or licensed professional.

## Answer first

Use the [MRX booking page](/book/) and complete seven checkpoints:

1. open the private appointment assistant;
2. confirm the timezone in which you want to see appointment times;
3. choose tomorrow afternoon, tomorrow evening, or the next available window;
4. select one of the live phone times shown;
5. provide the name, email, and phone number for the appointment;
6. answer each requested-call and confirmation-channel question separately; and
7. wait for the on-screen message that says you are booked.

The last checkpoint matters. Seeing a time, selecting a time, or typing contact information does not by itself prove that an appointment was created. If the calendar cannot be reached or the confirmation fails, the current flow says no appointment was created.

## Before you start: know what this booking does

The booking creates a phone appointment with the MRX team. It does not submit a sale agreement, transfer mineral rights, approve an offer, establish title, calculate a final value, or commit the owner to a transaction.

The current [booking page](https://mineralrightsxchange.com/book/) says phone appointments are free and there is no obligation to sell. The [MRX FAQ](https://mineralrightsxchange.com/faq/) describes the underwriter assessment as directional rather than a formal appraisal and says no card is collected for the free review.

MRX may later have a commercial interest in buying an interest. The booking page and FAQ state that this role is disclosed before an agreement is signed. Booking the discussion is therefore an intake action, not acceptance of any future proposal.

## Step 1: open the private appointment assistant

Start from the first-party [MRX booking page](/book/). Select **Find three phone times** or **Open appointment assistant**. The page opens a private scheduling conversation with Angela rather than sending you through a public calendar.

You can ask Tommy a mineral-rights question before booking. If a phone call would help, the booking flow can begin from the same site. Do not rely on an old screenshot, copied calendar link, or third-party message for current availability.

Confirm that the browser address is on `mineralrightsxchange.com` before entering appointment information. The [MRX privacy policy](https://mineralrightsxchange.com/privacy-policy/) and [terms](https://mineralrightsxchange.com/terms/) describe the first-party account, communications, and private-document boundaries.

## Step 2: confirm the timezone

Angela begins with the timezone detected by the browser and asks whether it is correct. Confirm it or choose the applicable timezone before requesting times.

This control prevents a common scheduling error: reading a displayed time in one zone and expecting the call in another. Use the timezone where you will actually receive the call. If you are traveling, decide whether the appointment should follow your current location or your usual home or business timezone.

The current flow recognizes the major U.S. zones, including Eastern, Central, Mountain, Pacific, Alaska, Hawaii, and Arizona. The live interface should be the authority for the label you select because daylight-saving treatment can change the clock offset.

## Step 3: choose a timing window

After the timezone is set, the current flow offers three search choices:

- tomorrow afternoon;
- tomorrow evening; or
- the next available time.

This is a search preference, not a promised appointment. Angela checks the current MRX calendar after the choice is made. The booking page describes three available choices, but a narrower window can return fewer choices or none when availability is limited.

If the preferred window has no opening, choose another part of the day or request the next available times. If the calendar is unavailable, the assistant should say that it cannot reach the calendar and will not make up a time.

## Step 4: select a live phone time

Review the date, clock time, and timezone on every displayed option. Select the one that works. The assistant then removes the other choices and tells you it will hold the selected option while the remaining booking steps are completed.

At this point, treat the status as **selected, not booked**. The slot still needs appointment identity, a working contact path, requested-call permission, and a successful final confirmation.

Do not create a second browser session to chase the same time. Availability is live and may change. Complete the active flow or let it fail closed, then request a new set of options if needed.

## Step 5: provide the appointment contact details

The current booking flow asks for:

- the first name to place on the appointment;
- an email for appointment details and secure MRX member access; and
- the phone number the MRX team should call.

Use information you can access and verify. A mistyped email can prevent a requested confirmation or secure sign-in link from reaching you. A mistyped phone number can prevent the requested appointment call.

These fields identify the appointment; they do not prove mineral ownership. You do not need to guess at acreage, title, lease status, a royalty decimal, production, or value in order to select a phone time.

Keep sensitive information out of the scheduling conversation. Do not provide passwords, one-time codes, full taxpayer identification numbers, bank credentials, or unrelated personal records. The [MRX terms](https://mineralrightsxchange.com/terms/) say users are responsible for keeping sign-in links secure and uploading only files they are authorized to share.

## Step 6: make each communication choice separately

The current booking flow does not treat every communication purpose as one combined permission. Read each prompt and answer it on its own.

### Requested phone call

The appointment is a phone call, so the flow asks whether MRX may call the supplied number for that specific appointment. If that permission is declined, the current assistant says no phone appointment was booked and returns to the on-site conversation.

### Email confirmation

The flow asks whether MRX may email the appointment confirmation. Choosing no should not be interpreted as canceling the requested phone call. The on-screen confirmation remains the primary record when no email confirmation is requested.

### SMS confirmation

The flow separately asks whether MRX may text the appointment confirmation. The booking page states that declining text messages does not prevent booking. If SMS is selected, the live disclosure describes message and data rates and the applicable opt-out instructions.

### Optional AI-voice contact

The current flow asks separately whether Angela may use an AI-generated voice for appointment or document updates the owner requests. The prompt says this permission is optional, may be revoked, and is not required to book or use website help.

### Marketing choices

Do not read an appointment permission as blanket marketing consent. The [MRX terms](https://mineralrightsxchange.com/terms/) say requested-call, transactional email, transactional text, and optional marketing choices are stored separately. Review the purpose shown with each choice.

## Step 7: wait for the booked confirmation

The booking is complete only after the first-party interface returns a success state. The current confirmation begins with **You’re booked for** and repeats the selected appointment label. It also says which requested confirmation channels were used, whether secure member access is ready or a sign-in link was sent, and which phone number will receive the call.

Capture or write down:

- the confirmed date and time;
- the displayed timezone;
- the phone number to be called;
- whether email or SMS confirmation was requested; and
- the first-party account or on-screen location where the appointment appears.

If an email or text was requested but does not arrive immediately, do not assume the appointment failed when the on-screen booked state is present. Check the displayed appointment details and the secure owner-account route. Conversely, an unconfirmed selected slot should not be treated as booked merely because it appeared on screen earlier.

## Booked versus not booked

Use this status register:

- **Assistant opened:** not booked.
- **Timezone or timing window selected:** not booked.
- **Live slots displayed:** not booked.
- **One slot selected:** not booked yet.
- **Contact details entered but permission prompts remain:** not booked yet.
- **Requested-call permission declined:** not booked.
- **Calendar unavailable or no valid options returned:** not booked.
- **Final confirmation failed:** not booked.
- **The first-party interface says “You’re booked for” and repeats the appointment:** booked.
- **The interface recognizes an existing future appointment and refuses a duplicate:** retain the existing booked appointment rather than creating another.

This distinction prevents an owner and the MRX team from preparing for different appointment states.

## What to do when the flow does not complete

### The timezone is wrong

Correct it before requesting availability. If you already selected a slot but have not received the booked confirmation, restart the first-party flow and choose a time shown in the correct zone.

### No times match the preferred window

Ask for another part of the day or the next available times. Do not convert or reuse an old option outside the live flow.

### The calendar cannot be reached

The current assistant says it will not invent a time and that no appointment has been created. Try again later from the first-party booking page. You can keep asking mineral-rights questions on the site while booking is unavailable.

### A selected time fails at confirmation

Treat it as unbooked. Return to the live calendar and request fresh availability. Do not assume the earlier slot remains held.

### You already have a future appointment

The current assistant is designed not to create a duplicate. Use the existing appointment context and tell the MRX team what you want them to prepare for the call.

## What to prepare after the appointment is booked

Booking needs scheduling and contact information. The review itself benefits from a different preparation list. Write down the question you want answered and identify the property using whatever state, county, legal description, operator, lease, unit, well, payor, or owner number is already available.

If you have them, organize relevant deeds, leases, division orders, royalty statements, production identifiers, or written offers. Mark missing or conflicting information as unknown rather than filling the gap from memory. The detailed [review-call preparation guide](/blog/what-to-bring-to-your-underwriter-review-call-essential-documents-and-preparation-guide/) explains how to group those records.

The [MRX How It Works page](https://mineralrightsxchange.com/how-it-works/) explains that a review can organize ownership evidence, production and royalty context, offer terms, assumptions, and questions that may require another professional. The actual scope depends on the owner’s question and available evidence.

## Preserve the no-obligation boundary

Free and no-obligation describe the appointment and review relationship. They do not promise a particular output, price, offer, timeline, payment, closing, or tax result.

Before relying on later analysis, separate:

- the free educational review;
- any directional range and its evidence and assumptions;
- any later written proposal;
- any purchase agreement or conveyance; and
- any independent legal, tax, title, accounting, engineering, or valuation work the owner chooses to obtain.

The booking is complete when the appointment is confirmed. Every later decision remains a separate owner-controlled step.

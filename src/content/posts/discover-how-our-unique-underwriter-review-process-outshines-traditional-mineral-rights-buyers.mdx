## Answer first

Evaluate a mineral-rights underwriter review by asking what information was considered, what assumptions remain, what written terms apply, and which questions are outside the review. This neutral approach is more supportable than rating one service against unnamed buyers or predicting a transaction outcome.

## Begin with scope and boundaries

A review should identify the property and interest being discussed, the records available, and the limits of the discussion. Ask whether the reviewer is providing educational context only or whether a separate legal, tax, title, or certified-appraisal opinion is needed.

The Texas Comptroller's [Property Classification Guide](https://comptroller.texas.gov/taxes/property-tax/docs/96-313.pdf) recognizes separate surface and mineral-interest categories in its classification guidance. That does not settle ownership or title for an individual owner, so a review should not be presented as a title opinion.

## Check the public-record context

The Texas RRC provides [reported production resources](https://www.rrc.texas.gov/oil-and-gas/research-and-statistics/production-data/) and [well-information resources](https://www.rrc.texas.gov/oil-and-gas/research-and-statistics/well-information/). Ask whether those public records were considered, what time period is relevant to the discussion, and whether any reported information has limitations.

RRC resources provide operator-reported production and public operating/status context. They do not establish ownership, title, or a required transaction value.

## Compare process questions, not marketing claims

Use the same questions for any service you consider:

- What records did you review?
- What assumptions or provisional facts affect the discussion?
- Which facts could change before a transaction?
- Will proposed terms be provided in writing?
- Which ownership, tax, title, or valuation questions call for an appropriately qualified professional for my own circumstances?

This checklist does not rank providers. It helps owners compare the clarity of information and boundaries available before making a decision.

## Read written terms before deciding

A review conversation is not a substitute for reading the terms of a proposed transaction. Ask for time to understand contingencies, adjustments, and questions that are unresolved. If a decision depends on ownership, taxes, or legal consequences, obtain advice from the appropriate qualified professional.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can public well data prove that I own an interest?

No. Public RRC well information can provide operating or status context, but it is not proof of ownership or title.

### Can a review establish a value or transaction result?

No. A review can describe the information and assumptions considered; it does not guarantee a value or transaction result.

### Should I compare only a headline number?

No. Ask about records, assumptions, written terms, contingencies, and professional boundaries as well.

### Is an underwriter review a legal or tax conclusion?

No. Seek a qualified professional for legal, tax, title, or certified-appraisal advice about your facts.

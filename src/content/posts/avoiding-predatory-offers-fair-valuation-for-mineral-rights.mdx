## What a Fair Valuation Is

A fair valuation for mineral rights is a market-based value range built on public production data, a published methodology, and a documented set of inputs. The valuation is not a single number; it is a range, with the math attached.

The [MRX published methodology](/methodology/) describes the inputs and the cross-checks that produce the range. The three methods are discounted cash flow, comparable transactions, and a royalty income multiple check. The convergence of the three is the defensible range.

A buyer who refuses to disclose the methodology is not offering a fair valuation. A buyer who discloses the methodology and the math is.

## The Red Flags of a Predatory Offer

Predatory offers share identifiable patterns. The red flags are the same regardless of the buyer's intent, because the buyer's pricing model is built on the assumption that the seller will not verify independently.

- **Unsolicited letter or call with a specific dollar offer.** Unsolicited outreach often arrives without the methodology, the multiple, or the discount-rate inputs an owner would otherwise document; treat those offers as a starting data point, not a final price.
- **High-pressure deadline.** Pressure to decide within a short deadline without allowing time for an independent review is a signal worth asking about.
- **Missing methodology.** A buyer who will not share the multiple, the discount rate, or the assumed decline is asking you to take the price on faith.
- **Clawback clause.** A provision that allows the buyer to reduce the price after the contract is signed. Some buyers structure these to reduce offers materially at closing.
- **Verbal-only commitment.** A buyer who will not put the terms in writing is not ready to transact.
- **Inflated headline number.** An offer significantly above the comparable-transactions range should be checked against the post-clawback number. If the buyer will not commit to a post-clawback floor, the offer is not yet ready to compare.

Each red flag is independent of the price. An offer with all six at a high headline number warrants more scrutiny than an offer with one at a low headline number.

## The Comparison Framework

The owner's defense is a written comparison framework. The same framework is used in the [MRX offer review pillar](/offer-review/) and described in [How to Identify Unfair Offers for Mineral Rights](/blog/how-to-identify-unfair-offers-for-mineral-rights/).

| Component | What to record |
|---|---|
| Total net dollar amount | After all deductions, fees, and post-closing adjustments |
| Price per net mineral acre | Calculated from your net mineral acres |
| Implied royalty income multiple | The offer divided by your trailing monthly royalty income |
| Closing timeline | Days from contract to funded sale |
| Financing contingency | Whether the buyer's funds are committed |
| Due diligence clawback | Whether the buyer can reduce the price after signing |
| Title requirements | Standards and who pays for title insurance |
| Operator consent | Whether the operator must approve the assignment |

A buyer who refuses to fill in this table is telling you that the offer is not yet ready to compare.

## The Royalty Multiple Check

The implied royalty multiple is calculated by dividing the offer by the trailing monthly royalty income. The [MRX published methodology](/methodology/) uses the multiple as a cross-check that the DCF and comparable-transactions methods are producing a market-consistent answer. The published methodology does not state a fixed market range; the multiple is a comparison tool, not a threshold.

- **Ask every buyer for the multiple.** A buyer who will not disclose the multiple is asking you to take the price on faith.
- **Compare the multiple across offers.** If one offer implies a multiple far above or below the others, the underlying assumption is different. Ask for the assumption table.
- **Compare the multiple against recent comparable transactions.** The multiple is most useful when matched against similar interests in the same county and formation.

The multiple check is not a substitute for a full DCF or comparable-transactions review. It is a fast first cut that surfaces the offers worth a deeper look.

## How to Generate Real Leverage

The single most effective way to avoid a predatory offer is to create competitive tension.

- **Get multiple offers.** Generating competitive offers is one of the most effective ways to maximize your sale price. Even before approaching multiple buyers, get an independent underwriter review to establish a defensible value range.
- **Anchor the offers to an independent valuation.** Comparing every offer to a documented DCF valuation, built on the [MRX methodology](/methodology/) and public production data, is how to determine which offer is competitive.
- **Share the valuation with each buyer.** Buyers respond to documented numbers. An offer that is materially below the independent range has to defend the gap.
- **Refuse to sign under pressure.** A buyer who will not allow time for an independent review is signaling that the price is not defensible.

Sellers who follow this sequence routinely achieve better outcomes than sellers who anchor to the first number.

## The Clawback Conversation

The clawback clause is a common mechanism for after-the-fact price reduction. The defense is to refuse the clawback, or to cap it at a fixed dollar amount with a clear definition of what triggers the adjustment.

- **Capped clawback.** A fixed dollar cap (e.g., 5% of the headline number) with a defined trigger (e.g., a title defect exceeding $X) is acceptable.
- **Triggered clawback.** A "due diligence adjustment" with no cap and no defined trigger is not acceptable. The buyer can use the clawback to reduce the price by any amount for any reason.
- **No clawback.** The cleanest terms. The buyer takes the title and the production as-is, and the price is the price.

## The Tax and Estate Layer

A disposition of mineral rights is a taxable event. The mechanics are governed by federal statute, including [26 USC § 1001](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/1001) for gain and loss computation and [IRS Publication 544](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p544) for sales and other dispositions of assets. The valuation itself is not tax guidance, but the after-tax outcome is material and should be modeled with a qualified CPA before any contract is signed.

For inherited interests, the federal stepped-up basis under [26 USC § 1014](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/1014) generally resets the basis to fair market value at the date of death, which can reduce the taxable gain on a subsequent sale. [IRS Publication 551](https://www.irs.gov/forms-pubs/about-publication-551) covers the basis rules in detail.

If a 1031 exchange is part of the plan, the [IRC § 1031 timelines](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/1031) (45-day identification, 180-day closing) apply, and the valuation at the time of the relinquished sale establishes the basis for the replacement property. See [How to Navigate a 1031 Exchange for Mineral Rights](/blog/how-to-navigate-a-1031-exchange-for-mineral-rights/) for the mechanics.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How do I know if a mineral rights offer is predatory?

Key red flags include an unsolicited letter or call with a specific dollar offer, pressure to decide within a short deadline without time for an independent review, offers that do not disclose the buyer's methodology, and clawback clauses that reduce the price after due diligence. A buyer who discloses the methodology, allows time for an independent review, and puts all terms in writing is signaling that the price is defensible.

### What is a clawback clause in a mineral rights purchase agreement?

A clawback clause allows the buyer to reduce the agreed purchase price after the contract is signed, typically citing "due diligence adjustments" related to title defects, production variances, or environmental issues. Some buyers structure these to reduce offers materially at closing using provisions buried in the fine print. Refuse uncapped clawbacks; cap them at a fixed dollar amount with a clear definition of what triggers the adjustment.

### Should I get multiple offers for my mineral rights before selling?

Yes. Generating competitive offers is one of the most effective ways to maximize your sale price. Even before approaching multiple buyers, get an independent underwriter review to establish a defensible value range. This prevents you from anchoring to a buyer's opening offer and gives you objective grounds to reject lowball bids.

### What is a fair mineral rights offer based on?

A fair offer reflects recent comparable sales in your county (price per net mineral acre), your monthly royalty income capitalized at a market multiple, the basin and formation you're in, operator quality, and current commodity prices. The [MRX published methodology](/methodology/) documents the inputs and the cross-checks used to produce a defensible range. An offer that does not disclose its methodology or that significantly deviates from market comps without explanation warrants skepticism.

### Are all unsolicited offers predatory?

Not all, but unsolicited offers are by definition the buyer's first attempt at a price, and the buyer's pricing model is built on the assumption that the seller will not verify independently. The owner's defense is the same regardless of the buyer's intent: compare the offer to an independent valuation, ask for the methodology, and refuse to sign under pressure.

### What is a royalty income multiple?

A royalty income multiple is the ratio of the value of a royalty interest to its trailing monthly royalty income. The implied multiple is a number any owner can calculate and any buyer should disclose. The [MRX published methodology](/methodology/) uses the multiple as one of three cross-checks to confirm the DCF and comparable-transactions methods are producing a market-consistent answer.

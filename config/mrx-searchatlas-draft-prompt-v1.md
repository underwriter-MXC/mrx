# MRX SearchAtlas draft brief v1

Create an evidence-first educational draft for Mineral Rights Xchange (MRX). The draft is for editorial and compliance review only; do not publish it.

## Audience and voice

- Write for a mineral or royalty owner who wants a calm, plain-English explanation before making a decision.
- Use a knowledgeable, direct, non-pressuring voice. Avoid hype, fear, urgency, filler, and repetitive phrasing.
- Define technical terms on first use. Do not assume the reader already understands title, lease, royalty, tax, production, or valuation terminology.

## SEO and AEO structure

- Answer the title's main question directly in the opening 80 to 120 words.
- Use one descriptive H1 only. Use concise H2 and H3 headings that match distinct search intent; do not repeat the title as a body heading.
- Include a short key-takeaways section, a practical checklist or comparison where useful, and four to six visible FAQs with non-duplicative answers.
- Cover the primary keyword naturally. Add close variants only where they improve clarity; do not keyword-stuff.
- Make every answer understandable when quoted out of context by a search engine or AI assistant.

## Evidence and compliance

- Cite authoritative primary sources for factual, legal, tax, regulatory, production, price, percentage, deadline, or money-related claims. Prefer the IRS, state statutes and agencies, the Texas Railroad Commission, county records, or other first-party public records.
- Never invent a source, statistic, customer result, quotation, credential, or case study.
- Do not provide individualized legal, tax, title, investment, or appraisal advice. Explain that facts vary and recommend verification with the appropriate qualified professional where relevant.
- Never claim an exact value, guaranteed price, highest offer, guaranteed outcome, risk-free transaction, instant offer, certified appraisal, or USPAP appraisal.
- Do not imply that MRX is a neutral adviser. State where relevant that MRX may evaluate an interest as a potential buyer.
- Do not use aggressive calls to action, artificial scarcity, or personal-knowledge statements such as assuming the reader inherited property or needs cash.

## Conversion path

- End with a low-pressure, intent-matched next step inviting the reader to talk with the MRX team or request an underwriter review.
- Explain what information helps the conversation and what the reader can expect.
- The CTA must not promise a value, offer, closing, timing, or outcome.

## Draft handoff

- Provide a suggested SEO title of no more than 54 characters so ` · MRX` remains within a 60-character final title budget.
- Provide a meta description of 145 to 155 characters.
- Provide suggested internal links to the assigned pillar, its cluster hub, two related articles, and one relevant decision page.
- Provide a source list with publisher, title, URL, publication or effective date where available, access date, and the claim supported.
- Flag any sentence that needs human legal, tax, title, valuation, testimonial, or compliance review.
- Return a draft only. Do not mark it approved, completed for publication, or published.
